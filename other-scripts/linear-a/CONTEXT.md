# Linear A in its world: history, geography, interactions (data only)

4 Oct 2026. Data only, no readings. Object counts from our `data/corpus.json`.

## 1. Chronology
- MM II (c. 1800–1700 BCE): Protopalatial. Phaistos has the earliest Linear A (27 MM II objects in our corpus).
- MM III–LM IA (c. 1700–1600/1530): Neopalatial peak. Thera's eruption falls at the end of LM IA. Radiocarbon from an olive branch buried in tephra gives 1627–1600 BCE ([Friedrich et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16645088/)). Annual-ring calibration has shifted the range toward the 16th century ([Pearson et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6093623)).
- LM IB (to c. 1450): HT, Khania, Zakros, Palaikastro, Gournia, Myrtos-Pyrgos, Petras, Tylissos and Sklavokambos are destroyed by fire, which bakes their archives. Knossos survives. 1,310 of our 1,721 objects are LM IB ([LM IB horizon](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/literature-and-writing/7fdadf1e-94d3-531c-b9d2-e0aaf529194f/a-new-look-at-the-late-minoan-i-b-administrative-horizon)).
- LM II–IIIA (c. 1450–1370): a Greek-writing administration at Knossos uses Linear B. The date of the Knossos tablets is disputed: LM II/IIIA1 or LM IIIB ([review](https://www.archeologia.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Crete_the-end-of-Knossos.pdf)).

## 3. Administration
- Hallager: **2,120 sealed documents** (roundels, noduli, flat-based and hanging nodules) ([Aegaeum 14](https://andromedabooks.gr/?p=233177)).
- Hallager counts **171 roundels**: Khania 114, HT 24, Knossos 12, Phaistos 10, five or fewer each at Malia, Zakros, Pyrgos and Tylissos, and 1 at Kea ([Heidelberg](https://books.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/propylaeum/catalog/view/327/448/80010)).
- The same seal rings are stamped at several sites, for example on Knossos-ring impressions found at HT, Zakros, Sklavokambos and Gournia. This gives a site network that does not depend on words. Data: CMS volumes II.6 and II.7 on [Arachne](https://arachne.dainst.org).

## 4. Economy and calendar
Commodities (approximate harvest season on modern Crete):
- grain: sown Nov–Dec, harvested May–June
- grapes: vintage Aug–Sep
- olives: Nov–Feb
- figs: Aug–Sep
- saffron crocus: flowers Oct–Nov
- sheep: shearing Apr–May; lambing in winter

Linear B Knossos Fp tablets date oil offerings by month names, so a month-stamped Aegean ledger exists to use as a control ([ref](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.4801)).

Purple dye:
- murex heaps at Kouphonisi (MM) and Palaikastro ([BSA](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/annual-of-the-british-school-at-athens/article/abs/palaikastro-shells-and-bronze-age-purpledye-production-in-the-mediterranean-basin/F1564750F109B952E3C0C09531399742))

Timber: cypress was exported ([note](https://camws.org/meeting/2009/program/abstracts/03C1-4.Koh.Meyer.Harwood.Ronnick.pdf)).

Metals:
- Copper oxhide ingots weigh 20–30 kg. HT has 19; Zakros and Tylissos also have them.
- Lead isotopes of LM I ingots point to non-Cypriot sources. Cypriot copper dominates after c. 1250 ([Gale 2011](https://ancientportsantiques.com/wp-content/uploads/Documents/PLACES/Crete-Cyprus/CyprusCopperOxhide-Gale2011.pdf)).

## 5. Weights
The "Minoan" standard ([Michailidou](https://helios.eie.gr/helios/bitstream/10442/8480/1/Weights%20in%20Context.pdf); [FHW](https://www.fhw.gr/chronos/02/crete/en/achievments/mesure.html)):
- unit about **61 g**
- 8 units = 1 mina of about 488 g
- 1 talent of about 29 kg, close to the mass of an oxhide ingot

Balance weights are lead and stone discs. They are found on Crete, Kea, Thera (Akrotiri), Samothrace and Miletus.

## 6. Foreign records
- **Mari (c. 1780–1760)**: tin, in minas, issued "to the Kaptarian" and to the interpreter of the chief Kaptarian merchant at Ugarit. Inventories also list Kaptarian weapons, a gold-capped dagger, leather goods and metal vessels ([Keftiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keftiu); [Iraq](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/journals/iraq/article/an-illustration-to-a-mari-inventory/F59AE1B40E41DC75C235C50D8A092F38)).
- **Egypt**: Keftiu are shown in the Theban tombs of Senenmut, User, Rekhmire and Menkheperreseneb (Hatshepsut to Thutmose III, LM IB to LM II). They bring metal vessels, bull rhyta and ingots ([Panagiotopoulos 2001](https://archiv.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/propylaeumdok/827/1/Panagiotopoulos_Keftiu_in_context_2001.pdf)).
- **Avaris**: Minoan-style frescoes of the Thutmosid period ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_frescoes_from_Tell_el-Dab%27a)).
- **Kom el-Hetan (Amenhotep III)**: an Aegean place list that repeats Amnisos, read as a ship's itinerary ([JAEI](https://journals.librarypublishing.arizona.edu/jaei/article/id/958)).

## 7. Ships and seasons
- Sailing season about Apr–Oct. Etesian north winds in Jul–Aug favour Crete to Egypt. The return runs along the Levant, Cyprus and Anatolia, or by short hops north of Crete ([CAA](https://journal.caa-international.org/articles/119)).
- Kommos (HT/Phaistos) and Zakros face south and east. Khania faces west and the Kythera route.

## Out-of-the-box ideas
1. **A common currency for mixed lists.**
   - Idea: KU-RO totals that fail on two-commodity tablets (HT 27a, 94a, 100, 123a) balance once each commodity is converted to one unit of value. Search 10^6 random exchange vectors on a log grid, including Ur III/Old Babylonian-style price ratios. Re-test survivors on held-out sections.
   - Data: our corpus; Ur III price tables from CDLI.
   - Kill: shuffled commodity labels, or totals permuted across tablets, balance as often. Planted control: an LB or Ur III list with known prices.
   - Why new: it uses arithmetic over the commodity frame and no sounds.
  - Tested (la30): killed. No rate vector balances held-out totals; the Ur III control passes; see FINDINGS.md.
2. **Receipt to ledger reconciliation.**
   - Idea: Khania's 114 roundels and the HT nodules are receipts whose word, logogram and number reappear in the tablet entries at the same site. Find the assignment by annealing.
   - Data: corpus supports; Hallager tables.
   - Kill: the same match rate with site labels swapped (KH roundels against HT tablets).
   - Why new: it links object classes, not words to languages.
  - Tested (la28): not supported. Receipts and ledgers share no more than regional vocabulary; see FINDINGS.md.
3. **The seal-ring graph predicts word sharing.**
   - Idea: sites that share sealing rings share more words than distance predicts.
   - Data: CMS II.6–7 (Arachne) find-sites.
   - Kill: degree-preserving rewiring of the seal graph explains the sharing as well.
   - Why new: an external administrative network, not place names.
4. **The destruction-season clock.**
   - Idea: each LM IB archive is a snapshot of one moment. Score 10^5 random crop calendars times destruction months on commodity stock profiles (entry counts, totals, fractions).
   - Data: our corpus; FAO crop calendars for Crete.
   - Positive control: the Knossos Fp month-dated tablets.
   - Kill: commodity-shuffled archives fit as well.
  - Tested (la25): killed. Base rate and season are confounded; see FINDINGS.md.
5. **Heaping on the physical weight ladder.**
   - Idea: quantities, especially of metal, hide and textile logograms, heap at ratios of the excavated discoid weights (61 g, 488 g, 29 kg). Run 10^4 random ladders as the null, and use LB weight lists as the positive control.
   - Data: Petruso or Michailidou catalogues of weight masses.
   - Kill: random ladders fit as well, or LB fails.
6. **Terroir.**
   - Idea: per-site commodity mix (about 15 Cretan sites) follows local environment: elevation, rainfall and slope from SRTM and WorldClim.
   - Null: 10^4 random spatially smooth fields.
   - Out-of-sample prediction: the commodity mix of a held-out site.
   - Kill: random fields fit as well.
  - Tested (la29): not supported. Land predicts no commodity mix or administrative sign beyond random fields; see FINDINGS.md.
