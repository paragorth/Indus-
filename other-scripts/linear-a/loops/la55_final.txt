la55 final (5 Oct 2026): WORDS GROW LIKE CITIES
Cycles: loops/la55_cycle1.txt, la55_cycle2.txt, la55_cycle3.txt. Tools: tools/la55_common.py, la55_c1.py, la55_c2.py,
la55_c2b.py, la55_c3.py, la55_c3b.py, la55_c3c.py. Checkpoints: data/la55_ckpt/ (git-ignored).

Idea: archives might grow like cities. Administrative function words would saturate (each office needs them once),
names would grow in proportion to the number of documents, and specialised goods would appear only above a size
threshold. For every word and sign, a scaling exponent was fitted across archives (35 Linear A deposits, 103
Linear B find areas, 277 Ur III site-year archives). The fits were compared with documents re-dealt to archives:
free (N1), within genre (N3), and within genre x length (N4), 300-1,000 times each. Further tests: bootstrap (N2),
a planted class, Heaps growth per independent class, leave-one-archive-out prediction, inverse estimation of
archive size, and settlement hectares from open sources.
Novelty: urban scaling of words exists for Twitter by city (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6837183/).
No one has done it for ancient archives. la5 fitted only one corpus-wide Heaps exponent.

VERDICT: NOT CRACKED. The urban-scaling frame is KILLED by its controls.

Grades
- A (method, calibrated negative): the expected scaling classes do not appear in any of the known administrations.
  - Function words do not saturate relative to names in Linear B (full size or Linear A size) or Ur III.
    - Beta AUC 0.39-0.59.
    - Occupancy AUC 0.27-0.39.
    - Permutation p >= 0.18 under every null.
  - Ur III function words are as often superlinear as saturating.
  - 'Names grow in proportion' holds only in full Linear B under N3 (p 0.037).
    - It weakens under N4 (p 0.056).
    - It disappears at Linear A size (p 0.23-0.60) and in Ur III (p 0.70-0.87).
  - Scaling-based predictions fail on held-out archives in the controls.
    - Saturating-word presence: LB -0.032 and LBS -0.056 bits against proportional sampling, worse than the shuffled data.
    - Archive size from a fragment: Spearman -0.08 in LB and LBS.
  - Planted classes are recovered for saturation (15/15) and linearity (11-14/15).
  - Planted superlinear words are not separable from threshold words at Linear A size (1-5/15).
    - The reason is that Linear A has only two archives above 100 documents (HT Villa R13 and Khania).
- B (calibrated negative): Linear A scaling exponents do not predict what a held-out Linear A archive contains.
  - All words: -0.008 bits against N3 +0.013.
  - Predicted-saturating words: -0.054 against N3 -0.026.
  - Fragments do not reveal archive size (rho 0.06, p 0.30).
  - Linear A archives show no lexical closure (all-word Heaps 0.788 against 0.797 re-dealt, p 0.38).
    - Linear B find areas re-use their own names and goods, and this is detectable at Linear A size (PERSON p 0.007, COMM p 0.003).
- B (descriptive, robust under N3 and N4): Linear A words in each scaling class.
  - SATURATING (higher per-document rate in small archives): GRA, OLIV, HIDE, the single signs KI, TI, *188 and *131B, *86-RO and TE-RI.
  - THRESHOLD (found only in the larger archives): CYP, MI, QA2, *411-VS, the single signs *164, *306 and O, and DI-NA-U.
    - *164, *306 and *411-VS occur only at Khania and HT R13, so here 'threshold' cannot be told from a regional or deposit habit.
  - SUPERLINEAR (N4): *401, NI, *131C, KU-PA3-NU.
  - Everything else is LINEAR (115-117 of 141 types).
  - Unstable between nulls (not graded): VIN, SA-RA2, KU-RO. KU-RO is saturating under N3 and threshold under N4.
- Killed: Linear A signs saturating (37/98 under N3) was document length; it falls to 7/98 under N4.
- C: la45 commodities lean to saturation and headers to threshold (chi-square p 0.018 under N3, 0.10 under N4).
  - Would support: p < 0.05 under N4 once more small archives are published.
  - Would kill: commodities spread proportionally in new small deposits.
- C: bigger towns left more documents and more kinds of logogram, sublinearly (exponent 0.42-0.51; logogram types rho 0.81, p 0.042; documents rho 0.70, p 0.067; 6 sites).
  - Type richness is fully explained by document count (rho -0.17).
  - Would support: the same ordering at Zakros, Khania and HT once open size estimates exist.
  - Would kill: a large town with a small, poor archive.
  - The site sizes come from:
    - Whitelaw, UCL Discovery 10071377: Knossos ~100 ha; Malia and Phaistos 50-60; Palaikastro 15-20.
    - news.gtp.gr: Akrotiri ~20.
    - Wikipedia: Gournia 1.68.

Size-based predictions (recorded but NOT graded, because LA-55.3c fails held-out): a new archive of 20 documents
should hold about 25 word/logogram types (proportional sampling gives 19), with GRA 0.83, VIN 0.79, KU-RO 0.57,
SA-RA2 0.42 and OLIV 0.42 (proportional 0.55, 0.49, 0.31, 0.21, 0.20). For 1,000 documents it should hold about 111
types (proportional 133).

What this rules out: a Linear A word's distribution across archives does not mark it as a function word, a name or
a specialised good. Archive size carries no recoverable lexical structure at this corpus size, and Linear B and
Ur III do not carry it either.
