Graded readings
- Grade A (calibrated negative, method): an archive's time depth cannot be read from which words share tablets at Linear A size. Two simulators that recover planted clocks give the same answer for real Ur III archives of 3 months and of 40 years (rho 0.03, 0.10). Planted recovery alone would have certified a false clock.
- Grade B (calibrated negative): writers-in-time simulators cannot write Hagia Triada. The real archive lies outside every simulated cloud, so ABC posteriors over its persons, tablets ever written and survival are extrapolations; the survival fraction is not identifiable from internal statistics at all.
- Grade B (data tool): the share of word tokens on new Hagia Triada tablets already seen in the corpus is forecast within about +-0.1 by Good-Turing (bias -0.011, 1,000 splits). Frozen: 0.46 [0.375, 0.545] for the next >= 50 HT tablets.
- Grade C (Ur III, outside Linear A): in dated Sumerian archives a short window is dominated by fewer names (Gini falls with window length in all 3 sites; a ridge transfers Umma <-> Puzrish-Dagan, rho 0.68-0.72, but not to Girsu, 0.11). Would support: the same leave-one-site-out transfer on a 4th dated archive (Garshana, Ur). Would kill: rho < 0.3 there.
- Grade C- (not graded further): Zakros fits ~10 active persons against ~300-700 at HT, KH and PH (one fit on 30 tablets). Would support: the same with 50+ Zakros tablets. Would kill: a Zakros Np posterior overlapping HT's.
- Killed: 'HT tablets list distinct entities' (low same-tablet repeats against the simulator): HT has more same-tablet repeats than a token shuffle (16 vs 9.6, P 0.99).
- Cycle verdict: not cracked. The idea was run with the controls the brief asked for; it fails the outside one. Simulation of writers is no better than a two-line estimator for forecasting and gives no calibrated number about the hidden administration.
