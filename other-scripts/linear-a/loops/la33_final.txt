# LA-33 final: QUEUES AT THE STOREROOM (scheduled rations vs demand-driven arrivals)
Scripts: tools/la33_common.py, la33_sims.py, la33_c1.py, la33_c1_model.py, la33_c2.py, la33_c3.py. Checkpoints: data/la33_ckpt/ (git-ignored; Ur III extraction, ~12,000 simulated archives and tablet sets, reports). Logs: loops/la33_cycle1-3.txt.
Prior check: LITERATURE.md, FINDINGS.md, STRATEGIES.md and a web search: no queueing / point-process analysis of Linear A (or Linear B) archives was found.
Data: Linear A 274 tablets with word + amount entries (892 entries); Linear B DAMOS (23 series of a priori kind); Ur III CDLI ATF (15,639 texts with entries; kind fixed a priori by keyword; scratchpad only).

Grades. B (method): archive-level queue statistics separate Ur III ration from delivery groups (AUC 0.87 at 150 texts, 0.66 at 30) and Linear B personnel/census from disbursement series (0.80); weak at Linear A group sizes. A (calibrated negative): the tablet-level regime model fails on real Ur III tablets (AUC 0.42-0.55), so tablet and word labels are not available. B (negative): the premises 'scheduled = low variance in list length' and 'demand recipients are places' fail in Ur III (ration texts have more overdispersed lengths, 13.6 vs 5.6; {ki} places 2.1 % vs 2.7 %). B (negative): no site or word regime difference survives size matching and a non-circular score. B: grain (GRA) lists hold amounts of similar size (CV P 0.012). B: Linear A list lengths are far more regular than Ur III lists of either kind. C: all Linear A groups sit at the demand end of the scale (would support: a calibration corpus in LA format, e.g. Linear B multi-entry lists, placing known ration lists above every LA group; would kill: known ration lists scoring as low as LA). C: grain lists are rations (would support: new grain lists with near-equal amounts to recurring entry words; would kill: new grain lists as varied as OLE/CYP lists). Killed: word classes by regime (KU-NI-SU, DA-ME, DI-DE-RU 'scheduled'; MI-NU-TE, SA-RU, DA-ME 'switchers/offices').
