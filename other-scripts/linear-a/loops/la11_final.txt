# LA-11 final: a blind compression contest against real languages
| LA-<n>.<cycle> | method and control | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| LA-11.final | 39 languages from 18 families (UD, eBible.org, CDLI; sources and licences in data/la11/sources.json; only syllable count tables and opaque held-out sequences are kept). Words are cut into open CV syllables under three spelling conventions (MIXED, DROP, ECHO). For each input and each of 35 target languages, simulated annealing (C core; about 21,200 anneals in 4 cycles) finds the best one-to-one map from the input's top 40 signs to the language's syllables. The map is free, so no sound value is assumed; no Linear B value is used anywhere. Score: compression gain of the real input over its token-shuffled versions, standardized against held-out words of unrelated languages. Controls: (a) shuffled inputs (built into the score); (b) Linear B with sign identities hidden (742, 1,500 and 3,678 types); (c) random Markov strings; plus self-identification and relative-identification for every language, a spelling-mismatch test, and LA from held-out sites. | **Power:** a language's own held-out words pick out its own model in 34-35 of 35 (every cycle, even with mismatched spelling). With the language removed, a relative wins only 6-11 of 31 (chance 4.3). **Linear B control:** Ancient Greek never wins; its rank is 17-33 of 35 under MIXED and DROP (also at 3,678 types) and 7-27 under ECHO; Modern Greek ranks near the bottom. **Linear A:** no language rises above unrelated real words in any cycle. Best z: +0.23 (Coptic, c2), +0.19 (Coptic, c3), -0.10 (Arabic, c1), -0.50 (Arabic, c4). Real orphan inputs beat LA's maximum in 72-87% of cases. Markov strings stay below LA (raw gap 0.05-0.09 vs LA 0.19-0.21 vs LB 0.27-0.32 bits/symbol). A recurring Afro-Asiatic lead is explained about half by absorbency of random structure, vanishes under ECHO (p = 0.51) and changes members at non-HT sites. | **No language family absorbs Linear A better than chance or better than the controls.** The method is too weak to answer the question: it recognizes a corpus, not a language, and it cannot identify Greek from Linear B even at 5x LA's size, ranking it at best 5th and usually mid-table. A null for LA therefore says nothing about any family; it is not evidence against Greek, Semitic, Anatolian or any other candidate. A side result fits LA-5: LA's sequential structure is real (19x the shuffle s.d.) but weaker than LB's (about 70% of LB's gap) and absorbed less well than real words by every language. Not cracked. |

Working readings (no sign values proposed; map-free method):
- Grade C, demoted: "LA bigrams are closest to Afro-Asiatic syllable sequences". Killed by the ECHO rerun (LA-11.4b).
- Grade C, kept as a weak residue: Arabic, Coptic, Sanskrit, Hindi and Nahuatl absorb LA slightly better than absorbency predicts (residual +0.2 to +0.6 z in 4 of 4 cycles for the full LA set). Would support: the same residue with a size-matched model from a second corpus of each language. Would kill: absence in LAn (already partial: only Coptic stays) or under a fourth scheme.
- Method lesson: a free-map compression contest needs a positive control from a different corpus and spelling of the same language (here LB vs literary Greek). That control fails, so blind language-ranking claims for undeciphered syllabaries made this way lack demonstrated power.
