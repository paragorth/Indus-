# LA-13 cycle 2: dialect alignment by annealed sign maps (every word counts, no shared vocabulary needed)
Script: tools/la13_c2.py. Output: data/la13/c2_report.txt, c2_main.json, c2_planted.json.
Idea: if site B writes Y where the rest of Crete writes X, then B's words look improbable to a model trained elsewhere until Y is read as X.
For each target group B, a bigram model (word types, ^/$ boundaries, add-0.1) is trained on all other groups; a sign map
s: B-sign -> reference sign is annealed (20,000 moves, T 8 -> 0.05) to maximise sum log2 P_ref(s(w)) - 10 bits per non-identity entry.
| LA-<n>.<cycle> | method and control | result | verdict |
| LA-13.2a | Real anneal for each of 6 groups (HT, KH, ZA, PH, KN, OTH as target). Null: 100 pseudo-sites per target from site labels permuted within support type (same number of documents, same support mix), same anneal. LB control: PY vs KN+TH (LA-sized subsample), 100 nulls. | Total map gain, real vs null: HT 1,414 vs 1,120 (P 0.11); KH 50 vs 112 (P 0.92); ZA 142 vs 49 (P 0.04); PH 54 vs 32 (P 0.15); KN 109 vs 36 (P 0.04); OTH 549 vs 436 (P 0.08). Best single entry beats null nowhere (P 0.11-0.90). LB PY: 122 vs 122 (P 0.45). | No group survives correction for 6 targets (best P 0.04 x 6 = 0.24). The overall tilt (5 of 6 above the null mean) says real sites are more self-contained than random document sets of the same kind, as in LA-13.1b: a vocabulary/archive effect, not a located sign law. Unplanted LB (one Greek dialect) shows nothing, as it should. |
| LA-13.2b | Positive control: 3 substitutions planted at Khania (LA) or Pylos (LB) at rate 1.0 and 0.5, 3 draws each (18 rules per script). Recovery = the map sends the planted Y back to its true X; detection = Y is among the top-5 map entries. | Identification: LA 1/18, LB 5/18. Detection: LA 10/18 planted signs reach the top 5 (e.g. WI 62 bits, ZU 34 bits, NI 23 bits), LB 12/18. The map sends planted signs to frequent "absorber" signs (JA, DA, TA, TO, RO), not to their true source. | The method can FLAG a sign whose site use is anomalous (about 55% power at LA size) but cannot say what it corresponds to (identification <= 1/18 in LA, i.e. < 3/18 upper bound). So it cannot recover sound laws; it can only list site-anomalous signs. |
| LA-13.2c | The real site-anomalous signs (top map entries), read only as flags. | ZA: JU, KO, SE, MU, DE, E, ME, PI; KN: NU, PI, KE, ZA, PA; PH: O, PA3; KH: *805, *352 (rare, also flagged in every planted run = artefact), U, E; OTH: TU, KA, *301, NU, TE, U. | Grade C flag only (would support: the same signs flagged by an independent method, e.g. cycle-3 grid or the rewiring null; would kill: flag lists as long and as strong in the null pseudo-sites, which they are: best-entry P 0.13-0.90). Effectively no finding. |
| LA-13.2 | Cycle verdict | Sign-context alignment has detection power but no identification power at LA size; real sites are not more anomalous than random document sets after correction. | NO DIALECT RULE FOUND; the method is calibrated (planted detection ~55%), so at Khania (real best entry 14 bits vs planted 18-62 bits) a whole-site substitution of a mid-frequency sign is unlikely; elsewhere real flags sit inside the null range (weak upper bound, detection power only ~55%). |
