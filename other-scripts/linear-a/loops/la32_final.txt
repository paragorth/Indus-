# LA-32 final: "the slips tell how they wrote" (dictation = sound slips; copying = shape slips)
Scripts: tools/la32_common.py, la32_c1.py, la32_c2.py, la32_c2b.py, la32_c3.py, la32_c4.py. Checkpoints: data/la32_ckpt/ (git-ignored: fonts, matrices, OCR pairs, reports). Logs: loops/la32_cycle1-4.txt.
Prior check: LITERATURE.md, FINDINGS.md, STRATEGIES.md and a web search: the idea that LA tablets were composed from oral data because they look messy exists qualitatively; no shape-vs-sound confusion test was found.
Data: LA one-sign word pairs (163 of 3+ signs, 2,745 of 2+ signs); LB (DAMOS KN+PY) one-sign pairs (1,759 / 3,417); 29 SigLA vs lineara.xyz re-readings; English: 900 human misspellings, 955 OCR errors. Shape = 4 image metrics on Noto Sans Linear A/B glyphs; sound = la21 blind consonant rows (LB values only for the LB control and as an outside check). Null = partner shuffle keeping each sign's confusion count.

Grades: A (control) = LB one-sign variants are sound-structured, not shape-structured. B (calibrated negative) = no dominant slip mode in LA; upper bounds as above; the 'dictated from oral data' idea gets no support from slips. B = LA one-sign alternations are sound-structured under the blind rows (z 3.65-3.95 after the circularity control), about 1/4 of LB's strength.
