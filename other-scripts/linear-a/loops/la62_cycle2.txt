LA-62 cycle 2: can we transcribe new Linear A from images? Blind test, then the new documents
(tools/la62_c2.py; data/la62_ckpt/blind_pick.json, blind_readings.json, c2.json, new_docs.json; images in the scratchpad only)

(a) BLIND TEST. 10 administrative documents drawn at random (seed 62) from the 283 with a lineara.xyz facsimile drawing
and >= 4 tokens: HT88, HT25b, HT115a, HT87, HT94b, HT82, KH26, ZA12b, PK1, HTWa1021. Each drawing was read and the reading
(words with '?' for unread signs, logograms, numbers, a confidence per token) was saved BEFORE the corpus text was
opened. Control: the same readings scored against a random other document from the pool (200 draws each).
Caveat: HT88 is a much-published tablet; its 11/11 named signs probably come from memory, not from the drawing.

| LA-62.2a | Blind transcription of 10 known documents from facsimile drawings, scored against the corpus; control = same readings against random other documents (200 per document) | Numbers: 20 of 57 true number tokens recovered with the right value (35 %; control 7.2 = 15 %); 13 of 33 read numbers were wrong or spurious. Signs: 20 signs named, 18 right (HT88 11/11, suspect; others 7/9), but only 18 of 182 true signs were named at all (10 %; 7/152 = 5 % without HT88); control 3.5. Token-kind counts: total absolute error 58 vs control 92. Fraction signs: none recovered. | Our image transcription is NOT usable for sign-level work. It gives the layout and about a third of the numbers (above chance), and almost no syllabic readings (5 % of signs without HT88). Any transcription of a new document from images alone would be grade C at best, and the sign-group (word) layer, which the frozen predictions P6 and P7 need, would be missing. |

Cycle 2 verdict: with open material the outside documents can be read only as skeletons. Our own image
transcription, measured blind, names about 5 % of signs correctly (10 % if the suspect HT88 is counted). This is a hard
ceiling on any outside test until the editors' facsimiles (RILA-S1, Anetaki II) are in hand.
