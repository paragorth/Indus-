# LA-7 cycle 1: does the depicted object of a sign predict the commodity? (tools/la7_render.py, la7_common.py, la7_test.py, la7_power.py -> data/la7_picture_codes.json, la7_c1.out/json, la7_power.out)
# Rule for this line of work: Linear B sound values are ignored. Transliterations serve only as sign IDs.
# Novelty check: published work on Aegean acrophony argues sign by sign (e.g. AB30 = fig/NI, AB78 as a round object, DU/SU, "sematograms" in Linear A/B ideogram studies). We found no corpus-wide test of first-sign picture class against the adjacent commodity with a label-shuffle null and a Linear B control. This test is new to the log.
| id | method and control | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| LA-7.1a | Blind coding. 100 syllabic signs (>=3 tokens inside words) were rendered from Noto Sans Linear A under random codes (G105..G995). No value, frequency or context was shown. Each glyph was coded as plant, vessel, tool, animal, body, building or abstract, with a weak flag, and the codes were frozen in data/la7_picture_codes.json before any context was opened. Limit: the coder knows the signary, so recognition cannot be fully excluded. | abstract 41, building 14, tool 14, plant 12, body 9, vessel 7, animal 3. 45 of the 100 calls are weak. Predictions fixed beforehand: plant -> grain/tree/spice; vessel -> liquid (OLE, VIN); animal -> HIDE/CAP etc.; body -> VIR. | Codes frozen. Only 31 signs fall in a predictive class (12 plant, 7 vessel, 3 animal, 9 body). |
| LA-7.1b | LA test. Pairs: a word directly followed (same line, dividers skipped) by a commodity logogram, 200 pairs (GRA 78, CYP 36, OLE 24, VIN 22, VIR 21, AROM 7, OLIV 6, HIDE 5, CAP 1); 192 have a coded first sign. Statistic H = pairs whose first-sign class hits its predicted family; also MI(class; family). Null: permute picture labels among the 100 signs, class sizes kept (2,000 runs). Run on tokens, word-commodity types, single-sign words and multi-sign words, under full and strict coding (weak calls -> abstract). | Full coding: tokens H 23 vs 19.7 (P 0.33), MI P 0.60; types H 18 vs 16.5 (P 0.41); single-sign words 10 vs 5.5 (P 0.16); multi-sign 13 vs 14.7 (P 0.55). Strict coding: tokens 11 vs 7.8 (P 0.25); single-sign words 7 vs 2.1 (P 0.09); multi-sign 4 vs 5.5 (P 0.56). No MI test below P 0.36. | No effect. The single-sign-word excess (P 0.09 to 0.16) does not reach significance across about 16 LA tests. |
| LA-7.1c | Linear B control. The same codes were carried to LB through AB numbers. 3,066 DAMOS word + ideogram pairs (animal 968, person 786, grain 603, cloth 330, liquid 159, flax 127, spice 74, tree 19); 2,457 have a coded first sign. Same statistics and null. LA was also re-run on AB-numbered signs only, for a like-for-like comparison. | LB tokens: H 81 vs 133 (P 0.87). Types: 67 vs 87 (P 0.74). Single-sign: 7 vs 16 (P 0.65). All MI P >= 0.21. LA, AB signs only: tokens 21 vs 14.8 (P 0.22); types 16 vs 12.2 (P 0.24). | LB shows no effect, and is even below chance. LA sits slightly above its null, but the LA-LB contrast is not significant on its own, because LA P is only 0.22. |
| LA-7.1d | Power (planted control). On the real LA pairs, each token whose first sign is in a predictive class has its commodity redrawn from the predicted families with probability q. 60 replicates per q, 300-run permutation each. | Detected at P<0.05: q 0.1 0/60, q 0.2 6/60, q 0.3 34/60, q 0.5 60/60. | The test would see a picture effect if about 30% or more of predictive-class words obeyed it. An effect that weak or weaker cannot be excluded. Absence is an upper bound, not proof. |
