LINEAR A: ANCHORS AS ROLE PROBES (A) AND AFFIX PARADIGMS (B)
Tool: tools/attack_anchors.py. Numbers: data/attack_anchors.json.
Data: data/corpus.json (Linear A) and data/damos_items.jsonl (Linear B, DAMOS).
No published readings or interpretations were used. Seed 11, 1000 control draws.
Note: the "summary" block in the JSON was added by hand. Re-running the tool rewrites the file without it.

======================================================================
A. LINEAR B ANCHORS AS PLACE / PERSON PROBES
======================================================================

Step 1. Classifying the anchors in Linear B, by explicit rules.
- Place points:
  (a) a derived form exists in DAMOS: -de, -pi, -jo-de, or for 3+ signs -jo/-ja,
      or the last vowel changed to i plus -jo/-ja (pa-i-to -> pa-i-ti-ja).
  (b) the word is in 3+ documents with 3+ partners on the same line, and it
      co-occurs with a seed place more often than the base rate (0.061).
      Seed places are the 46 stems that occur with -de, plus ko-no-so, pa-i-to
      and ku-do-ni-ja.
- Person points:
  (c) the word starts an entry and is followed by VIR 1 or MUL 1.
  (d) the word starts an entry and is followed by a bare "1".
- "Total" words (half or more of their uses on the last line) count as other.
- Rule check inside Linear B: the person pattern hits 1 of 33 seed places,
  but 17 of 150 random words. The person rule avoids places, so it works roughly.

Result for the 12 anchors of 3+ signs:
- Place-like: PA-I-TO (53 docs, pa-i-ti-ja), SE-TO-I-JA (22 docs, se-to-i-jo,
  seed co-occurrence 0.14), SU-KI-RI-TA (su-ki-ri-ta-jo, su-ki-ri-ta-pi).
- Person-like: I-TA-JA (MUL 1), PA-RA-NE (bare 1).
- Unclear: DA-I-PI-TA, KI-DA-RO, TA-NA-TI, A-RO-TE, DA-MA-TE (follows to-sa),
  I-JA-TE, MA-TE-RE. Each of these has a single Linear B document.
- For 2-sign matches (weak, since about 3 in 4 are chance): 4 more place-like
  (A-PI, I-NA, PA-DE, TO-SA) and 7 more person-like (I-PI, MA-DI, PU-RA, RU-NA,
  SA-RI, TI-MA, WI-DU). TO-SA is very likely a quantity word. The rule misfires there.

Step 2. Slots in Linear A.
- Features per token: tablet heading; first word; followed by "1"; followed by
  a larger number or a fraction; no number; followed by a commodity sign;
  relative position; Hagia Triada or not; document class.
- Linear A tokens: place anchors 4 (PA-I-TO x2 at HT, SU-KI-RI-TA on a Phaistos
  nodule, SE-TO-I-JA on a Prassa stone vessel). Person anchors 3 (all HT tablets).

Step 3. Controls.
- (i) Random Linear A types matched for sign length and token frequency, 1000 draws.
- (ii) Place/person labels permuted among the anchors.

Results, place-like vs person-like (3+ sign anchors):
- Heading 0.25 vs 0.00.
- Followed by "1": 0.00 vs 0.33.
- Larger number: 0.50 vs 0.67.
- Commodity sign: 0.00 vs 0.33.
- Hagia Triada: 0.50 vs 1.00.
- Tablet: 0.50 vs 1.00.
- Every difference has p >= 0.24 against matched words and p >= 0.29 under permutation.
- With 5 types, the smallest possible permutation p is 0.10. This test cannot succeed.
- Adding the 2-sign matches (10 vs 15 tokens): still nothing (all p >= 0.13).
  Place-like words lean toward headings (0.40 vs 0.13) and objects (0.40 vs 0.13).
  Both are not significant.
- Site check: the three place-like anchors are in Linear B only at Knossos.
  In Linear A they turn up at HT, Phaistos and Prassa, all in central Crete.
  That fits, but there is no test here, because HT is 65% of the corpus.

Verdict A: NOT SHOWN.
- The anchors do not reveal a place slot vs a person slot in Linear A.
- The reason is lack of data (7 tokens), not evidence against such slots.
- We cannot yet sort unknown Linear A words into places and persons this way.
- New: a reproducible classification of the anchors that uses Linear B data only.
  PA-I-TO, SE-TO-I-JA and SU-KI-RI-TA are place-like in Linear B.
  I-TA-JA and PA-RA-NE are person-like.
- New: in Linear A, PA-I-TO is an entry with a number (6; 3 + fraction) on HT
  tablets, not a heading. If it is the same place, places can be list entries
  with quantities, as at Knossos (pa-i-to OVIS 1509).

======================================================================
B. AFFIX PARADIGMS
======================================================================

Productivity: is the stem attested both bare and with the affix?
- Control 1: a random sign of matched position frequency added to the same stems.
- Control 2: the sign's rank among signs of similar frequency.

| affix | stems | random-sign control | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| A- | 11 | 3.4 | <0.001 |
| I- | 8 | 3.4 | 0.02 |
| -JA | 7 | 3.1 | 0.03 |
| -ME | 6 | 3.0 | 0.07 |
| -TE | 6 | 3.1 | 0.10 |
| SI- | 5 | 3.4 | 0.25 |
| KI- | 5 | 3.5 | 0.28 |
| -RE | 5 | 3.1 | 0.21 |
| JA- | 1 | 3.5 | 0.98 |

- SI- and KI- still rank first among signs of similar frequency.
- JA- shows up only through the A-/JA- alternation, for 9 stems. Of these,
  SA-SA-RA, SA-SA-RA-ME and WA-PI have 2+ signs.
- 64 families (cores with 2+ forms). See paradigm_table in the JSON.

Feature ties.
- Each affix's forms were compared with the other members of the same families.
  Tested by permutation at the token level and at the type level, and against
  random-sign forms on the same stems.
- STRONG. -TE, -ME and JA- belong to stone/metal objects and vessels, not tablets:
  - -TE: objects 0.55 of types (0.72 of tokens), vs 0.23 for siblings and 0.27
    for random-sign forms. HT 0.09 vs 0.45. Type p = 0.014 (objects), 0.008 (HT).
  - JA-: objects 0.80 of types, vs 0.24 and 0.17. Type p = 0.012.
  - -ME: objects 0.50, vs 0.23 and 0.27. Type p = 0.07. Token level p < 0.001.
  - A- vs JA- on the same stems: JA- forms are on objects 0.77 of tokens and
    at HT 0.00. A- forms are on objects 0.50 and at HT 0.14.
  - So A-/JA- is not free variation. JA- is the form on objects.
- MODERATE. SI- forms always carry a number. None of 6 types lacks one, vs 0.51
  for siblings and 0.58 for random-sign forms (p = 0.006). Mostly larger numbers.
- WEAK. Below; about 99 comparisons were run, so these are probably noise:
  - -RE followed by "1": 0.36 vs 0.14 (p = 0.02).
  - KI- followed by a commodity: 0.44 vs 0.19 (p = 0.04).

Hold-out prediction.
- Families built from Hagia Triada only.
- For each family with 2+ forms, predict the missing common affixes
  (A-, KI-, I-, SI-, -JA, -RE). That gives 94 forms.
- Check them on all other sites: 1 hit (A-MI-TA, Zakros ZA 10b, predicted
  from MI-TA and SI-MI-TA).
- Random-sign control: mean 0.43, max 3, p = 0.35.
- Forecasts for new finds (all sites, stems with 2+ affixes) are in
  forecasts_for_new_finds. Examples: A-KI-RA, I-SA-SA-RA, A-SA-SA, TA-NA-JA, A-MA-RU.

Verdict B: PARTLY.
- A-, I- and -JA attach beyond chance, which confirms FINDINGS 2f with a stricter control.
- New: the affixes split by register.
  - -TE, -ME and JA- are object/vessel morphology (the libation-type texts).
  - SI- marks counted entries on tablets.
  - A-/JA- is conditioned by document type.
- Not shown: a predictive paradigm. Held-out sites give 1 hit, at chance.
- Caveats:
  - The object-register words are few and repeat a lot (the SA-SA-RA family), so
    token-level p-values overstate. The type-level p of 0.01 is the honest figure.
  - Only 35% of the data is outside Hagia Triada, so a hold-out has little to hit.
  - Absences are upper bounds only.
