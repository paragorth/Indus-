| Run | Method and control | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| V-12.F | (1) Fixed weaves: lag-k link bits, parity / mod-3 / mod-4 streams, streams carried over line breaks, glyph-class streams searched on a training fold. (2) Latent weave W-k (context = last word of the SAME hidden stream) vs an HMM H-k with identical parameters (context = previous word), k = 2 and 3, EM with random, parity and glyph-class starts, held-out by folio parity. (3) Tandem swaps: Hamming-1 neighbours by glyph pair at lags 1-3, phase by junction, second-order (A B A) information, decoded-stream entropy, P(switch) at swaps, position-preserving null. Controls: 4 planted weaves (verbose Latin + verbose filler, alternating and random switch; Latin + Italian; Voynich A-stream + B-stream), 4 single texts (verbose Latin, Italian, Dante; plain Latin), text+gloss pairs, line-shuffled Voynich | Planted weaves found: lag2/lag1 2.8-6.6 (single texts 0.09-0.35); W2 - H2 +0.98 (alternating filler), +0.13 to +0.29 (random switch, streams decoded 89% right, filler stream 5.6 vs 10.0 bits). **Voynich: lag2/lag1 0.16-0.18; parity streams over line breaks 0.01-0.02 bits (shuffle level); class splits give no gain; W2 - H2 -0.14 to -0.40 and W3 - H3 -0.26 to -0.28, the most negative of every corpus; second-order A B A information 0-6 millibits (lowest of all non-shuffled texts); decoded 'streams' differ by only 0.2-0.5 bits and are the Currier-like modes (daiin, dar vs qokeedy, qokedy)** | **Voynich lines are NOT woven from separable streams** under any scheme tried: odd/even, every 3rd or 4th word, by glyph class, by a hidden 2- or 3-state switch, continuous across lines or reset per line. Each word is best predicted by the word right before it, more so than in prose. There is no filler stream and no text+gloss pairing. Power limit: two weaved streams that look alike are invisible to the latent model, but they are caught by the lag test (lag 2 >> lag 1), which Voynich fails |
| V-12.S | Tandem swaps characterised | Lag-1 swap pairs above chance: l/r (93 vs 69), k/t (69 vs 49), l/o (32 vs 13), ch/cth (24 vs 13), ch/k; same in IT. a/o is not a one-glyph swap (34 vs 32). Swaps sit at lag 1 and lag 2 alike, do not raise the switch probability (0.15-0.19 at a swap vs 0.32-0.35 elsewhere), and show no odd/even phase. **One pair has the weave shape: ch/sh is enriched only at a one-word gap** (shedy _ chedy, chey _ shey, chol _ shol; ZL 63 vs 35 and IT 72 vs 35 under a position-preserving null, z 4.4 / 6.0; adjacent 44 vs 42; gap of 2 words 37 vs 37), mostly words 2-4 of a line, with a q-/o- word in the middle | The swaps are neighbour assimilation inside one stream, not stream boundaries. New grade-C lead: an 'sh-X ... ch-X' echo across one intervening (usually q-) word. Small (~30 extra pairs in ~30,000), transcription-robust. Would support: the same echo in held-out hands/sections and a preference for the order sh -> ch; would kill: disappearance within one Currier language or hand |

Plain statement: Voynich lines are not two or more interleaved texts. The tandem swaps, the junction link and the line reset are properties of a single word-to-word process that restarts at each line.
