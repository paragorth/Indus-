## Plain answer
On no test does the manuscript look more like notation than like language. Wherever chant gives a strong notation signal,
the Voynich gives prose-level values or less:

| fingerprint | chant (real notation) | Voynich ZL / IT | prose (verbose cipher) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| line-final symbol special to its unit (ratio last / mid-line word-final) | 1.9 | 1.0 / 1.0 | 0-1.4 (rhymed Dante 22) | language-like |
| closing line ends on the unit's usual final | z 10.3 | z 2.5 / 2.1 | z 1.1-3.6 | prose level |
| closing glyph enriched in its own unit (tonic echo) | +0.09 | 0.00 / -0.02 | 0.00 | absent |
| section/mode carried by the closing glyph vs a mid-line word-final glyph | 1.26 vs 0.13 bits | 0.05 vs 0.05 | - | absent |
| metre: periodic repeats blind to word breaks | none (local repeats only) | lag-6/7 peak, but it follows word length (neighbour similarity) | flat | no metre |
| word-length alternation | - | persistence (lag 1 +), no alternation | alternation | gibberish-like (known) |
| line length quantised into a unit | - | no (\|z\| < 1.7) | - | no |
| transposed phrases on a scale | scale recovered blind; x46-50 | x23-88, but word-shuffled Voynich x46-84 and no stable order | x27-33 | null (comes from word structure) |

## The one notation-like trace (small, transcription-robust, not explained)
V-4.1: the glyph that closes a PARAGRAPH can be predicted from what the paragraph contains, beyond section x Currier
language (z 3.1 ZL, 5.0 IT; accuracy 0.29-0.31 vs null 0.14-0.15), better than the final of line 2 or of the
penultimate line. Chant shows the same direction within mode. But the closing glyph is not over-represented in the
paragraph (no tonic echo, V-1.4) and carries no extra section information (V-2.4), so it is not a 'mode final'. Most
likely a layout effect (the last line is short and ends differently: y 47% vs 36%, m 10% vs 15%).
Would support notation: the effect survives matching closing lines to interior lines of the same length.
Would kill it: it vanishes under that length matching.

## Side finding (to follow up as 'concord', not melody)
V-3.4: adjacent words change a glyph in tandem more than chance (x1.2, z 4; trigram control x1.0): k/t, a/o, d/e, l/r,
ch/sh (okedy qokeey ~ otedy qoteey; al dal ~ ol dol). Neighbour words agree in gallows and similar glyphs. Chant does
not do this (x0.73), so it is not a musical trait. Novelty not checked.

## Verdict
The notation hypothesis, in the forms tested (modal finals, metre, transposition, section 'mode'), is not supported.
The Voynich line has start and end rules (known), but its end glyph behaves like a word-final glyph in prose, not like a
cadence. Nothing here is 'cracked'.
