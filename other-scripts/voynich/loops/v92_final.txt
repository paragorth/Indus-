GRADES
- A (method): a slot search finds the syntactic slot of page-recurring words in plain Konrad and Circa (held-out z 7-10), but sees it in only 2 of 5 texts through a Voynich-like surface. A spelling-transplant score separates real texts (0.57-0.73) from strong page glyph mood (0.77). Fade tests and writer-ABC have no positive control: real entries do not fade, and a name layer looks the same as seed vocabulary.
- B (measurement, frozen sha256 c2f77630..., three transcriptions): Voynich page-recurring frames can be put back on their own page from the spelling of the page's OTHER words with held-out accuracy 0.80-0.82, even when neighbouring pages are excluded. This is above every real herbal, recipe and astronomy text through the same surface (best 0.73), and only a strong drifting glyph-mood generator gets close (0.77).
- B (measurement): recurring frames are not clustered in neighbouring lines (real texts are), and few are confined to their page (0.03-0.04 vs 0.11-0.17).
- B (negative): no syntactic slot (0/60 survivors), within the method's limit.
- C (guess): each page (or run of pages) carries a spelling key, a page-level choice among variant spellings. The frames that recur on a page are common words in that page's key, not page-specific names. Would support: normalising each page's spelling by its own glyph profile (a learned page key) makes the same words recur ACROSS pages of a section, raising cross-page word sharing above shuffles, and the v90 page-recurrence excess falls below 2. Would kill: after removing the page glyph profile, the v90 frame excess stays at z >= 3.7, or a real text with a known spelling convention reaches 0.80 transplant accuracy.
- Demoted: page-recurring frames as names of things (from the v90 open question) to C-. Six candidates logged; K2 and K5 remain untested, with K5 close to v76.
- Not cracked. Nothing is predicted outside the corpus.
