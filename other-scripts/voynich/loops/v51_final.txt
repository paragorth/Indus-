Idea: change the medium. Render text as sound under thousands of random phone mappings (glyph rank -> random vowel/nasal/liquid/fricative/stop, random durations, pitch, declination, stress rule) with a numpy formant synthesiser, and ask a speech recogniser trained only on human speech (99 languages, 39M parameters) whether it hears speech: no-speech probability, language-ID entropy/confidence, transcription-token confidence, syllable-boundary peakiness, 3-8 Hz rhythm. A literature/web check found no prior rendering-and-listening test of the Voynich text.
Compute: 565 random mappings, 18,000 rendered 6 s clips, 2 CPU workers.

Grades:
- A: none.
- B: the rendered Voynich carries the same small speech-likeness over its own shuffle as natural languages and as its Markov and copy generators (z ~1-3); the listener cannot separate them.
- B: no random mapping makes the Voynich generalisably speech-like on held-out text; the listener cannot pick out glyph vowel classes (its control fails on known alphabets).
- B: no reliable section or hand ordering (stars/recipes and hand 1 highest nominally; pharma lowest; split-half rho 0.18).
- C (demoted): generator-like score profile. Would have supported: lex down / language-ID up on fresh mappings. Killed: Voynich lex z -0.0 on 245 fresh mappings, languages inconsistent.
Why it failed (for anyone retrying): random phone mappings destroy the vowel/consonant alignment that carries prosody; what survives is the unit-transition structure, which a Markov chain already has. A listener would need a mapping that respects the hidden phone classes, and the search could not find one even for Latin.
