v63 WHICH TWIN IS THE SLIP? cycle 1 (5 Oct 2026): orientation of adjacent repeats against 1,775 real struck words
Tools: tools/v63_lib.py, v63_slipcat.py, v63_plant.py, v63_c1.py, v63_c1b.py. Checkpoints: data/v63_ckpt/ (git-ignored; Plaoul TEI clone, slipcat.json, c1*_metrics.json, logs)
Prior-art check and change of idea: the brief (rebuild the lost exemplar's line width from dittography and eye-skip) was already run in-house as v17
('Ghost of an exemplar': 16,800 widths x phases + flexible DP, planted Latin dittography recovered at width 34 vs true 33.2; Voynich p 0.54-0.98).
A web search found only forum lists of scribal error types (voynich.ninja thread 61; ciphermysteries 2014 'errors') and no controlled test. So the
idea was turned round: instead of asking WHERE the exemplar's lines ended, ask WHICH TWIN of each adjacent pair is the mistake. A copyist who
writes a slip and then the intended word (never struck: the Voynich has almost no strikes, v24) leaves an ordered pair; a copy-and-modify
generator leaves the opposite order (source first, mutant second); a language or a symmetric generator leaves no order. Real reference: the
SCTA diplomatic TEI of Petrus Plaoul (5 witnesses, 1.04 M words, manuscript line breaks), walked letter by letter so that the scribes' FIRST
writing (struck words kept) and the corrected text are both available.
