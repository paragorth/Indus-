# v60 final (5 Oct 2026): every medieval herb is hot or cold, wet or dry
External key measured from six herbals (Old French Circa instans, Banckes 1552, English Macer 1543, Lyte 1578, Gerard 1633,
Pechey 1694; TCP and archive.org texts in data/v60_ckpt/, git-ignored): hot-dry 0.65-0.80 of entries in every source, hot
share 0.74-0.85, odds ratio hot~dry about 3.7, stated degree 2nd > 3rd > 1st ~ 4th; position at the head (Circa instans) or at
head/end (Banckes, Macer). Hot vs cold is predictable from the rest of an entry (AUC 0.60-0.79); dry vs moist is not.

Voynich (ZL, IT2a) = Markov, star and recipe nulls | A: the 2x2 grid is too low-information to find a slot; Voynich uninformative |
| V-60.F.2 | Cycle 2: 0.5 million complexion-shaped binary labels per corpus, confirmed by held-out predictability from the rest of the paragraph | Control: true slot enriched (top 0.1-1%), not singled out. | V-60.F.V | Overall | No complexion slot survives. Grade C (open): a complexion slot written as a rare or abbreviated sign, or stated per page rather than per paragraph, is not excluded.
