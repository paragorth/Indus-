Hypothesis: some Voynich words are pointers whose payload frame (o a i n y l r + gallows) encodes the address of another page.
Score: the context of the pointer must share rare payload skeletons with the target page beyond its section, Currier language and
distance band, and beyond a shuffle of the same targets. Held out: word types never seen in training. About 2.5e8 rule
evaluations in total (additive, alphabetic-numeral, positional base 2-12; absolute/relative; page/folio/quire; plain, marked and
table pointers; current and re-nested bindings).

Results
- Grade A (method): a residual-sum score lets free rules overfit, and a page split lets topical words pose as pointers. The final
  score (top-10 hit, unseen types, type-robust z, pairing correction) fixes both. A planted 2-per-page positional code is recovered
  exactly in 1 of 4 full blind runs (held-out z 5.9 vs null max 4.9), and the true rule is order-specific (6.6 vs 2.3).
- Real cross-references (Culpeper's herbal, as Roman numerals with Voynich-like padding) are BELOW detection (true rule held-out
  <= 2.8), with or without a "vide" marker. This bounds the negative.
- Grade B negative: no arithmetic pointer rule in ZL or IT2a beats relabelled, Markov or self-citation nulls (ZL absolute 3.50,
  relative 3.15; IT2a 2.95/3.70; null maxima 4.91/5.67). Cross-reference graph: empty. No bifolio order supported (Grade C, partial
  coverage).
- Grade B measurement (new): Voynich words tie pages together topically 5-10x more weakly than two real herbals (table z 4.5-10 vs
  52-57; specificity ~0 vs 11-13). The padded Culpeper control still scores 43-57, so padding alone does not explain the gap.
  Grade C reading: pages behave like independent records, not a cross-referenced treatise. Would kill: a real herbal matched to the
  Voynich type/token ratio scoring about 5.
