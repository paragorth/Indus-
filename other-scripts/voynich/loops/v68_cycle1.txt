v68 THE MELODY IS THE PLAINTEXT, cycle 1 (5 Oct 2026): chant vs language written through random codes
Novelty: the brief's idea (Voynich glyphs = notes, scored for chant-likeness) was already run in-repo in v5 (GregoBase chant, finals,
modes, transposition, blind scale search; negative), and the web shows only forum talk (voynich.ninja threads 3968, 5724). So the idea
was turned round: the melody is what was WRITTEN, not what the glyphs ARE. v53 showed language encoders cannot reproduce three Voynich
traits (line-edge pools, word-junction coupling, adjacent repeats); a melody supplies such traits for free (intonations and finals,
stepwise continuity across syllables, repeated notes). Does chant written through random codes reach the Voynich where language does not?
Data: GregoBase chant (dump 2019-10-24, github.com/bacor/gregobasecorpus commit ad5060fe, CC0; 5,840 deduplicated pieces, 768k syllables,
modes 1-8), parsed in v5. Language: v53 corpora (Latin encyclopedic herbal, Italian herbal, German herbal).
Tools: tools/v68_lib.py, tools/v68_c1.py, tools/v68_c1_report.py. Checkpoints: data/v68_ckpt/c1_* (git-ignored). 2 workers.
