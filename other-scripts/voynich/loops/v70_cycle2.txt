v70 cycle 2 (6 Oct 2026): do ink alphabets track pages better than EVA? Tools: tools/v70_c2.py. Out: data/v70_ckpt/c2_V.json, c2_SV.json, c2_L.json.
Task: page of a held-out line (naive Bayes on unit unigrams + bigrams inside words; train on even lines, test on odd and the reverse). Voynich: the 23 Q20 pages (chance 0.043). Each corpus: 78 k-means ink alphabets (3 thetas x K 10-58 step 4 x 0/8 merges) against 78 matched random-Voronoi alphabets (same page-centred embedding, so the same ink drift) and against the reference transcription of the same words (EVA; true letters for Latin). Drift baseline: page from the line's mean unit embedding (no alphabet).

Verdict cycle 2: Grade B (negative): no ink alphabet of 10-58 units tracks Voynich pages better than EVA (0/78; best 0.231 vs 0.267), while the same pipeline on a known hand keeps only ~0.74 of the true-unit information, so 'beating EVA' was within reach only if EVA lost more than ~25% of the page signal. It does not lose that much. Grade C: the Voynich pages (Q20 hand) give ink alphabets no page-identity bonus, unlike six Latin hands (would support: a multi-hand Voynich sample, e.g. herbal A pages of hands 1 and 2, shows the Latin-like bonus; would kill: none on such a sample).
Next (cycle 3): choose the alphabet with no language prior (MDL on self-supervised codes); read off which EVA pairs the pen actually joins; test whether an ink-informed EVA tracks sections better.
