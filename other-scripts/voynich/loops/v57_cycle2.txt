# v57 cycle 2 - WHERE THE PEN RAN DRY at sub-word resolution (tools/v57_c2.py; data/v57_ckpt/c2.json; traces from Yale IIIF and CREMMA images in the scratch cache only)
# Method: per text line a column-by-column ink-darkness trace (mean contrast of ink pixels in a band around the v18 line track: 1,152 Voynich lines, 915 Latin lines with 1,611 punctuation gaps). Step detector = median darkness in the next w ink columns minus the previous w (w = 1.5, 3, 6 glyph widths; top 20/10/3% of |step|; gap tolerance 0.5 or 1 glyph). Content changes make darkening and lightening steps alike, a re-dip makes a darkening; statistic A = (dark - light)/(dark + light). Null: random per-line mirror (4,000 / 1,000 flips).
| V-57.2.1 | Latin control: where do darkenings fall? | At word gaps A +0.07 to +0.31 (z 2.4-5.9 in 15/18 settings); inside words A ~0 (-0.07 to +0.03); darkening events are over-represented at gaps (24-27% of events vs 17% of trace at tol 0.5). Punctuation gaps A +0.12 to +0.39 (z 2.2-4.2) | PASS: a Latin scribe's darkening steps sit at word gaps, not inside words. Re-dips (or fresh pen pressure) happen between words |
| V-57.2.2 | Voynich, same 18 settings | At word gaps A +0.11 to +0.31 (z 3.4-12.0) with 1-glyph tolerance, weaker with 0.5 (+0.01 to +0.20; box alignment is looser than in Latin). Inside words A NEGATIVE: -0.07 to -0.18 (z down to -10) at 1-glyph tolerance | Voynich darkenings fall between words, as in Latin: Voynich 'words' are pen units. Inside words ink LIGHTENS more than it darkens, unlike Latin (A ~0): a Voynich word starts dark and fades toward its end. Grade B (measurement); cycle 4 (V-57.4.1) shows most of it is word shape, so 'pen units' is C |
| V-57.2.V | Verdict cycle 2 | - | Re-dips (darkening steps) are at word boundaries in both scripts; none concentrate inside Voynich words, so no hidden sub-word unit boundary shows up in the ink. The Latin control does NOT put more darkenings at punctuation than at other gaps once the dark/light balance is compared directly (cycle 3, V-57.3.3), so the brief's premise (re-dips at clause ends) is not calibrated by real Latin |
