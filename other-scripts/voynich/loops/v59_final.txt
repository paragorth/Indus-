VERDICT: NO ROSETTA STONE INSIDE THE BOOK. Nothing was cracked.
- The surviving transducer: none. Neither a random rule set nor the v30 ending shift (-chy/-chor/-chol -> -chedy/-edy,
  word-initial cth cut) makes A words appear in B's line contexts on held-out pages. Grade B: the A->B change is a
  change of form, and in this score it does not carry meaning with it.
- Which glyph positions are kept: from the subject test, the first three glyphs of a word carry the shared
  herbal vocabulary across A and B (AUC 0.76 / 0.81, as good as whole words), and the last three do not (0.56 / 0.64).
  Grade C: word beginnings are kept and endings are re-encoded between A and B. This fits v30 and v54, where the
  rewriting sits in the endings and the e-run. Would support: a B->A map that rewrites only endings, learned on
  some pages, raising subject transfer on disjoint B pages. Would kill: endings-only maps doing no better than
  random ending maps.
- The implied A-B word pairs: none survive the nulls. daiin->chedy is a frequency artefact.
- Shared words keep their usage profile across A/B about as well as two German dialects writing different texts
  (context consistency 0.05 vs A-A 0.155; Bav->Alem 0.07). So A and B are distant in usage, not only in spelling.
  Grade B measurement.
