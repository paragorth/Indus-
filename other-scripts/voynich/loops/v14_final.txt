# v14 final: "the words were rolled, not written"

Question: if a scribe produced Voynich words by dice (one die per slot, sum of two dice, die + lookup table, coin + die, astragali), the
slot-filler frequencies would sit on an arithmetic lattice (k/6, k/12, k/36, triangular sums, astragalus .1/.4/.4/.1) and the slots would be
independent. Tested on the v7 4-slot template (73% of ZL3b tokens).
Files: tools/v14_lib.py, v14_cycle1.py, v14_cycle2.py, v14_cycle3.py, v14_cycle3b.py, v14_cycle3c.py, v14_report.py, v14_report3.py;
loops/v14_cycle1-3.txt; checkpoints data/results/v14/.
Data: ZL3b paragraph text (main), IT2a (same pages = transcription-robust only). Prior work: none found that fits dice laws to slot
frequencies; Rugg 2004 (table + grille) is a generator, not a fit, and v9 already rejected volvelles.

Plain answer: No randomising device (d6, coin + d6, two dice through a table, 2d6 or 3d6 sums, 2d6 products, one or two astragali, with
any merging of faces) fits the Voynich slot frequencies better than chance, in the whole text, in Currier A or B, in any large section or in
IT2a. And the slots are not independent, as dice would make them: within-word slot MI is 0.12-0.30 bits (dice 0.000-0.001; a half-rolled
text 0.03-0.12), every section and language shows it, the end of one word predicts the start of the next, and a mixture of dice tables
never plateaus. The Voynich behaves like natural text under verbose substitution on every test here. The words were not rolled.
Side observation (grade C, untested further): a 16-class product-of-slots model leaves less residual slot dependence in the Voynich
(0.045 bits) than in verbose Latin/Italian (0.10-0.12): the Voynich vocabulary is closer to a few dozen families of freely-combining slots
than enciphered prose is. Would support: the same in IT2a and within A and B separately; would kill: equal residuals once the
controls' cipher is made as slot-regular as the Voynich (73% template coverage).
Nothing here is a decipherment.
