# v86 final (7 Oct 2026): THE SCRIBE UNDER PRESSURE LEAKS THE CODE? (squeezed positions as natural compression experiments)
Data: ZL3b and IT2a (VOY_MODE=glyph), paragraph text; drawing intrusions from IVTFF '<->'/'<~>'. Tools: tools/v86_lib.py, v86_c1.py, v86_c2.py, v86_c3.py, v86_c3b.py, v86_c3c.py, v86_c3d.py. Rows: loops/v86_cycle1-3.txt. Checkpoints: data/v86_ckpt/ (git-ignored).
Prior art: glyph-rate shifts at line ends and drawings (arXiv 2404.13069; v34 crowding; v61 base forms) were not re-run. New here: ~4,000 random compression rules per corpus (about 430,000 rule scorings over 65 corpus x slot x control runs) scored by how uniquely they RECOVER squeezed out-of-vocabulary tokens from the page's unsqueezed vocabulary, chosen on half the folios and re-scored on the other half.
Controls: Latin, Italian and German laid into the Voynich page/line/intrusion template, with planted medieval abbreviation (found: held-out +0.019 to +0.037; filler class = vowels, mark = '~') and without (+0.006 at most); Voynich plants on position-shuffled text: meaning-preserving compression (found, +0.055 to +0.072) vs random habit edits (+0.005 at most); kill controls: words permuted inside lines (8+3 seeds), re-dealt over the page (4 seeds), shuffled sections.

Answer: squeezing does not leak a code. The only thing that changes recoverably at the edge of the line is final r/l -> m, and it does so whether or not space is short. Not cracked.
