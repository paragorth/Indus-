v70 THE TRANSCRIPTION ALPHABET IS WRONG; LET THE INK DECIDE, cycle 1 (5-6 Oct 2026). Tools: tools/v70_lib.py (unit cutting, patches, battery), tools/v70_synth.py (synthetic stroke hand), tools/v70_build.py, tools/v70_c1.py, tools/v70_c1_report.py, tools/v70_c1b.py. Out: data/v70_ckpt/c1_*.json, c1_report.json, c1b.json (git-ignored). Patches and embeddings stay in the scratch cache.
Prior art (web, 5 Oct 2026): unsupervised clustering of cipher glyph images exists (Histocrypt "Unsupervised alphabet matching in historical encrypted manuscript images"; Tartu thesis on automatic alphabet identification; Yin et al. "Decipherment of historical manuscript images"), aimed at matching a cipher's symbols to a known alphabet or at decipherment. No study was found that generates thousands of ink-derived Voynich alphabets and scores them for language-likeness and page tracking against matched random alphabets, a real Latin manuscript control and generator texts in a Voynich-like hand. Our own earlier merge work (V1 byte-pair merges on EVA, v35 twin merges, v28 split/merge EVA variants) started from EVA, not from the ink.

Corpora. V: 11,599 words on 25 Beinecke pages (Q20 + f58), v18 word boxes (ink-gap blobs; their alignment to the transliteration uses word counts only). L: 9,583 words on 28 CREMMA-Medieval-Lat pages (6 MSS, diplomatic, ~30 abbreviation signs). Synthetic, rendered in a jittered Voynich-like stroke hand with 18% touching glyphs and joined benches: SV (the same Q20 + f58 ZL text, truth = EVA units), SG_self / SG_grille / SG_mk3 (self-citation, table-and-grille and glyph-trigram Markov generators trained on that text), SL_la / SL_lac / SL_it / SL_es / SL_de / SL_cs (Isidore, Caesar, Manzoni, Cervantes, Kafka, Czech; 9,000 words each, fixed letter->shape key, two planted ligatures = top letter bigrams drawn joined, one planted split letter drawn as two separate strokes).
Units: word band +-0.6 pitch, ink mask, cut at blank columns (theta 0) or also at column minima <= 2.5 / 4 px (pitch-scaled); 24x32 patch + width; PCA 30 centred per page (removes page ink/scan drift). Units per word: V 3.2 / 3.8 / 4.8 (EVA glyphs 4.8); L 1.75 / 2.2 / 3.4 (letters ~5.5: the Latin book hands join most letters).
Alphabets: k-means at K = 10..60 (step 2), 2 seeds (1 for SL), 3 thetas, then 0/4/8/12 ligature merges (most frequent adjacent pair) = 1,248 ink alphabets per corpus (624 for SL), and as many matched NULL alphabets (random Voronoi cells in the same embedding, same K and merges): 11,232 ink alphabets + 11,232 nulls. Battery on page-half A and held-out half B: normalised h1, h2/h1, h3/h1, word-length CV, Zipf slope, type/token, hapax share, first/last-unit entropy ratio, effective alphabet size.

Verdict cycle 1: the ink pipeline works (units recovered at NMI ~0.7 vs 0.5 random, ligatures partly, split letters re-joined) and the battery separates languages from generators when the hand is held constant. The Voynich text in a constant hand reads as generator-like under every ink alphabet (SV 0.000 against 0.91-1.00 for six languages). But the premise 'a better alphabet makes the text more language-like' fails its own controls: true units lower the language score in all six languages. Grade A (method, negative): language-likeness cannot choose an alphabet. Grade B: no ink alphabet of 10-60 units moves the Voynich text (constant hand) toward language. Real-scan scores are domain-shifted and uninformative.
Next (cycle 2): a criterion that does not reward noise. Page tracking of held-out lines against EVA and matched random alphabets, plus the EVA anatomy of the ink units.
