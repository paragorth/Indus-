## Graded readings
- A (method): through a random meaning-preserving encoder, plaintext genre (list vs running text) is not recoverable from surface statistics (AUC 0.62 in-prior, 0.50-0.52 under a richer or out-of-prior encoder). Vocabulary richness and word length are faintly recoverable (r 0.2-0.45). The encoder family is recoverable (71% vs 33% chance). Any 'genre of the Voynich plaintext' claim from surface statistics should be treated as uncalibrated.
- A (method): extrapolating vocabulary growth from 2,000 to 10,000 tokens through accepted encoders and plaintexts is accurate on planted texts (TTR r 0.975, MAE 0.039; hapax MAE 0.061).
- B (negative): the Voynich posterior over plaintext genre, vocabulary size and word length is the prior. The list lean (+0.02) flips sign under a second encoder prior (-0.03 to -0.04). The vocabulary lean (460-478 vs 508 types/1000) vanishes, and a meaningless trigram copy reproduces it. The frozen posterior (sha256 edbc3c1b...) is recorded as killed.
- B: no random meaning-preserving encoder (22,000 in two priors) reproduces the low last-glyph entropy (2.4 vs 3.7 bits), the positive length correlation of neighbouring words (+0.12 vs -0.01), adjacent repeats (0.010 vs 0.001) or the line-first-glyph shift (JSD 0.24 vs 0.02). The real Voynich fits the bank worse than its own glyph shuffle (distance 1.59 vs 1.11).
- B (prediction failed): vocabulary growth. At 10,000 tokens the Voynich has fewer types (0.24-0.31) than its own chunks predict through any accepted encoding (0.30-0.36). More of those types are used once (0.68-0.69 vs 0.55-0.62; above the 80% band in 5 of 6 windows, at its edge in the sixth). Both transcriptions agree.
- C+ (within this encoder space): if the text is an encoding, every section uses the same kind of encoder. Between-section encoder-family differences are 0.08-0.14 (base prior) and 0.11-0.19 (wide prior), against 0.39 for a scheme change. Bio B leans most to whole-word codes and fits worst. Would support: the same stability under a third, structurally different encoder prior (e.g. verbose syllabaries with positional tables) and in GC2a. Would kill: any section pair reaching scheme-change level (>= 0.39), or a single meaningless generator with section drift producing the same pattern of stability while planted scheme changes are still detected.
- C: the Voynich has a productive layer. It makes new single-use word forms from a small recurring core (variant spelling, combinatorial word building, or qualifiers and numbers fused to words) at a rate no fixed codebook over a real plaintext gives. Would support: an encoder with a productive variant or compounding layer, fitted only on 2,000-token chunks, predicts the 10,000-token TTR and hapax share inside its band in all windows of ZL3b, IT2a and GC2a, while plain codebooks still miss. Would kill: plain codebooks reaching the 10,000-token hapax share once the prior includes high-homophony letter codes (homophones 4-6 were in the wide prior but not in this test), or the hapax excess disappearing on three-way-agreed words (transcription noise making one-off forms).
- C- (demoted): 'the plaintext is a list' and 'the plaintext has a small vocabulary' as readings from surface statistics.
