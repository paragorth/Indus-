Question: the strongest surviving Voynich feature (v77 #1, C+) is the chain of line-initial glyphs: one glyph wide, first-order, made by no generator, not language (v6/v10/v50). Is it a non-linguistic margin channel of a kind medieval books carry?
Calibration: real channels written into an opaque Voynich-like surface (v72 verbose code + v72 surface; the channel glyph replaces the line-start marker on 70-90% of lines; glyphs from the Voynich line-initial pool): Dante's verse numbering, a Julian calendar's dominical letters and golden numbers, Brumati's running entry numbers, part-of-entry marks and Linnaean class letters, a genus key, Isidore's chapter index and an index column (initial of the line's rarest word). Five generators fitted to ZL3b must, and did, give nothing.

What the chain behaves like: a mark chosen to DIFFER from the mark above (exactly as strongly as a bare 'not the previous mark' habit), with a few favoured successions (q>ch, o>q, y>o, s>p, t>s) that do not chain into any cycle. It holds no count, period, calendar rule, entry number, line class, catchword, index letter or detectable item key, while each of these, when real and written into the same opaque surface, is recovered with its rule. The rule, as far as it exists: 'pick a line-opening mark unlike the one above, preferring a short table of successors'. Grade B: this is a scribal alternation habit or a layout device, not a data channel. Grade C (open): a cross-page reference key (power too low here); a channel whose symbol is spread over more than the first glyph (v6/v77 found nothing beyond one glyph).
Meaning extraction: none from this channel. If meaning is carried, it is not in the left margin; the margin chain should be treated as a layout layer to peel off (as v72's surface does) before searching the payload.
Not cracked.
