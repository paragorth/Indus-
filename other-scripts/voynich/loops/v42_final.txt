v42 final: THE VOYNICH AMONG DELIBERATELY MEANINGLESS BOOKS (4 cycles; tools/v42_lib.py, v42_feats.py, v42_cycle1.py, v42_battery.py, v42_cycle2.py, v42_cycle2b.py, v42_cycle2c.py, v42_cycle3.py, v42_cycle4.py; checkpoints data/v42_ckpt/, git-ignored; the CS transliteration and all downloads stay in the scratchpad, v42/)

Idea: compare the Voynich with a new reference class, long illustrated books of deliberately meaningless text written by one hand over months, and ask whether such writing drifts toward generator-like statistics.
Data: the only long asemic book with a machine-readable transliteration found is the Codex Seraphinianus (Serafini 1976-78, declared asemic by its author): M. Ponzi's neural-network transliteration (github.com/marcoponzi/codex_seraphinianus_ocr; 303 pages, 44,290 tokens after dropping '?' and numeral tokens; 33 stroke-level units; validation character error 18%). Not obtainable: the Rohonc Codex (no public transcription; meaning disputed), Xu Bing's Book from the Sky (no transcription; characters are mostly unique), other long asemic manuscripts (images only). Short gibberish = the 37 Gaskell & Bowern writers. Ciphers = Copiale, Borg (v35). Languages, conlangs, generators, voces magicae = the v31 corpora.
Novelty (web, 4 Oct 2026): Melka & Stanley 2012 measured ~200 hand-transliterated CS words; Ponzi 2023 (Medium) compared his CS transliteration with the Voynich descriptively (entropy, MATTR, reduplication, line-initial glyphs, two 'volumes' like Currier A/B). Not found: a calibrated multi-class placement with noise-matched controls, a hub test, an ASEMIC class with kill tests, gap ratio / arrows / forgery detectability on the CS, or a hardening test.
Noise control: because the CS is machine-read at 18% character error, every other corpus was also passed through an OCR-like channel (substitution by the unit's context twin, deletion, duplication, word split/merge) at 9% and 18% ('n09', 'n18').

Where the Voynich falls: not with languages, ciphers, conlangs or spells; on texture between generators and short gibberish (v31 repeated); on the classic battery and in nearest-neighbour terms next to the Codex Seraphinianus, the one long asemic book available, which itself is classed 'generator' by most survivors. It differs from that book in one respect that matters: the CS has page topic and is easy to forge-detect; the Voynich has no topic and is harder to tell from its forgeries than any other text tested.
Limits: one reference book, machine-read at 18% character error in a stroke scheme chosen by its transcriber; three unit schemes and a noise-matched channel bracket this but cannot replace a hand transcription. Sampling: absences are upper bounds (0 of 91 absorbed -> < 3.3%).
