# Voynich MS 408: the world it came from (data and dates, not readings)

Compiled 4 Oct 2026. Facts only; no one's decipherment is used.

## Object
- **Parchment 1404–1438** (95%). Four folios (f8, f26, f47, f68) carbon-dated at Arizona, 2009; combined uncalibrated age 1434 ± 18 years. This dates the skin, not the writing. [voynich.nu/extra/carbon](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/carbon.html)
- **Inks (McCrone 2009):** one iron-gall ink for both text and drawings, and different inks for folio numbers, quire numbers and the Latin alphabet on f1r. [ciphermysteries summary](https://ciphermysteries.com/2011/06/01/voynich-the-mccrone-report-now-online)
- **Make-up:** about 225 × 160 mm. There were 20 quires (about 116 folios); 18 quires and 102 folios survive, so 14 folios are lost. Several fold-outs, the largest about 45 × 45 cm. [voynich.nu/descr](https://www.voynich.nu/descr.html)
- **Five scribes** (Davis 2020, *Manuscript Studies* 5(1), [doi](https://doi.org/10.1353/mns.2020.0011)).
- **Later additions:** month names were added to the zodiac pages after the main text. They are in a Romance hand ("aberil", "mars", "may"), whose dialect is disputed between Occitan, Catalan and northern French. The f116v marginalia mix German-looking words with Voynich glyphs. [voynich.ninja 4745](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-4745-6.html)
- **Drawings:** a castle with swallow-tail merlons appears on the rosettes fold-out; this crenellation is common in northern Italy and the Alps.

## Provenance chain (documented links only)

|---|---|---|

He lived in the house of Leonhard Rauwolf, a botanist who travelled in the Orient | sale to Rudolf II on 1 March 1599, for 500 thalers (= 600 florins), of "rare books" shipped in a small barrel (Guzy 2022) [voynich.nu](https://www.voynich.nu/curricula.html), [Widemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Widemann) |

Route: Augsburg → Prague → Rome. Nothing places the book before 1599.

## The 1404–1438 world: events and routes
- **Prague:** after the Kutná Hora decree of 1409, German masters left Prague to found Leipzig (1409). The Hussite wars followed (1419–34). Books left Prague for Saxony, Bavaria and Austria.
- **Church councils:** Constance (1414–18) and Basel (1431–49) were Alpine meeting points for scholars, physicians and the book trade, and they fall squarely inside the carbon window. Poggio's 1416 letter from **Baden (Aargau)** describes mixed bathing in linen shifts. [Swiss National Museum](https://blog.nationalmuseum.ch/en/2022/03/the-baths-at-baden-switzerlands-first-tourist-hotspot/)
- **Prague:** a bath-maid with tub recurs in the Wenceslas Bible (1390s). [Wenceslas Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslas_Bible)
- **Kyeser's *Bellifortis* (1402–05):** written in exile in Eichstätt by illuminators who had left Prague, and full of astrology and magic. [Bellifortis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellifortis)
- **Křišťan of Prachatice** (d. 1439): Prague physician and university rector. He wrote on the astrolabe (c. 1407), bloodletting and plague, and a herbal of about 350 entries with Czech plant names. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_of_Prachatice)
- **Johannes Hartlieb** (c. 1410–68): Bavarian court physician, trained in Padua. Wrote a German herbal, a memory art and on forbidden arts. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Hartlieb)
- **Cipher practice:** a Mantuan key of 1401 gives several shapes for each vowel, the earliest Western homophones ([ciphermysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/2016/07/06/fifteenth-century-cryptography)). Fontana's *Bellicorum instrumentorum liber* (c. 1420, BSB Cod.icon. 242) enciphers captions in invented signs. DECODE holds 1,000+ keys (15th–18th c.), 41% transcribed. [DECODE v2](https://ecp.ep.liu.se/index.php/histocrypt/article/view/397)
- **Scripts in the region:** Glagolitic at Prague's Emmaus monastery (1347), incl. Old Czech in Glagolitic ([Emmaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmaus_Monastery)). Hus's diacritic spelling reform of Czech dates to about 1406–12.
- **Invented and occult writing:** Hildegard's Lingua Ignota (1,011 coined nouns, 23 *litterae ignotae*; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_ignota)). The *Ars notoria* has "notae" and prayers in voces magicae ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Notoria)). Lull's *Ars* used combinatory wheels ([Llull DB](https://www.ub.edu/llulldb/)). Tironian notes, a shorthand of about 13,000 signs, survived as notarial notae ([Schmitz 1893](https://archive.org/details/commentariinotar00schm)).
- **Medical genres:** herbals, the *Tacuinum sanitatis* (from Ibn Butlan's Arabic *Taqwim*), spa treatises, bloodletting and lunar calendars, the Trotula.

## Control corpora we can actually download
- **Early New High German (ReF), 1350–1650**, diplomatic transcriptions with dialect regions. East Upper German (Bavarian) texts of 1350–1450 are included. [linguistics.rub.de/ref](https://linguistics.rub.de/ref), [data](https://talar.sfb833.uni-tuebingen.de/erdora/cmdi/DATENZENTRUM/fnhd.UP)
- **Old Czech Text Bank**, 312 sources, 6.7 M tokens, covering both pre-Hus and post-Hus spelling. [Vokabulář](https://korpus.vokabular.ujc.cas.cz/first_form?corpname=SSTB-1.1.11.1)
- **Medieval Latin:** Corpus Corporum ([mlat.uzh.ch](https://www.mlat.uzh.ch/)). Abbreviation-preserving line transcriptions: HTR-United/CATMuS (used in v18).
- **Hebrew medical and legal texts:** [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org) (already used in v23).
- **Cipher keys and ciphertexts:** DECODE. Fontana's captions exist only as images (BSB digital).

## Out-of-the-box ideas
None is a cipher-family search, label study or cognate-script analogy, or in STRATEGIES.md.

1. **Line-final glyphs as signs of a crowded line.** Fifteenth-century scribes abbreviated more as the margin approached. Measure from IIIF images how much empty space each line had left before its last word (automatic boxing as in v18). Then test whether line-final `m`/`g`/`am` enrichment rises with crowding.
   - *Data:* Yale IIIF; CATMuS abbreviated Latin as a positive control.
   - *Kill:* the enrichment is flat with crowding, while the Latin control shows the slope.
   - *Why it is new:* it treats the line-edge rule as physical layout. Forum posts guess at abbreviation (voynich.ninja 5309), but nobody has measured line fill.
2. **Charm texture.** Build corpora of 15th-century voces magicae: *Ars notoria* prayers, the charms in German *Arzneibücher* (ReF), and Hildegard's coined words. Score them on the v21 feature battery against language, gibberish and generators.
   - *Data:* ReF, archive.org editions.
   - *Kill:* the Voynich falls no nearer the charms than to the language or gibberish clusters on held-out features.
   - *Why it is new:* it compares texture with a real genre of ritual nonsense. A forum claim (thread 5161) exists but has no controls.
3. **A/B dialect ladder.** Run a random search (tens of thousands of rewrite-rule sets) for the cheapest glyph transducer that maps A onto B. Calibrate its cost on real pairs: Bavarian ↔ Alemannic, 1400–50 (ReF); Czech before and after Hus's reform; Latin ↔ Italian.
   - *Kill:* A split against A split costs as much as A against B, or A → B costs more than Latin → Italian.
   - *Why it is new:* it measures what kind of distance separates A and B. It does not decode either.
4. **Calendar beat in the starred paragraphs.** Scan the sequence of paragraph-head features in quire 20 for the periods of astrological medicine: 7, 12, 27/28 (lunar mansions), 30, 36 (decans).
   - *Data:* ZL3b and IT2a. For the positive control, a real lunary or bloodletting calendar is planted through a verbose code.
   - *Kill:* the planted calendar is not recovered, or the Voynich stays below the max-statistic null under paragraph shuffles.
5. **Feature harmony.** Generate thousands of random stroke-feature definitions (v25 decomposition) and test whether a feature spreads to neighbouring glyphs and across word junctions, as Czech palatal assimilation and Hungarian/Turkish vowel harmony do.
   - *Kill:* the observed spread is no higher than under within-word shuffles, or the harmony languages are not detected.
   - *Why it is new:* asks if the junction effect is phonological agreement.
6. **Nestedness ecology.** Treat stems and endings as species and sites, and compute nestedness (NODF, with curveball nulls) for the Voynich, for inflecting, agglutinative and root-pattern languages (Latin, Czech, Hungarian, Hebrew), and for v21/v26 generator output.
   - *Kill:* the generators match the Voynich as well as the languages do.
   - *Why it is new:* an ecology metric no Voynich work has used.
