# Three undeciphered scripts: what the data-only attacks found (2–3 Oct 2026)

Every claim below has a control. The details are in each script's FINDINGS.md. LITERATURE.md has the related papers.

## Proto-Elamite (CDLI, 1,585 tablets, 95% from Susa)
**Holds:**
- Strong slot grammar: an optional prefix, then a middle, then a final class sign, then the number. M288 ends 237 of 250 entries.
- The final class sign predicts the number system: measured with capacity units (M297, M002…) or counted (M263, M264…).
- Openers: M157 heads 37% of tablet headers.
- Middles are as varied as free combination, which fits names or spelled words.
- Counting units 1/10/100/300 (grade B).
- Decimal or sexagesimal is chosen per tablet (p = 0.0005).

**Failed:**
- Linear Elamite values do not make the middles read as Elamite names (p = 0.65 against value shuffles).
- The arithmetic joint fit missed its pre-set 70% bar.
- No fixed ration per head.
- No seasonal time order (pe11): a blind search for a yearly herd/harvest cycle across undated tablets finds only a loop of commodity types that a timeless mixture reproduces; calibrated on Ur III Drehem tablets with known months, the method needs a season about 40x stronger than real herd accounts carry.
- Names are not built from a used-up set of counters or stamps (pe9): a sign used once on a tablet becomes 2-3x more likely to recur there (held out on 52 tablets), never less; 0 of 90 signs behave as depleted tokens. What remains is a per-tablet name pool that is reused freely (grade B).
- Not written from a fading memory and not copied (pe13): a flat per-tablet topic fits, with a dip at the next entry (M288 and M346 alternate) that no control corpus shows; the reverse recalls the obverse rather than resetting; a weak shared order of entries holds out of sample (grade B).
- Administration as a food web (pe15): name x goods webs are nested and modular only as far as their degrees force, unlike Ur III and Linear B. Three sign 'offices' (grain, class-sign, bare-count) do replicate on reserved tablets (+0.13 bits/entry; none of 3,000 random partitions does better). The signs of a new name predict its goods class (grade B); hidden links are predicted by popularity alone.
- Hunger as the ruler (pe16): sizing units from human and animal food needs recovers the Ur III sila blind (0.83 l, truth ~1 l; daily and monthly issues sorted on 97-100% of tablets), but random rulers fit as well and month/year alias 1 time in 3. On the PE two-line records N39C = 4.6 l (90% 1.5-14) on a lattice x{1/30, 1, 12}; the scale follows which units scribes used. Only the /30 point makes a unit bowl-sized (N30C ~ 0.6 l, N24 ~ one adult day), a check with a 43% chance hit rate. Grade C.
- The scribe knew the length? (pe21): clay area grows only with about the square root of the text on PE (elasticity 0.40; 0.65 on lists of 8+ lines), as in proto-cuneiform (0.44-0.49) and below Ur III (receipts 0.91, compiled accounts 0.72). About 42% of PE lists of 5+ lines sit on a sized-to-fit line (re-dealt texts 2-7%), and they are no particular genre, system, office, seal or site. Token piles written unexchanged are excluded (< 1%), and there is no hand-counting cap at 5. Totals go to the reverse while the obverse still has room. Grade B for these points; nothing is read.
- Counting the unseen signs (pe22): about 750 [680-850] base signs including compounds, of which 551 are seen. The simple signs are a closed set of about 425 (353 seen). Most entry strings are unseen (coverage 0.22). Hashed predictions made from pre-2000 tablets matched the later tablets: 6 new signs against 8 [3-14] predicted, 37 new strings against 39 [33-43], and a known-sign AUC of 0.92. These tablets behave like any batch of the old archive. Sofalin and Ozbaki add no genuine new sign. A further hashed prediction covers 89 untransliterated Tehran tablets. Grade B; nothing is read.
- Forensic accounting (pe23, `proto-elamite/loops/pe23_final.txt`): Digit fingerprints were computed (round-number heaping, mod 5, leading digit, repeats) against a smooth null that keeps the notation. They separate Ur III counted, measured, rule-allocated and computed-estimate numbers (0.91-0.97, shuffled 0.29-0.38), but Ur III estimates are less round than measurements, not more. PE counts look counted and PE capacity looks measured (B). No PE sign, office or site carries estimated or target numbers (B, for the ~8 signs large enough to test). The top-edge '1(N34)' is a constant tag of M157-headed tablets (21% vs 7%, p 4e-10) and never equals the entry sum (B). Nothing is read.
- Red ink? (pe24, `proto-elamite/loops/pe24_final.txt`): Do variant markers or signs flag entries that count against the total (-1), not at all (0) or at a ratio? An exhaustive and annealed search over every such assignment on 49 clean totalled tablets found nothing: not one extra total closes (held-out gain 0.00; random totals gain 2-6). The same search recovers the credit side of 21 Ur III balanced accounts and planted negative markers. So ~a is not a debit marker (A, negative); failing totals are off by about one entry (C). Nothing is read.
- Two hands on one tablet? (pe25, `proto-elamite/loops/pe25_final.txt`): Writer fingerprints from free variant choices, scored within a tablet against same-stratum tablets and calibrated on planted checker corpora. Reverses and totals are in the obverse's own hand (B; share of reverses in another hand -0.03, CI to 0.18; planted checkers 0.94-1.02). No single official writes the reverses of an office group (B). Edge tags and headers cannot be tested. Nothing is read.
- The clay's colour (pe26, `proto-elamite/loops/pe26_final.txt`): colour photographs of CDLI tablets cannot separate find site from photo batch, because the photos were taken drawer by drawer and carry no colour reference. Clay appearance beyond batch is not established (C). ST-11, the Yahya-clay tablet found at Susa, has the most plateau-like clay of 446, but YT-01 misses (C). Within a batch, tablets of similar colour share sign vocabulary (r 0.05, beyond size and drawer; B weak, cause unknown). Nothing is read.
- Numbers in two languages (pe27, `proto-elamite/loops/pe27_final.txt`): exact count-to-capacity ratio scans over tablets, sign pairs and line windows, checked against Ur III (the 60-sila worker ration is recovered) and planted rates. There is no corpus-wide ration rate (A, negative). Two local rates survive: M288 holds half an N01 of capacity (sometimes one N01) per counted M388/M054-type unit, held out on lines never used before (13/64 vs 3.3, p 1e-5; B). M106 is one per head of the herd in the MDP 17,085/097 column lists, as a count or as 1 N30C (B). Together they pin the capacity ratios N30C:N24:N39B:N01:N14 = 1:3:6:30:180, matching the shape-based set (B-). Nothing is read.
- Propagate the rates (pe28, `proto-elamite/loops/pe28_final.txt`): the pe27 rates used as anchors in a crossword / Bayesian solve of every count-to-entry equation, checked on held-out tablets, Ur III (one anchor) and planted networks. No hidden rate network: known rates unlock no further rates (A, negative). The M288 allotment of 2(N39B) 1(N24) per unit is tied to the M288 entry and follows person-like lines of 14 kinds, held out (B-). |M036+1(N30D)| = 5 per M263 unit (C). Nothing is read.
- What the totals leave out (pe30, `proto-elamite/loops/pe30_final.txt`): 100,521 global exclusion rules (by position, sign, unit, size, duplicate, relation to the total; one-category sums; carried constants) on 91 totalled tablets (49 clean + 42 damaged). The same search recovers the Ur III credit-section exclusion and every planted rule, but on PE the best rule gains 1-2 tablets and loses on held-out tablets (-0.55 / -1.34), no better than four nulls. No category is left out by rule (A, negative); which entry a failing total misses is random (B). Nothing is read.
- Interchangeable name signs (pe31, `proto-elamite/loops/pe31_final.txt`): no sign pair is interchangeable in PE names after search correction, but calibration shows the test cannot single out homophones even in Ur III, Old Babylonian or Linear B names, or planted ones (A, method limit). ~variants share their base sign's slot and neighbours (B); whether that means graphic variants or sub-kinds is open (C). The closed-syllabary question cannot be answered this way: the test fails to find the syllables in Old Babylonian names.
- Who gets the standard ration (pe32, `proto-elamite/loops/pe32_final.txt`): the 14 signs before the standard M288 allotment are one class (B; z 3.1-3.6 against frequency, M288-predecessor and same-tablet nulls), and their features re-find hidden members on unseen tablets (p 0.003). But no new member is found (3/33, base rate), and ration sizes do not split the class into sub-classes (p 0.97; the Ur III check recovers gurusz/geme2/dumu but has low power at PE size). The standard allotment is one share per unit, sometimes doubled, never paired with a smaller share.
- Rebuild the writing sessions (pe29, `proto-elamite/loops/pe29_final.txt`): tablets were linked into sittings from weak evidence (rare-sign pool, variant habits, clay colour, museum-number neighbourhood, size), holding each evidence out in turn. Museum neighbours share vocabulary, variant habits and size (B, weak). Nothing finer than the excavation lot is shown (C). The rebuilt sessions hold no cross-tablet sums or shared rates beyond a drawer null (B, negative; planted sums are found). The Ur III control finds true days only 1 link in 25, so sittings cannot be rebuilt from this evidence (A, method limit). Nothing is read.
- The seal picture (pe33, `proto-elamite/loops/pe33_final.txt`): CDLI seal ids PES0001-0339 turn out to be Legrain's MDP 16 figure numbers (A, data), giving published seal pictures for 63 tablets (39 seals). No picture (cattle, caprids, lions, animals acting like people, boats, storehouses) predicts any sign, number system, header or office beyond volume and seal-block nulls, and random picture-to-sign guesses fail on held-out seals. The calibration shows this sample is about 10x too small: the same test finds Ur III seal-title links 8/8 at ~480 tablets but 2/25 at PE size (A, method limit). Grade C: caprid-sealed tablets count larger herds than cattle-sealed ones (median 50 vs 17, p 0.31). Nothing is read. pe35 enlarged this to 97 tablets via Louvre records (CDLI PES08xx-13xx = Amiet MDP 43 numbers, A): still no link (strong links now excluded, B); the caprid-herd and M139 leads failed on the new tablets and are dropped; power stays 15-20%, and even all ~180 known sealed tablets would give < 70% (A, method limit).
- Compound signs are sums? (pe34, `proto-elamite/loops/pe34_final.txt`): a modifier's behaviour shift learned on some bases does not predict its compounds on unseen bases (p 0.08-0.67 over transfer and three factorisations), although planted modifiers are found at 20% strength and Linear B sex markers pass (p 0.001-0.05). Proto-cuneiform insets fail too, so PE modifiers are not behavioural operators (B, negative); bases carry behaviour. One lead: M342 makes a sign unnumbered / header-like on 5 of 7 bases (C). Nothing is read.
- The bones as a ruler (pe37, `proto-elamite/loops/pe37_final.txt`): Malyan Banesh animal bones (Zeder, Open Context) give a goat-led herd with heavy culling of 1-3-year-olds; a herd-demography model gives a living herd of 29% young and 64% adult females (A data, B model). Matching sign assignments to this profile cannot name the herd signs: planted truth is recovered at chance, random invented herds fit as well (p 0.85), and held-out tablets fit worse than random (A/B, negative). With contemporary bones, the Ur III control finds the goat terms but not the sheep terms, so bones cannot kill a reading either. Nothing is read.
- Type inference for sign roles (pe38, `proto-elamite/loops/pe38_final.txt`): every sign given one of nine roles under accounting type-checking, searched with many Gibbs chains. The method fails its controls (proto-cuneiform forced roles 0-4% correct, Ur III none; 1,200 random rule systems fit permuted labels better than true ones), and no PE role is forced beyond shuffled corpora or replicates on held-out tablets (0.243 vs 0.246) (A method limit, B negative). Data fact (A): PE totals carry no dedicated total sign (142/330 total lines have no sign; no sign is > 20% on total lines). Nothing is read.
- Translation with numbers as the only bridge (pe39, `proto-elamite/loops/pe39_final.txt`): 44 unsupervised translation systems were trained between PE and proto-cuneiform (denoising + back-translation, numerals the only shared tokens). They recover only 1-10% of a known renamed proto-cuneiform half, worse than simply matching numeral profiles (10-16%). Held-out back-translation gain does not separate right from wrong pairs, and Ur III <-> proto-cuneiform fails (A, method limit). Seed-stable PE translations are about 2x the count under fixed numeral shuffles (B, weak signal). Leads are C / C- only: M354 -> GAR (shared N51 notation, a restatement), M136 / M111 -> SZE, M147 -> U4. Nothing is read.
- Machines reinvent writing (pe41, `proto-elamite/loops/pe41_final.txt`): 2,238 simulated societies with random economies invented their own record systems; aligned to PE by structure, their votes are reproducible across PE halves (0.50-0.54 vs shuffled 0.20-0.29) and read unseen machine scripts (0.43-0.53 vs 0.15-0.18), but they fail proto-cuneiform: known grain/animal/worker signs are not voted commodity (p 0.70-0.99) (A, negative), so PE label votes (names M218, M004, M370...; header type M157; total M297~b) are C. B: PE's shape needs a big inventory with frequent new variants; archaic scribes wrote more redundantly than reconstruction games reward. Nothing is read.
- The names as a breeding population (pe45, `proto-elamite/loops/pe45_final.txt`): 85,000 simulated naming grammars with genealogies; PE names cluster by tablet (B) but no inheritance or kin groups are visible, and the method cannot see real Ur III families either (calibrated blind spot). Grade C: name-frame signs M056, M010, M001 (final), M157, M210 (initial), the PE counterpart of Sumerian ur-/lugal-/-la.
- The form tree is not a dictionary (pe43, `proto-elamite/loops/pe43_final.txt`): variants and compounds treated as a phylogeny of forms. Variants are used like their root (A) and compound parts are kin (B; proto-cuneiform lexical lists 1.7x chance). But tree families in proto-cuneiform match the lexical lists only weakly (p 0.02), the tree gives no time order (proto-cuneiform sheds variants; tree dates = tablet size) (A, negative), and coining follows a sign's own variant richness, not its meaning family (B, PC). PE twig families (capacity, header, name-string groups) are C. Nothing is read.
- Printouts of one hidden spreadsheet (pe47, `proto-elamite/loops/pe47_final.txt`): random table shapes, tablet-to-tablet sums and a multiplicative table find a planted sliced master table, and Drehem's row indices (officials, transaction type), but exact-cell and sum reconstruction fail on Ur III (A, method limit). PE has no duplicated or extracted tablet pairs (0 vs 2-4 in Ur III at the same size, B-) and no header sign that indexes a row (B). C: tablet pairs sharing a rare number share signs (p 0.02). Nothing is read.
- Signs as goods in a trade network (pe48, `proto-elamite/loops/pe48_final.txt`): gravity/diffusion models across 8 find sites recover site vocabularies in Ur III and Linear B (A). Words homed at an outpost turn out to be commodities and office terms, not names (A, an inversion of the premise), and travel alone cannot label names. PE plateau tablets use a different sign mix from Susa (A, not a date artefact). C: a small Yahya lexicon (M056, M044, M219 with capacity numerals; M136 as a header, echoed by \|M136+M365\| at Malyan), with frozen forecasts for new Yahya tablets. Nothing is read.
- Clerks by their tics (pe49, `proto-elamite/loops/pe49_final.txt`): hands clustered from unchosen habits recover Ur III dub-sar scribes weakly (NMI ~0.1) and their specialties (profile r 0.34), but planted clerks without any specialty yield equally many replicable 'job descriptions', because habits follow content (B, method limit). With content removed, PE shows no habit groups beyond a shuffled null (B-). No clerk or office identified; C: N30C, N30D, M009, M001, M346 used evenly by all groups.
- What would the clerk miss? (pe51, `proto-elamite/loops/pe51_final.txt`): 64 small random models fill in masked tablets and every frequent sign (and pair) is erased to see which predictions break. Calibrated on planted roles, Ur III and proto-cuneiform: the game finds number carriers and keeps names silent, but cannot see total or document-type markers (A, method limit). 137 of 141 PE signs are silent and shuffled corpora give as many classed signs (B). Number carriers M297 and M376 (B); the capacity triangle M387-M036-M297 shares number-system information inside lines (B-). Nothing is read.
- The lambs tell the month (pe53, `proto-elamite/loops/pe53_final.txt`): all 6,050 assignments of the herd signs to young / adult female / other were scored against a biological birth-pulse curve. The pulse is real in the Ur III Drehem books (young share peaks about January, p < 0.001), but only in pooled totals (A). Blind, the method finds neither the Drehem lamb terms nor their months (A, control fails). On PE the young-share "season" is just whether a record carries an M362 count, and it dies under written-only, bootstrap and sign-pattern-matched controls (B, negative). Nothing is read.
- Follow one flock through the archive (pe54, `proto-elamite/loops/pe54_final.txt`): herd entries were linked into flock histories by composition under random demographic hypotheses. The method cannot recover time order or class roles even on planted flocks, and fails on Ur III herdsmen with the true categories (A). PE entries are less linkable than shuffles and predict nothing held out (B, negative). Three herd names recurring on two tablets have similar counts (C+, convention-dependent, gap 0). Nothing is read.
- Agronomy and physiology as the ruler (pe55, `proto-elamite/loops/pe55_final.txt`): random unit sizes and sign-to-activity labels scored by how many count/capacity pairs land in physical ranges (rations, fodder, sowing, yield). This recovers the Ur III sila blind (0.73 l), but at PE size it cannot tell people from animals even in the control (43% right), nonsense windows fit as well, and sowing cannot be calibrated (A, negative). Only the scale is kept: N39C about 0.6-0.8 l or 5-7 l (C, agrees with pe16). No activity sign read.

- Hire forgers and watch where they fail (pe56, `proto-elamite/loops/pe56_final.txt`): ~1.5 M tablets forged by 120 random surface-only architectures per corpus; residues found on half the tablets tested on the other half, and dissected by oracle forgers. Ur III totals and dates and planted long-range constraints come back (3-19x); PE's only residue (M157 headers under-forged) is a forger artefact that also appears in forger worlds. No ordered entry constraint survives own-tablet permutation in PE (0/48; proto-cuneiform 4). B: serial dossier P008718-P008731; C: M297 closing totals. Not cracked.
- The crack attempt (pe59, `proto-elamite/loops/pe59_final.txt`): every B-grade result was frozen into one machine-checkable reading and run as a whole-tablet generator and decoder on 782 held-out tablets. The accounting skeleton (unit ladder, sums, one system per tablet, M288 = 60 N39C per head) beats all simpler models and its shuffled-role and random-unit twins on numerals and totals (B), and decodes half the tablets under lax checks; but roles add ~0.005 bits/entry and nothing per tablet. With its true readings, proto-cuneiform gains no more (A, method limit): meaning cannot be validated this way. Predictions frozen. Not cracked.
- Unseen tablets (pe60, `proto-elamite/loops/pe60_final.txt`): live CDLI holds only 5 newly transliterated PE tablets (~23 entries) beyond our corpus; 88 of the 89 Tehran tablets (P10) are photo-only. A blind-validated photo reading recovers totals (97%), a capacity flag and the M157 header, but not line numerals (12%) or signs (3% recall) (A, method limit). Every frozen prediction is below its frozen sample size: none killed, none supported. Four tablets (Hameeuw 2025) and a Hermitage tablet are unseen texts in print that need a human. Not cracked.
**Open:** the phonetic layer. The varied middles say "maybe", and the name test says "not with these values".

## Linear A (lineara.xyz, 1,721 inscriptions; DAMOS Linear B 94%)
**Holds:**
- KU-RO = total: 9 of 30 sections add up exactly, against 0.5 by chance.
- KI-RO = a residual amount or a list heading.
- The fraction signs have a strict writing order (0 of 500 random relabellings match it).
- Which fraction sign appears depends on the commodity.
- Linear B shared words: 12 of 3+ signs against 2.0 by chance.
- Commodity pecking order (la20): logograms are written in a fixed order (CYP, GRA > VIR, OLE > OLIV, VIN). This holds on unseen tablets (0.715 against 0.50; FWER 0.005) and beats a large-amounts-first null (P 0.002). The order is by commodity, not by size (A; grain-first sequences known in kind).
- Affixes are real (z ≈ 6). -TE, -ME and JA- belong to objects, not tablets.

**Failed:**
- The fraction values cannot be solved from the totals.
- The "J < 1/2" argument rests on two doubtful J+J readings (photo check).
- The anchors are too few to separate places from persons.
- The affixes do not predict new forms.
- Tablets as pieces of split ledgers (la12): no cross-tablet sum beats chance; with 2-3 tablets almost any total can be matched by chance, so arithmetic alone cannot rejoin ledgers.
- Linear A as its own dialects (la13): no cross-site or cross-scribe sign substitution beats nulls, and no sign grid emerges; sites share too few words (43 types) for even a planted rule to show. Words differing by one sign are site- and hand-local (B).
- Counting the words like wildlife (la15): at least ~4,500 word types were in use (925 seen; ABC 5,200-82,000) and ~170-200 syllabic signs (139 seen). Words are site-endemic and anti-nested like Linear B's. A hashed prediction of the post-1985 documents got new words (33 vs 32), new signs (1 vs 0) and returning words (AUC 0.76) right; new findspots were underpredicted (1 vs 6). Census, not a reading.
- Private spellings as a Rosetta stone (la16): one-sign variants are not hand-private even in Linear B, so no sign equation can be read off scribal differences; no LA equation survives held-out re-test. LA hands are recoverable from shared words (B), not spellings.
- Words that avoid each other (la18): in Linear B, forms of one word share a tablet (3x) but avoid the same entry, and tablets keep to one case. Linear A shows neither (calibrated, B); perfect avoidance cannot be seen at LA size. SI- prefix alternation stays C.
- The palace spoke many tongues (la19): mixtures of sign-level phonotactic models choose K = 4 for Linear A, but 33/36 single real languages also choose K >= 2, and LA is less mixed and less stable than the median language. Its components track nothing outside the model, and foreign-looking outliers (Phaistos, roundels) are rare-sign effects. No multilingual lexicon detected (B, calibrated).
- Words spread like epidemics (la17): SI/SIR outbreaks fitted by ABC (~390,000 simulations) to the site x word and site x sign incidence cannot recover a planted source (1/20, 0/20) or an alternative-generator history (chance), and the Linear B control (Knossos first) passes only where shuffled data pass too. No Linear A source or adoption order is identifiable; the inferred orders do not match the MM II-LM IB deposit dates (P >= 0.14). Grade C: time-separated sites share fewer words.

- The pecking order (la20): no word, entry-word or first-sign hierarchy recurs across tablets (0.51 vs Linear B Pylos at Linear A size 0.61). People and places are not listed in a fixed rank or tour order (B, calibrated); a tour that was only ever written in pieces would not be detectable.
- Universals build the grid (la21): a blind grid from phonotactic universals recovers Linear B's consonant rows (not its vowel columns). Linear A's blind rows agree with the LB-derived consonant series (B), but no specific new grouping replicates across sites; QA acts like a pure vowel (C).
- The rings know who talked (la26): the published seal-ring network (7 shared seals) does not predict shared words, sign variants or entry structures, and multi-site words do not follow ring paths (P 0.16-0.66 against all graphs; the doc-perm null passes 38% of random graphs on Linear B). Only a ring process ~4x the real cross-site sharing would be visible (upper bound). Khania is an island in signs and entry structure too (B).
- Predict what the fire destroyed (la22): random-ensemble restorers, calibrated on hidden known items at the real break pattern, restore signs at 28 % top-1 / 52 % top-5 (Linear B 45 %) from word-internal regularities only (shuffled tablets give the same), and numbers and commodity signs from the accounting skeleton (gain gone in shuffled tablets) (B). Frozen predictions (hashed) for 974 break edges do not anticipate SigLA's later re-readings (1/19 in top 5, B negative); edge hits 1/4 clean (C).
- Count the people (la23): capture-recapture calibrated on Linear B (whole-archive name counts predicted, 5/6) puts the Hagia Triada tablets at ~1,000 [510-2,430] distinct small-number entry words; perhaps a few hundred people, the size of a town (C). Persons cannot be separated blindly from other entry words at LA size (AUC 0.59). Prediction: ~55 % of such words on a new HT tablet will be new.
- The clerk ran an apportionment algorithm (la24): about 90 M share-rule fits find no tablet built by dividing a total by share classes. The MDL search fails its planted and Ur III ration controls, so it is blind at Linear A list lengths. Lists are not built on one unit share, although Linear B lists are (calibrated, B). Fraction-absorption fits prefer simple values, not the conventional ones as such. Grade C: HT 9b scales three of HT 9a's recipients by 4/5, which needs J = 1/2.
- The fire set the clock (la25): ABC over ~1.5x10^7 random crop calendars x destruction months cannot date any LM IB archive from its commodity mix: planted months are not recovered even at 64x corpus size (base rate and season are confounded), and the Linear B spring control fails. Whether the sites burned in one season is untestable (A, calibrated negative).
- The balance pans know the numbers (la27): 70 published Minoan weight masses show no quantum (no 61 g unit, no mina step), and Linear A integers do not heap on the 1:8:60 unit-mina-talent ladder or on any of 10^4 random ladders beyond decimal counting. Planted 5 % heaping is found (B, calibrated negative). The Linear B ceiling rule (a sub-unit is written at most r-1 times) recovers M:N = 4 and LANA:M = 3 from numbers alone. On Linear A it finds D written 2-4 times and K, L2, JE, F, H never repeated (B). So D and B are counted parts (>= 3 per next unit), consistent with D = 1/5 and B = 1/3, not with binary values (C).
- The receipts fed the ledgers (la28): the terms on roundels and nodules reappear on tablets of the same site no more than vessel words do (z 1.7 vs 2.4). A Linear B sealing control passes (z 4.2), and planted 10 % copying is found (B, calibrated negative). Khania roundel classes never appear on Khania tablets. 19 % of receipt types use signs no tablet uses, and 9 of 14 receipt amounts are fraction-only (A). Receipts and ledgers are separate layers (B). Sums cannot be tested (4 integer receipt amounts).
- The totals are in a hidden currency (la30): about 8x10^7 random exchange-rate vectors with free fraction values, fitted on half the tablets, balance no held-out KU-RO totals beyond shuffled labels or random totals. A planted currency at Linear A size is found 11/12 (B, calibrated negative). The same totals-only search recovers Ur III silver prices (held-out 7.4/29 vs 1.6-2.5; bulk prices within ~20 %) (A, method). D and B never occur in a KU-RO section, so totals say nothing about their values.
- The slips tell how they wrote (la32): one-sign variants scored against glyph-shape similarity and blind sound rows. Linear B variants follow sound, never shape (A). Linear A shows no slip mode: copying-type (shape) slips are below the ~20-30 % and dictation-type below the ~10 % that planted controls detect (B, calibrated negative); its variants are weakly sound-structured (blind rows z 3.95, robust to a circularity control; B). The shape score does catch modern misreadings of LA signs (z 2.6).
- Human ears are the answer key? (la59): one-sign alternations were fitted to human confusion data (Miller-Nicely consonants, five-vowel formants, Index Diachronica sound changes) with ~500,000 random assignments and annealing. No key beats its own shuffles in Linear A or Linear B, held-out gains are negative, and planted syllabaries are not recovered at 1-30x Linear A's data. No phonetic feature map results (B, calibrated negative).
- The land wrote the ledger (la29): 53 per-site land variables (DEM, rainfall, land cover, soil) predict no held-out site's commodity mix and no administrative sign's site frequency better than random smooth fields, site size or shuffled labels; planted x2.7-per-SD effects and a modern land-cover 'ledger' are found (B, calibrated negative). The only land-linked signs are the libation-formula signs at mountain sanctuaries (genre). Gaussian field nulls are too lenient for such data (A, method).
- The winds carried the words (la31): 1.48 M simulated voyages over ERA5 daily winds, plus Tobler walking times, give travel matrices for 29 find-sites. Symmetrised, sailing time ranks site pairs exactly like km (rho >= 0.96), so only direction can separate wind from distance. Inside Crete, nearer sites share more rare signs and logograms (P 0.023, B), but no travel model, season, month or leg beats km. Words do not sit downwind (P 0.91). The directional test has AUC 0.68 at Linear A size (planted), so this is an upper bound. Linear B behaves the same.
- Queues at the storeroom (la33): queue statistics from ~12,000 simulated archives rank Ur III ration groups above delivery groups (AUC 0.87 at 150 texts, 0.66 at 30) and Linear B personnel/census series above disbursement series (0.80), but a tablet-level model fails on real Ur III tablets (0.42-0.55), so no Linear A tablet or word can be labelled ration recipient, partner or office (A, calibrated negative). Ur III also breaks the premises: ration lists are the more overdispersed, and both regimes name persons. Grain lists are uniform in amount (P 0.012, B); LA groups sit at the demand end of an uncalibrated scale (C).
- The number bends the word (la35): no Linear A ending changes with the number that follows it (1 / 2 / >= 3, round or not) beyond the -RO of the total words. A planted rule on 15% of entries is found 3/3, and Linear B's real number endings are found at Linear A size against a site null (5/6). The suggested 'longer form before 1' pattern is word length (killed). Calibrated negative (B); an LB-sized plural suffix cannot be excluded with 37 stem pairs (C).
- Find the doublets (la37): two blind detectors (context interchangeability; held-out gain from merging two signs) find Linear B doublets and same-consonant pairs (A, method) and planted LA doublets down to a 15-25 % use rate, but no Linear A pair of common signs replicates (0 vs 2-8 in every LB sample of LA size; B, calibrated negative). LA near-interchangeability follows LB-derived consonants as LB does (B, outside check). SI~TI, TI~TE, RE~ME as word-final alternations (C); A~DA, A~PA demoted.
- The hand in the strokes (la34): blind shape features from SigLA's per-occurrence drawings recover published Linear A hands within site (nearest-neighbour 0.37 vs 0.19, unseen hands AUC 0.58 vs 0.49; sides of one tablet pass; B). Form similarity tracks shared vocabulary without labels (B), agreeing with la16. No commodity specialisation; one-sign variants are not one writer's alternation (C); hands moving between sites cannot be tested because site differences dominate.
- Are the borrowed values better than chance? (la38): with Linear B-derived values, Linear A obeys language-independent phonotactics (consonant OCP, onset principle, phoneme compressibility) better than 99.996 % of 10^5 relabelings (consonants) and 99.96 % (vowels), as strongly as Linear B's own true values at the same size; wrong values sit in the middle (B). Rows supported: pure vowels, M, T (held out), R, K (B); D, S (C). Vowel fit weak at Hagia Triada (C).
- Type inference as a compiler does it (la40): ~1e9 Gibbs updates over role assignments (person, place, qualifier, heading, total, number-word) for all word types at once, with tablets required to type-check. The checker fails its Linear B control (known classes not recovered at LA size or full size; learned rule systems score at chance on Pylos), so no LA word class is established (A, calibrated negative). LA role partitions replicate across disjoint halves at ~0.4x Pylos strength (ARI 0.12 vs 0.30; B). KU-RE, U, A, DA, KA-NA as headings (C).
- Is the layout the grammar? (la47): tablets were rebuilt as 2-D pages (GORILA lines, SigLA boxes, DAMOS for Linear B), and about 100,000 random rules over layout alone were scored on held-out tablets. Layout carries about 0.045 bits per word, the same as word order, and almost nothing beyond it; a planted line-start rule was found and Linear B behaved as expected. Linear A lines are run-on: 14 % of line breaks split a word, and line breaks follow entries only weakly (strongest at Haghia Triada, absent at Khania). Totals sit at the bottom on their own line, header signs start lines, and no class is written larger. The structure is not hidden in space (B).
- Hire a forger and watch where he fails (la46): relations on held-out tablets were compared with ~3 M tablets forged by 200-300 random architectures. Calibrated on forger worlds, shuffles, planted constraints and Linear B (which yields ko-wa -> ko-wo). Linear A beats the forgers only on the KU-RO total (A, known) and on one ordered basket after SA-RA₂ (GRA/CYP, NI, VIN; B; fits la20). Casts, word-number pairings, site choices and sign pairs are all forgeable. Earlier 'casts' were a side-duplication artefact.
- Resurrect the ancestor by simulating history (la44): ABC over 190,000 simulated proto-language histories (sound change, borrowing, random spelling) recovers Greek-like inflection from Linear B at 583 types (4-5/5) but not at 107 (Cypriot fails). For Linear A every unconditional posterior ('isolating') is reproduced by shuffled LA, because word length drives it (B, calibrated negative). Given affixation, LA leans Greek-type suffixing (0.57; shuffle P 0.10; C). Real scripts (LA and LB) avoid same-series and doubled signs beyond any simulated history (B).
- Dissect a machine that learned Linear A (la49): 115 tiny transformers and GRUs were trained to fill masked words and numbers, then dissected. At this corpus size no model of Linear A, Linear B or Ur III learns totals, number-word heads or word functions; the controls miss to-so, szunigin and planted totals (A, method negative). Word geometry recurs across seeds and model populations and recovers Linear B and Ur III classes (B). One Linear A circuit survives ablation in every model: the single signs of sealings (*301, KU, KA, SI, RO, TE, ZE and others; B, agrees with la45). Commodity and libation-formula groups are stable but not causal (C).
- The farming year is the answer key? (la50): the fixed order of goods was scored against an Aegean harvest calendar (FAO, Hesiod, Varro), 10^5 random calendars, a northern-European calendar and shuffles. It fits no better than random (P 0.55; the SA-RA2 basket P 0.35), and goods do not cluster by season. Planted calendars are found, but Linear B fails the same test because its order is administrative (B, calibrated negative). The order is a staple ranking shared with Linear B, not a harvest sequence: grain, then the olive good, then figs, then wine (C).
- Clay is attention, attention is status? (la53): words were ranked by layout surplus (own line, extra words, first place, length beyond what quantity and commodity predict) over thousands of random specs. Planted hierarchies are found, but Linear B gods and the wanax (AUC 0.56) and Ur III gods and kings (0.46) are not, and recipes do not carry between the two administrations, so scribes did not mark rank with clay (A, calibrated negative). Linear A's high-surplus words are heading and transaction words, not status referents (B).
- The scribes' mistakes show how they thought? (la56): about 2,000 random inclusion rules per run (words, signs, commodities or roles left out, subtracted or doubled; fraction swaps; sub-totals; re-cut sections), with fraction values as a nuisance, beat no null. Planted exclusions are found 21/22 times and Ur III gives back a real excluded commodity, but the Linear A mismatches look like noise on balanced totals (B, negative). C: HT 127b KU-RO 292 is a running total that also counts an erased KI+MU 14 line.
- Words grow like cities? (la55): urban-scaling exponents were fitted for every word across Linear A deposits, Linear B find areas and Ur III site-years, against documents re-dealt within genre and length. In the Linear B and Ur III controls, function words do not saturate and names do not grow in proportion, and scaling predicts no held-out archive content or size, so the frame is killed (A, method). In Linear A, staples and hides saturate and the Khania/HT nodule signs are 'threshold' (locality); nothing predicts held-out (B). Bigger towns left more documents and logogram types, sublinearly (C).

- The crack attempt (la60): every B and best C result was assembled into one machine-checkable reading of the administrative documents (total, residue, commodity class, heading signs, heading/transaction words, OLE+X entries, commodity order, site defaults, libation register), turned into a generative document grammar, frozen on training documents (sha256) and used to decode the held-out half. The reading beats its own role shuffles (10/10 splits) but adds nothing to a bare logogram/sign/word/number grammar except the KU-RO arithmetic. Frozen, it explains 22-26 % of held-out documents in full and gains 2.7 % of documents over shuffled readings, the same as a true partial Linear B role reading at the same size (2.9 %). Post-1985 documents (n = 11) give no gain. Entry words return with the same commodity on other tablets (0.47 vs 0.16, C). Not cracked: the ledger is parsed (B), the language is unread (69 % of word tokens, every entry name).
- Every bureaucracy on earth votes (la57): seven administrations (proto-cuneiform, Ur III, Old Babylonian, Ebla, Old Assyrian, Linear B, Inca khipus) were reduced to value-free structure and ran ~142,000 random role taggers, leave-one-civilisation-out, with label-permuted nulls. Only COMMODITY transfers across civilisations, and it recovers Linear A logograms (B). There is no universal layout of a total: cuneiform-trained taggers score Linear B and khipu totals below chance (A, negative). Persons, places and transactions cannot be told apart in Linear A by outside models (B, negative). C: DI-DE-RU, DA-SI-*118, U-*325-ZA, TE-TU are word-commodities.
- Simulate Minoan government (la58): about 280,000 simulated Cretan administrations (palace, capital with offices, peer centres, temple, merchants, none), with paperwork thinned to Linear A survival and scored by ABC. No centre, dependency link or office word can be read; the controls (Ur III, Linear B) fail to locate their own centres (B, calibrated negative). Linear A paperwork splits by site. The Hagia Triada rooms share vocabulary at 2.5x the shuffled level, while every pair of sites shares less than random. The class 'no shared institution' gets 0.75, against <= 0.45 in shuffles (B). Read as separate site administrations (C).
- The crack attempt on outside data (la62): the only open Linear A outside our snapshot is the Knossos ivory ring and handle (KN Zg 57, 58), known from prose only; RILA-S1 (107 items) is closed. Of ~17 frozen predictions, 3 are testable (1 trivial survive, the Knossos commodity profile fails, 1 order pair agrees). The frozen reading explains no more tokens than an empty reading. Not cracked; the real outside test needs RILA-S1 and the Anetaki II edition (human items in `linear-a/loops/la62_final.txt`).
- Minoan numbers must obey physiology (la64): unit sizes fitted to ration, output and vessel windows (thousands of random hypotheses, held out). Entry size alone fails its Linear B and Ur III controls by x10-x50. The per-head route recovers Linear B grain (100-110 l vs 96) but not any Linear A unit: there are only 66 person/commodity pairs, and a planted archive of that size is not recovered either (A, calibrated negative). No ration word and no fraction value can be read. C-: a jar-sized wine unit (7-24 l), consistent with the ZA Zb 3 pithos at chance level.
- What would the Minoan clerk miss? (la63): 60 small models fill in held-out documents and each word, sign or word class is erased (pe51 port; plants, Linear B, Ur III controls). Number carriers are KU-RO (the total line), CYP and the fraction-takers (*308 C); SI and NI predict the commodity (C). No syllabic word class (43 sign families, the la57 candidates, the between-line signs) carries number or commodity information beyond random words, though Linear B's commodity-bound class passes the same test (B, calibrated negative); la57's syllabic commodity words demoted to C-.
- Dossiers as controlled experiments (la65, pe63 port): 2 M random templates per run find Linear B series at Linear A size (5-9 series-pure dossiers per draw) and 30/30 planted copies, but only one strict Linear A series (ZA 4/5/15, wine) and 26 FDR dossiers. Inside them names swap alone and numbers float (B); the commodity, fraction, step and transfer tests have no power even on Linear B at this size (A, limit). C: *28B-NU-MA-RE and SI-PI-KI are series-only words of the Zakros wine template.
**Open:** the fraction values. They need specialist re-reading of PH 9b, PH 22a, ZA 8 and HT 104 in GORILA.

## Voynich (ZL3b and IT2a transliterations, 39,019 words)
**Holds:**
- Letters are too predictable for simple or verbose substitution of Latin or Italian. On letter statistics it equals a no-language Markov text.
- The last glyph of a word predicts the next word's first glyph within a line, and this resets at every line break, even between ordinary words.
- Lines have opener and closer vocabularies. m/g works as about one end marker per line.
- What picks a twin glyph (v40): not the word (k/t and ch/sh carry no word-identity information beyond adjacent glyphs; a meaning control shows +0.11 bit) and not the line above or the ink load (planted rules found). ch/sh is positional (sh x3.4 in paragraph-first lines, x2.3 line-initial, x0.6 right half, same word-shape and page); k/t and ch/sh also persist for a few words as a hand habit. Grade A measurement, B reading (position-and-habit variants, not letters).
- The two word classes (q-type and a-type) are mostly a property of section and page.
- No hidden letter stream in word lengths, gaps, line counts or line totals (v15: ~10 M random codebooks, 7 languages; planted Caesar recovered at +0.3 to +1.3 bits/letter, Voynich +0.03). Grade A, negative.
- The spaces are not a mechanical lie: no re-spacing rule (80,000 tried) or free MDL re-segmentation beats the written spaces beyond a Markov-2 null, while planted spacings on Latin/Italian are beaten 2-3x harder. The written words save nothing over a glyph-bigram code (Latin words save 0.4-0.65 bits/glyph); the junction effect survives re-spacing (v16). Grade B.
- Nothing is sorted: no page, window, line-initial run, label set or margin list is in any glyph order (v19: ~1.2 M order optimisations; planted lists and Isidore's alphabetical Etymologiae X found under substitution). Grade A negative for runs of >= ~25 entries. Side result: q-initial words drift up and a-initial words down within pages and paragraphs (Grade B, cause open).
- Not written to a quota: no glyph, pair, word class or linear combination is held even per page, paragraph or bifolio (v20: ~2,150 features x 4 levels, planted quotas found; Voynich pages 2-5x more uneven than natural texts). Grade A negative for hard quotas. The one conserved per-line balance is reproduced by a word-bigram chain (Grade B).
- No calendar beat in the starred paragraphs (v32): quire 20 entries repeat nothing at 7, 12, 27-30, 36 or any period from 2 to 40 (folding, combs, circular HMM with a lost-bifolio gap, day-counter; p 0.18-0.96 in ZL and IT2a). A real martyrology is caught at z 45-64 when its date is written but not from its text alone, and planted day words in at least 20 % of entry openings are caught. Grade A negative for written dating; undated calendars remain untested. The star drawings alternate dotted and plain, and the text ignores this.
- The pen does not reset the text (v18): word choice, length, junction and repeats flow across ink-darkness dip proxies (25 Yale IIIF pages, 6,048 detector settings) as in Latin manuscripts; a planted generator restarting at each dip is found 3/3. Grade B negative. Leads: a darkening looks slightly like a line start (C); words on one pen-load are slightly more alike (C+).
- Not a spell (v31): against 123 corpora (voces magicae from 40+ grimoires, natural languages, conlangs, 37 gibberish writers, generators), held-out grimoires are recognised 10/10 and 99% by 252 surviving random classifiers (0 survive permuted labels), but the Voynich scores magic 0.000 and 0/252 survivors vote magic. 83-86% vote generator; its extreme slot rigidity and word families are shared only by Pinyin and copy-and-vary generators. Spell words pushed through copy-and-vary do not fit either (0-3% of splits). Grade A not-spell, B generator-like.
- Forge it, then catch it (v21): twelve forgers, from the junction resynthesis to a GRU and Timm-Schinner copy-and-vary, are all told from real pages by ~52,000 page discriminators (best AUC 0.93; forgery vs forgery 0.50; Latin and Italian herbals forged the same way 0.84-0.93). The herbals are caught by topic re-use, the Voynich never is: only local copy-and-vary and layout effects survive, each closable by one more rule until another appears. Generator-like in kind, beyond any single known generator. Grade A (no forger passes), B (no topic signal).
- Corrections are too few to read as a rulebook (v24): only 24 marked in all transliterations, 4 with a before-state, and ink anomalies do not find them. Controls show real Latin corrections fix legal, often real-word slips, and a copied meaningless generator does the same at half the strength. Telling them apart needs about 50 genuine pairs. Grade A census, C for any Voynich lean.
- Glyphs are built from features (v25): glyphs that share strokes behave alike (font-image shape vs context r +0.28 to +0.44, ZL, IT, Currier A and B), as in Hangul (+0.25 to +0.53) and unlike Latin or Greek (~0); a held-out glyph's behaviour is predictable from its strokes, and the bench and p/f crossbar act as additive features. Not misreading or position; stroke-edit copying explains part. Grade A (shape predicts behaviour), B (compositional), C (sound values). Swap pairs are not one stroke apart (demoted). Kill attempt (v28): handwritten graphic-unit transcriptions (allographs, abbreviation marks, minims) give a weaker real link (+0.1 to +0.26, printed ~0); the Voynich stays 1.5-3x above them and Ethiopic, under any EVA segmentation; positional and free variants cannot produce it. Survives. Gap closed (v35): invented scripts with arbitrary shapes (Cherokee, Deseret) and real cipher alphabets (Copiale, Borg) are null to weak (-0.01 to +0.18); featural inventions are positive (Tengwar +0.39 to +0.54 but only +0.08 to +0.20 without its vowel marks; Canadian syllabics +0.13 to +0.21 hand; Shavian +0.10 to +0.13), so the Voynich (+0.28 to +0.43) is not explained by being a designed script or cipher (A). New: its most similar glyphs (gallows k~t, p~f, benched forms, ch~sh) are near-interchangeable in context (index 0.81 vs 0.36 frequency-matched, like a planted homophone cipher, unlike any featural script at 0.17-0.37), while the graded link survives without the gallows (+0.33 to +0.37): twins plus features (A measurement, C reading).
- The lines were not a shuffled deck (v27): re-sequencing body lines for continuity (junction trained only within lines, shared words; exact DP / annealing, also over the whole book) restores Italian prose at 4x chance and finds planted continuity in >= ~30% of joins, but the Voynich matches its Markov, word-shuffle and cross-page nulls everywhere; no chains across pages. Lines do not run on into each other in any recoverable order. Grade B negative (book scale low power).
- Features do not spread (v29): no stroke feature agrees through words as vowel harmony does (Turkish, Hungarian, Finnish and planted harmony found blind; Voynich 0), and the strong word-junction dependence is a pairing (y.q-, n.o-, n.ch-) with same-glyph avoidance and no sandhi, not feature agreement. Grade A negative / B.
- The dialect ladder (v30): the cheapest A->B glyph rewrite costs what Bavarian->Alemannic costs (ReF, 1350-1450), more than two scribes copying the Commedia, far less than Latin->Italian, a spelling reform or any cipher key change. But it is unusually regular: in every run the top rules turn A's -chy/-chol/-chor endings into B's -chedy/-edy, and the rules learned on herbal pages carry over to unseen sections. Two conventions of one system, not two languages or keys. Grade B. B's hands 2 and 3 are at null distance.
- Similar plants, similar text (v38): a real herbal (Gerard 1636) lets learned image embeddings predict the words of unseen pages (rank 0.60, z 5.6-7.4); the Voynich herbal does not (0.49), keeping only a faint diffuse link in Currier A hand 1 (z ~3, four networks, two transliterations, marginal after a handwriting-image control). Grade B that the text does not describe its plant as a herbal does; Grade C lead. Hand-crafted shape/colour descriptors are blind even in Gerard (A, method).
- Collapsing the twins does not help (v39): merging the interchangeable look-alike glyphs (k~t, p~f, benched gallows, ch~sh), every pair the data call interchangeable, or any of 260 random merge sets leaves the Voynich generator-like on held-out pages in ZL and IT2a (P(language) 0.11-0.21; no frequency arrow; no lexical gaps), while the Copiale cipher merged by its key flips from generator 0.97 to language/conlang 0.92 and planted splits in Latin/German/Italian are repaired. The twins do act as variants (word types collapse 4-10 sd beyond random merges, B). Grade A negative.

- Among deliberately meaningless books (v42): against the Codex Seraphinianus (one hand, declared asemic; OCR transliteration at 18% error, every other corpus noise-matched), the Voynich's nearest real neighbour among 130+ texts is the CS, and an ASEMIC class trained only on the CS absorbs the Voynich (0.64-0.98 at 3000 tokens). It absorbs no held-out corpus, odd text or minim-stroke rewrite. The CS is itself called "generator" by most v31 classifiers, so "generator-like" does not mean "mechanical generator". Grade B. Habits do not harden: the CS drifts away from generator statistics (B negative). Difference: the CS has page topic and forges like a herbal (AUC 0.99); the Voynich has no topic (0.94). Grade C: long asemic book rather than generator, with n = 1 machine-read reference.
- No island of language (v43): scanning windows, groups and held-out line halves with ~300 random calibrated detectors finds planted German/Latin islands (>= 600 tokens) at their place and nothing in uniform generator text. The Voynich's most language-like stretch is biological quire M, hand 2 (~f75-f84, 2-5k tokens; both transcriptions, held-out lines), Grade B, but it only reaches the edge of the language band, keeps the generator-like gap ratio (1.03 vs languages 0.65-0.87), and its shift is a repetition texture, not a move toward language. Grade A negative for a language island >= 1,200 tokens; scattered page-sized islands remain below resolution.
- No source shape (v58): entry-length sequences of 25 candidate works (herbals incl. Circa instans, Macer, Dioscorides; recipe books incl. Antidotarium Nicolai; Hyginus, Pliny, Celsus, Konrad von Megenberg) do not align with any Voynich section in folio order, in 1,500 guessed leaf orders per search, or under glyph/line rulers, although a translated + opaquely encoded copy is found from ~87 units (z 8-24). Grade A method, B negative (bounded by corpus and recension).
- Let the ink decide the alphabet (v70): 11,232 alphabets learned from the Beinecke ink (no EVA), with random-alphabet nulls, a synthetic Voynich-like hand and real Latin pages as controls. Choosing an alphabet by language-likeness is invalid (true units lower the score in all 6 control languages; A). No ink alphabet makes the Voynich language-like or tracks pages or sections better than EVA, and the pen shows no pair EVA mis-splits (B, negative). Unit width alone identifies pages (A, confound). The real Latin control fails at 2000-px scans, so EVA stands but the question is bounded, not closed.

**Already published:** most of the above (arXiv 2608.17096, 2604.19762; Vogt 2012). Ours are independent replications.

**Possibly new, small:**
- the m/g one-per-line quota;
- the ch↔e trade-off by line;
- the split of word-class variance across section, page, paragraph and line;
- the verbose-cipher merge test with encrypted-language controls.

## Random programs (r2)
- ~8.9 M random and evolved small programs (copy rules, counters, lookup tables, L-systems), scored by held-out description length against Kneser-Ney, with shuffled, Markov-resynthesised, planted (grille, ledgers) and natural (Latin, Linear B) controls. Planted generators recovered 3/3. In no script does a program beat the baselines beyond its nulls: LA and PE programs only 'copy from the entry above' (gone against a document cache), Voynich gains nothing over a 3-glyph Markov chain. Grade A negative for short generators of this kind.

## Grow the script in a box (r3)
- 62,000 simulated societies evolve scripts (iterated learning) for ledger or text worlds; ABC on blind statistics. Controls: Latin/Italian come out as text and LB as a ledger; Ur III's 'many people, few goods' is never recovered, and people/goods counts and learning history cannot be identified from these sample sizes. Inferred worlds (all C): PE = mostly nameless counted lines with 100+ logograms; LA = named numbered ledger, no secrecy ('one commodity per tablet' killed by a held-out miss); Voynich = text in a small alphabet with word-final end sets and line-initial markers (~30-40% of lines). The Voynich 'needs secrecy' guess was killed. Held-out predictions: PE 3/4, LA 3/4, Voynich 1/4.

## Which script teaches which (x3)
- Predict the clay (pe17, `proto-elamite/loops/pe17_final.txt`): a blind, hash-frozen ranking from 2,000 random classifiers (plateau vs Susa writing) was scored against hXRF clay provenance (Yeganeh et al. 2025). A weak plateau writing fingerprint exists (held-out AUC 0.69, B). The five chemically foreign tablets point the right way in three of four tests (ST-11 top 10%), but Fisher p is 0.14 and power is only about 12% (C). There is no itinerant-scribe signal.
- Blind seriation (pe19, `proto-elamite/loops/pe19_final.txt`): hash-frozen orders of all PE tablets by evolving traits agree with the CDLI stratigraphic levels above random (Susa rho +0.47, Malyan AUC 0.92), but mostly through tablet size. A planted 40-trait drift is invisible, and Uruk IV/III is not seriated in the right direction. No relative chronology of signs (C). One prediction stands: the order of the 5 CahDAFI 8 Acropole tablets.
- Flock arithmetic (pe20, `proto-elamite/loops/pe20_final.txt`): 8 herd signs were assigned blind to animal categories by herd biology (200,000 draws plus Gibbs). Herd counts are coupled out of sample (held-out p 0.008; imputation +0.86 bits per cell, B). M362 and M362~a are reference counts, and the numbers alone recover the scribe's plain vs ~a split (B-). Biology does not name the categories, because the same search mislabels Ur III ewes and kids, so ewe/ram/young readings stay C.
- Transfer test (x3, `proto-elamite/loops/x3_final.txt`): transfer learning across 10 corpora with relabelling controls. Accounting corpora teach LA and PE by format, not by sign identity; Voynich learns from nothing; identity transfer appears only where transliterations share labels (LA-LB +0.12 bits/token, a replication; LA-Greek none). Frequency rank cannot reveal shared signs (planted control), so LA-PE-Voynich sign sharing is untestable this way.

## Bottom line
The cheap data-only attacks on these three scripts are largely spent:
- Residual test (v45): the word choices the Voynich rules leave open repeat within bifolios and quires but track no drawing or section; they behave like rule misfit plus drift, and the free vocabulary concentrates on paragraph-first lines (`voynich/loops/v45_final.txt`).
- Opening-line rule-breakers (v49): they do not act like subject terms or borrowed names and form no second language layer. Calibrated against a real Italian-Latin herbal through a planted code, they are body vocabulary written with the opening-line gallows habit (`voynich/loops/v49_final.txt`).
- Let a speech model listen (v51): Voynich text rendered as audio under 565 random phone mappings and scored by a multilingual speech recogniser sounds slightly more speech-like than its own shuffle, but so do Markov chains and Voynich-fitted generators, and the languages pass only weakly. No mapping, section or heard-language signal survives held-out tests (`voynich/loops/v51_final.txt`).
- Which twin is the slip (v63): checked against 1,775 real struck words in five Latin manuscript witnesses, Voynich adjacent near-repeats show no copying order: no wrong-word-first, no gain from deleting either twin, no link to line ends. About 4 million held-out slip-operator sets find only line-position drift (o>l, ch>d, -l before -y). Glyph slips >= 1% and any slip-and-rewrite >= 10% are excluded; no lost exemplar is recoverable (v17 stands). Grade B negative (`voynich/loops/v63_final.txt`).
- Textual criticism without a language (v54): near-repeated passages collated as witnesses. The method recovers planted and real medieval spelling classes. The Voynich's recurring passages return with a one-glyph change (ch~sh, e-run/-edy ending, q-) about as often as unchanged, 6-8x real copying and like a copy-and-modify generator; no stemma by hand (`voynich/loops/v54_final.txt`).
- Where the pen ran dry (v57): ink fade used as a clock (4,914 writing orders) and re-dips located on sub-word ink traces. Latin scribes do not re-dip at punctuation, so the ink cannot mark phrases. The Voynich's -y|q- re-dip preference is glyph shape (killed by shuffled junctions). The text was written in reading order (C), and a Voynich-only word-level ink sawtooth is C+ (`voynich/loops/v57_final.txt`).
- The book's own Rosetta stone (v59): Currier A and B treated as a parallel corpus without alignment. Random A->B transducers (~45,000), a spelling-blind word aligner (validated through an opaque encoding) and a drawing pivot find no translation that carries contexts or words across. The v30 ending shift is a change of form only. B herbal pages do share A-herbal vocabulary through word beginnings (grade B), so the endings look re-encoded and the beginnings kept (C) (`voynich/loops/v59_final.txt`).
- Hot or cold, wet or dry (v60): the medieval complexion grid measured from six herbals and used as an answer key for millions of candidate slots. Real coded herbals do not give back their own complexion slot under the same searches, so the grid cannot find a slot at this corpus size; the Voynich equals its nulls. A published "ched- = hot, qok- = dry" reading is killed: it makes hot the rare quality (0.03-0.15 against 0.74-0.85 in every herbal) and tracks Currier language. Not cracked.
- Undo the sandhi (v61): about 600 million edge-rewrite rules and 45,000 rule systems, calibrated on Sanskrit, Italian, Welsh and planted sandhi. No coupling crosses a Voynich line break, yet line-final words are not base forms. Undoing the coupling joins different words instead of edge variants, and making beginnings look like line-initial forms strengthens the coupling. Sandhi is killed in both directions (B). Currier A's line-edge lean is unexplained (C). Not cracked (`voynich/loops/v61_final.txt`).
- The text remembers which page came next (v65): about 130 million physically allowed quire arrangements scored for page-to-page carry-over, calibrated on continuous prose in an opaque code and checked against published physical adjacency (picture across the Q13 centre, paint offsets, quire signatures). Voynich pages carry over weakly (a fifth of prose) with no direction, and no page order is stable. A copy-and-modify generator carries over 20x more than prose, so carry-over cannot show meaning. Grade C lean: the physically documented quire-centre bifolios are the ones whose halves share most vocabulary.
- The Voynich is verse? (v62): rhyme schemes, metre and refrains searched with thousands of random ending definitions, glyph weightings and line signatures, with rhymed, hexameter, terza-rima, litany and prose controls through an opaque code. The controls are recovered on held-out pages; the Voynich shows no rhyme (slight anti-rhyme), no metre (line totals vary more than chance, because a line's words are alike) and no refrain beyond the known first-glyph margin chain. Not verse (grade B). Not cracked.
- Each line says one thing? (v67): each line read as one word written in variants, with a consensus token per line (1,156 rules, 120 learned compressors, planted Latin and Italian controls). Voynich lines are far too unlike for this (about one variant per line); what a line shares is a first-glyph mode, and the line-token streams show page-local drift, not word order. Grade B negative.
- One word per line is the message? (v73): about 8,100 rules picked one marked word per line, with real ingredient lists hidden one per line as controls. Positional and pointer marking is recovered in the controls. In the Voynich every search ends on the line-final word, which has a vocabulary of its own but no page-level information. Grade B negative; spelling-only marking stays open.
- The book's mind map matches a real one? (v66): the Voynich's page-bursty keyword network was aligned with a medieval medical concept network built from five-language herbals (about 65,000 annealing restarts, rewired, Markov and concept-graph nulls, held-out halves). The Voynich beats every null, but so do Kafka, Caesar and Descartes, and coded herbals never recover their own concepts, even with the right keywords handed in. Graph shape at this size carries no subject information. Grade B (method, negative). Not cracked.
- The crack attempt (v72): the surviving results were assembled into one two-layer model (a message layer of payload tokens under a surface layer of twins, benches, e-padding, optional q-, edge pairing and sealed lines). The extraction was frozen on even leaves and tested on odd leaves against five generators and against planted Italian, Latin and German texts put through the same machinery, which it gives back (purity 0.96-1.00). The Voynich payload has no page topic, no recurring phrases and no lexical gaps. 77% of its word-to-word information is the last-glyph pairing, and the rest (about 1/5 of Latin) is repeats, space-split pieces and ch-/qok- alternations. B (negative, calibrated). Frozen predictions concern space widths on the vellum. Not cracked.
