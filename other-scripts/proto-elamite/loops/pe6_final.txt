Plain answer: a fitted fake Susa writes tablets that match the real corpus on 40 of 43 statistics. Its writing habits are well pinned: one general opener, often missing. Most tablets list named units, and the class sign is usually omitted after a name. Totals are frequent and often off. Its size is not pinned: the number of offices (2-49) and workers per office (2-104) stay open. Mapping signs back to roles gives grade A to only 4 class signs (M072, M346 counted; M002, M297 capacity), all already known. It also gives A to 11 'name-spelling' signs (M099, M136, M005 ...). The planted economies show that the method works. The real script, however, fits the simulated roles poorly: the shuffled corpus gets class probabilities up to 0.82. The model also fails on one point: the number system belongs to the tablet more than to the good.
