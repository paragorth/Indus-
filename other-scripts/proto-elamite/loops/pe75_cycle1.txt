pe75 cycle 1: WAS THE CLAY CUT TO A RULER? (7 Oct 2026)
Pivot note: the brief (tablet size planned from the header; blank room vs signs) overlaps pe21 (clay area vs text amount; sized-to-fit tablets are an ordinary cross-section of systems, offices, seals, totals; size does not predict count size beyond text amount; totals sit in reserved room) and pe10 (squeeze, end-of-face crowding, 20,000 random squeeze-sign sets). It was therefore NOT re-run as briefed. Cycle 1 pivots to a stranger physical question the text never touches: do the tablet dimensions themselves carry a length unit (a quantum), i.e. a ruler in the clay? Cycle 2 then asks the brief's question through variables pe21 did not use (thickness, aspect, thickness-for-area, slack) and runs the reverse (sign) test.
Searched first: other-scripts/LITERATURE.md, FINDINGS.md, STRATEGIES.md (no quantum / unit-of-length analysis of tablet dimensions); a web search found only general Mesopotamian metrology (finger ~17 mm), no tablet-dimension quantum study.
Data: CDLI catalogue (cdli-gh/data release cdli_cat.csv, scratchpad; height/width/thickness mm). Re-checked live against https://cdli.earth/artifacts/<id>.json for 15 random PE tablets: 15/15 identical. 781 PE tablets with all three dimensions and no 'fragment'/'%' preservation note (155 marked 'complete'). Controls from the same catalogue: proto-cuneiform (Uruk IV-III) and Ur III tablets. Scripts: tools/pe75_common.py, pe75_cycle1.py, pe75_cycle1b.py, pe75_cycle1c.py, pe75_cycle1d.py. Checkpoints data/pe75_ckpt/.
