# Idea: the 14 counted signs before the standard M288 allotment (pe28) should be a class in every other respect, should let us name
# unseen members, and (as in Ur III) might split into sub-classes by ration size.
| PE-32.F1 | Controls | Calibration: random 14-sets give composite p <= 0.05 in 0/40 (position and left-neighbour families anti-conservative, 20-22%, not used). Planted PE sub-classes 120 / 30: 7 of 8 signs grouped correctly | PASS; sub-class power at PE size is low |
| PE-32.F2 | Is C14 a class? | Composite z 3.1-3.6 (p 0.0005) against frequency-matched sets, against the other M288 predecessors and against signs on the same tablets. Shared: small counts (mostly 1), tablet headers (z 4.3), next-line system (3.6), tablet co-occurrence (6.2), little herd context (2.6), line system (2.1). Unalike in what precedes them (left neighbour z -2) | B: the rationed units are one class of person-like line ends, with varied names in front |

| PE-32.F6 | Format | The standard amount sits on a bare 'M288 , n' line after the person line (36 of 37); M288 lines that end a name string are almost never standard. No standard M288 line is followed by a smaller capacity line (0 of 37) | B: the standard allotment is a two-line format of its own, distinct from the pe16 adult-plus-smaller records |
Bottom line: the 14 signs before the standard ration form a real class (B): they share counts, headers, next lines and tablets beyond every null, and their features re-find hidden members on unseen tablets. But the class does not grow: the features name no new member that then takes the standard ration, and allotment sizes do not split it into sub-classes. Everyone in the class gets the same 1/2 N01, with an occasional double. Not cracked.
