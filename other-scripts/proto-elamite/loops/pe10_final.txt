Overall verdict: NULL. Proto-Elamite scribes show no measurable response to running out of clay -- no
shortening at face ends, no short forms, no merges, no variant switching. The decoder therefore yields no
"bilingual of the script with itself". Two by-products are solid: (i) the Ur III calibration (squeeze-driven
abbreviation exists but explains < 0.5% of length variance; edge placement is an outcome of length, not a
cause), which tells any future layout-based attack on PE, Linear A or Voynich that it needs > 5,000 repeated
units; (ii) the first entry after the header is +0.8 signs longer than the tablet mean (layout fact, not squeeze).
Grades: A none; B "no end-of-face shortening > 0.4 signs" (calibrated by the planted control); C the other
absences; the single C hint (bare M387 as a short form) is killed.
Next stranger idea suggested by the data: the space-insensitivity means entry length is fixed by content, so
entry length itself is a clean content variable (no layout noise) -- usable as a covariate in name/role models.
