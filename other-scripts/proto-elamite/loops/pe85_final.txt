Grades
- B: the header end is MADE square when a header is written, not CHOSEN from a ready blank. A choose-the-squarer-end writer
  could give at most top r 0.18 and would make the other end rounder (r -0.22); the data show 0.36 and +0.07. Tablets
  without a header have no squared end (top minus bottom 0.000), as in proto-cuneiform. Would kill: 3D scans showing that
  headed and unheaded blanks have the same total end shape distribution and only differ in orientation.
- B (replication on held-out tablets): pe83's top-end claim holds on 103 line-art-only tablets never used in pe81/83/85
  cycles 1-2 (r 0.364, p 0.002).
- B (negative, upper bounds at this n): no storage or handling use-model explains which tablets carry the square end.
  Template series (24-41 tablets), seal-mates (6-19), excavated same-square tablets (38 pairs) and header families are
  not alike at the header end; heavier tablets are not flatter there (no drying-on-end); retrieval wear and grip
  predictions fail on held-out volumes; planted versions of each model were found.
- C (meaning): squaring the top is part of the act of heading a record, done the same way in every office, series and
  find lot: a 'this is the start' gesture or a step of preparing the writing surface for the header, not a filing
  device. Would support: the squared end shows tool marks or a flat facet on 3D scans of headed tablets only. Would kill:
  a storage find in which tablets of one group share end shape regardless of header.

  difference <= 0 on new tablets.
