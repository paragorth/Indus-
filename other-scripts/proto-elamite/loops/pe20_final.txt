pe20 FINAL -- "the flock does the arithmetic"
Tools: tools/pe20_common.py, pe20_cycle1.py, pe20_cycle2.py, pe20_cycle3.py. Numbers: data/pe20_cycle*_*.json. Cycle rows: loops/pe20_cycle1..3.txt. Checkpoints and logs: data/pe20_ckpt/ (git-ignored).
Idea: if herd-entry signs name animal categories, their counts must obey flock demography. 8 herd signs were assigned blind to categories (breeding female, male, young by sex, total, wether, unconstrained; two "species"). Assignments were scored by a herd-biology likelihood with pastoral rate ranges, by 200,000 random draws plus Gibbs posteriors, on the 10 herd blocks of MDP 17,096+325+380. The best assignments were re-tested on 35 held-out records from 22 tablets and on the chained herd lists.

Plain answer: the herd numbers do behave like flock arithmetic. Inside each herd block of the 10-herd account, the counts depend on one another. A model fitted on other blocks predicts hidden counts (+0.86 bits per cell, as well as planted herds), and the best reading transfers to 22 other tablets better than shuffled numbers. Without seeing the sign names, the search splits the signs into the scribe's own plain and ~a series, each with its own reference count (M362, M362~a). The biology does not name the categories, though. On real Ur III herding texts with known categories, the same search confuses ewes with rams/wethers and kids with males, and random "biologies" fit PE about as well as the real one (35% of 300 beat it). So M362 = ewes, M006 = rams and M367 = young remain guesses (C). Not cracked; no sign reading is established.
