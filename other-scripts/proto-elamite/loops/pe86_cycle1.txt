pe86 cycle 1 (7 Oct 2026): the clay as witness. Where do the clay and our text header definition disagree, and why?
Plan and kill lines written before running (tools/pe86_cycle1.py; data/pe86_ckpt/c1.json).
Clay label: residual top-corner fill (cf_top ranked, size/aspect/thickness/line count/damage/reverse removed; pe83/pe85
outlines, no new photos), 589 tablets with intact line 1. Text label: common.header() (line 1 has signs, no numerals).

 1a Disagreement scan: inside the 184 text-UNHEADED tablets, which text features (line-1 form, line-1 tokens, first
    token, line-2 first token; >= 5 tablets) go with a square top? Inside the 405 HEADED tablets, which go with a round
    top? Family-wise null: max |r| over all features under 500 stratified shuffles of the squareness labels.
    A feature counts only if it beats the family-wise 95% line.
 1b Pre-named natural test: a header-shaped line 1 (no numerals) whose sign the editor could only read as x or '?'
    (the loader turns '?' into x, so our definition misses it). If these are real headers, their top should look like
    headed tablets. Kill: mean residual top of this group closer to the other unheaded tablets than to the headed.
 1c Planted header sign: token PLANT put into line 1 of 15 unheaded tablets drawn with weight rank(top)^2; 5 plants.
    Pass if PLANT is the top feature and beats the family line in >= 4/5.
 1d Bottom-end control: the same scan on the bottom end. A feature that explains the top AND the bottom is a whole-
    tablet effect, not a header effect.

Results (data/pe86_ckpt/c1.json). 589 tablets with intact line 1 (405 text-headed, 184 not). Family-wise 95% lines from
500 stratified shuffles of the squareness labels: unheaded scan 0.214 (23 features), headed scan 0.155 (47 features).
