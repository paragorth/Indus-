# pe78 cycle 1: fluctuation scaling (Taylor's law) of the accounts (7 Oct 2026). tools/pe78_common.py, pe78_cycle1.py; log data/pe78_ckpt/c1.log (git-ignored).
# Prior-art check / PIVOT: the brief (agent-based herd simulator scoring random sign -> age/sex-class assignments and season orders on count vectors, held-out re-test, bones as outside ruler) was already run as pe20 (200,000 random assignments + Gibbs, herd-biology likelihood, held-out; labels uncalibrated on Ur III), pe37 (Malyan bones, 5.76 M assignments, A negative), pe53 (lambing-season clock) and pe54 (flock histories with random demography, A method fail). Web search: no published herd simulation of PE. So the brief was not rerun. Pivot: simulate the animals only to learn how living counts FLUCTUATE. Ecology's Taylor law var = a*mean^b, computed per count key (last sign of the entry) across the tablets that carry it >= 2 times, gives a unit-free signature (slope b barely depends on decimal vs sexagesimal values). Question: do living populations (herd ABM with random births, deaths, culling, environment, years) leave a fluctuation signature that rules (rate x heads), measured goods (lognormal) and no-biology Poisson do not, and does it type keys of KNOWN kind in Ur III and proto-cuneiform? No study applying fluctuation scaling to ancient accounts was found (web search 7 Oct 2026).
# Raw first look: b is about 2 for almost every key in all three corpora (PE M288 2.13, M346 1.45, M367 1.82; PC SZE 2.04, UDU 2.30; Ur III udu 2.19, sze 2.22). Key-level Mann-Whitney LIVING vs GOODS: PC no feature (all p >= 0.44); Ur III intercept a (AUC 0.79), scatter (0.72) and overall CV (0.84) differ, slope b does not (0.54).
