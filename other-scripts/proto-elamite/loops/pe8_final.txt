Plain answer: NO. Proto-Elamite administration (Susa) did not use a small set of fixed forms. The same method finds
6 planted forms, but on the real corpus it finds only the 5 loose clusters that a no-form null corpus also produces.
These are habits, not templates (with the share of tablets each covers):
  A headed list, total on the reverse (21%); B short headed list, 2-3 entries, no total, reverse blank or continued
  (39%); C no header, one-sign entries (11%); D headed list of class-sign entries plus a total (13%); E longer headed
  list, entries carried onto the reverse, sometimes 2 columns (16%).
None of them predicts the number system. The decimal/capacity choice belongs to the individual tablet (same-tablet
entries: 0.34 bits) but is invisible in its layout and only weakly shared by museum or publication neighbours.
Headers, not forms, are what neighbouring tablets share.
