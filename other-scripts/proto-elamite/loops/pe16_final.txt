Idea: size units from biology. An allotment = need (litres/day, by recipient class, from energy needs, grain energy and bulk density, and animal feeding ranges) x period / unit size. The class and the period of each tablet are latent. The texts are asked only for the unit in adult-man-days (a common factor on needs is degenerate with the unit); litres come from the physiological prior for an adult man.

Posterior summary for the user's questions:
- Unit size: N39C = 4.6 l (90% 1.5-14) x {1/30, 1, 12}; the all-entries estimate is 0.6-1.4 l. Robust statement: the PE capacity units span a few tenths of a litre to tens of litres; one N24 is between one adult-day and one adult-month of grain.
- Period: not identified by amounts alone (exact degeneracy with unit size, broken only by tablets that mix periods; aliasing 1 time in 3 even on Ur III subsets). Fitted: record tablets split monthly / yearly; with the bowl check: daily / ~12-day.
- Recipients: one large plus one smaller ration per record; man-sized vs dependant-sized; animals not separable from children.
- Prediction outside the corpus (grade C): if PE-period (Susa III) standard vessels are measured, a pair near 0.6 l and 1.9 l in a 1:3 ratio would support N30C/N24 at the /30 point; vessels clustering near 4-5 l would support the fitted point (N39C). Nothing here is "cracked".
