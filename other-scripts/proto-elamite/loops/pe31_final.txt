pe31 FINAL -- interchangeable name signs
Tools: tools/pe31_lib.py, pe31_cycle1.py, pe31_cycle1b.py, pe31_cycle2.py, pe31_cycle3.py, pe31_cycle4.py. Numbers and logs: data/pe31_ckpt/ (git-ignored). Rows: loops/pe31_cycle1..4.txt.
Idea: if some PE signs in name-like strings write sounds, spelling variation should make some sign pairs interchangeable (same string with X or Y, same neighbours, same positions). Every pair of signs (base signs; variant graphs separately) was scored inside 1,416 name-like strings (entry lines, final class sign stripped, 2+ signs) with minimal-pair frames, the v35 split-half context index, position profiles, tablet co-occurrence and a Bayesian merge-evidence score, all against frequency-matched pairs, 200 within-string shuffles and family-wise / FDR correction. Controls: Ur III and Old Babylonian seal names (same value, different index), Linear B doublets, planted homophones (per token and per tablet). Prior work: Born et al. 2019 cluster PE signs by distribution; nobody calibrated a homophone / allograph detector on known-homophone name corpora, and pe10 only tested variants as space-savers.

Plain answer: no interchangeable sign pair can be shown in Proto-Elamite names, and the controls show why: even in Ur III, Old Babylonian and Linear B names (and with planted homophones), spelling variants are too rare in 1,000-1,500 names to stand out from slot-mates. What does hold is that a sign and its ~variants behave alike inside names (B): same slot, same neighbours. That fits graphic variants but also sub-kinds of one thing (pe20 split M362 from M362~a by their numbers); it gives no sign of the ~variants writing different sounds. Whether a syllabic subset exists cannot be answered this way: the same test cannot even find the syllables in Old Babylonian names. Nothing is read; not cracked.
