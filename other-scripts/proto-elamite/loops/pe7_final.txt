pe7 FINAL -- "treat the names like DNA": do Proto-Elamite entry middles carry kinship / lineage structure?
Tools: tools/pe7_build.py, pe7_common.py (stochastic edit model fitted by hard EM; NJ; quartet delta; Fitch parsimony on element characters with SPR search, NJ + random restarts; Metropolis tree MCMC; label-association and pair-permutation tests), pe7_cycle1..4.py, pe7_cycle4b.py, pe7_sum1.py, pe7_sum2.py. Data: data/pe7_corpora.json, data/pe7_cycle{1,2,3,4,4b}.json + logs, checkpoints data/pe7_ckpt/. Cycle rows: loops/pe7_cycle1..4.txt.

Plain answer: Proto-Elamite middles do NOT show detectable kinship/lineage structure of the kind real Near Eastern names have. They form no strong tree, no clock, and no clustering by office or header. What they do show, very strongly, is that names on one tablet share rare signs (2.4x chance), far above father-son sharing in Ur III (1.11x) or OB (1.25x), and not as adjacent kin blocks. That fits tablet-local naming pools better than families.
Element grades (as inherited elements): none A, none B. C (would support: same sign shared by co-listed names on tablets of different scribes/sites; would kill: sharing confined to single tablets): the tablet-shared signs M388, M124, M370, M054, M001, M376, M075, M149, M103, |M217+M388|, |M136+M365|; the herd-name compound |M362+X| with M207/M059/M005. |M288+1(N01)| (a numeral compound) is among the top sharers, a warning that part of the effect is non-name tablet vocabulary.
Caveats: 95% Susa; names = pe4 middles (may include non-name descriptors); Ur III/OB positives are seal patronymics 1,000+ years later and in a glottographic script; distinct names lose repeat counts; the clock test has low power.
