control mark computed from the rest of the entry (a checksum, residue, parity or size-class mark) for an auditor?

Plain answer: Proto-Elamite entries carry no check digits. Among about 130,000 candidate rules, none predicts the first
sign, the last sign before the number, or the sign after the header from the number (residues, digit sums, running sums,
random modular functions) better than the shuffled null. The same search finds a planted check sign on 10% of entries at
z ~10 and the real Ur III weight-unit marker at z 43. Within a tablet and a number system, entry size does not choose
the slot sign either: the only size-related sign split is the known capacity / counted / fraction split between systems.
Totals carry no verification mark and no check sign, and the arithmetic mismatches are not the one-sign copying slips
that a check digit would catch.
Grades: A none. B (negative, calibrated): "no residue-type check sign on more than ~3-5% of entries in the first or last
slot of PE entries"; "no size-class sign beyond the number system within tablets"; "PE total mismatches are not
single-numeral slips". B (positive, structural): "a minority of PE lists (~20 tablets) are written as fixed two-line
records; the companion line repeats after each main line". C guess: the companion line (e.g. M376 with 1(N08A) after a
person line with M288, 2(N01)) is a per-record allotment written once per record (would support: the companion's number
scales with the main line's number across lists; would kill: a constant companion whatever the main line says).
Sampling: 56 fully legible total tablets for cycle 3; any absence is an upper bound (< 3/56 for tablet-level marks).
