ANSWER IN PLAIN WORDS
1. A text is a sequence. Among texts of >= 3 signs, the same sign multiset recurs in another order in 24 of 2,811 copy pairs (0.9%), against 46%
   expected if order were free inside the frame slots and 92% if the text were a set (cycle 1). Seals: 1 of 184-227. Held-out sites: 0 of ~150
   (< 3/150). The few anagrams are swaps of two neighbouring signs (235-240 fish, 740-760 before the suffix) or whole-text reversals that are
   recording errors (413-892-824 x11 vs 824-892-413 x1).
2. How much of the order is rule-governed: of the sign pairs that can be tested (co-occurring in >= 5 texts), 50% have a fixed order after FDR
   correction, 8% are free, 28% too rare to call, 14% lean one way without reaching the cut. By pair instances: 40-43% fixed, 4% free, 43-46%
   too rare. Of the 395-399 fixed pairs, 74% follow the frame's slot order (opener -> marker -> middle -> title -> closer -> suffix) and 26%
   order two signs INSIDE one slot; 0 go against the frame. Same-slot fixed pairs: 84-98 vs a frame-only null max of 27-31. The fixed pairs form
   one consistent partial order (a single cycle, 741/820, 6 texts) with a longest chain of 16-19 signs: 320 -> 920 -> 60 -> 741 -> 2 -> 803 ->
   32 -> 350 -> 798 -> 415 -> 220 -> 233 -> 705 -> 255 -> 435 -> 690 -> 740 -> 400. So the middle is not a bag of name signs: it has its own
   ordered positions (marked jar 741 and leaf-tree 803 first; then numerals 32; then 350 -> 798 -> 415; then the fish words; then 705-33 or
   the 255-435-690 title; then the closer). Held-out sites agree with the Mohenjo-daro + Harappa directions 0.90-0.91 (frame-only null 0.83-0.84),
   and for same-slot pairs 0.83 (null 0.59-0.60), at all three levels.
3. Where the free part lives (cycle 3): the numeral signs (which of 1 / 31 / 32 / 2 stands first: 2 <-> 31 19:17, 31 <-> 32 7:10), the plain
   fish 220 against the modified fish 240 / 235 (15:12, 7:13; S296's chain holds only among the modified fish), and W2 with name signs (its
   post-opener connective use vs its numeral use). Free pairs co-occur barely above chance (O/E 1.2; fixed pairs 1.6) and are not avoided, so they
   are independent signs, not coordinate members of a set and not alternatives. One genuinely loose construction: the Harappa 2-sign voucher
   'count + W700', reversed on 15% of tablets (22% of moulded TAB:B vs 8% of incised TAB:I), either a label whose two parts can stand on either
   side or mirrored moulds; a photo check of H tablets reading 700-33 would settle it.
4. Object types: seals and tablets are equally rigid (new-order share 0.012-0.018 vs 0.78-0.83 for a set). Sealings 0 of 29. Pots: the prediction
   'labels are freer' is UNTESTABLE here: 0 multisets of >= 3 signs recur on the 80 complete direction-recorded pots (P = 0.70). Direction-unrecorded
   pots were excluded because 21-30% of them are stored backwards (S-DARK-8.4), which would manufacture anagrams.
5. Calibration (cycle 3): planted free order gives 0 fixed / 47% free pairs and 67% anagram groups; planted strict order gives 100% fixed / 0 free / 0
   anagrams; Ur III seal legends taken whole give 73% fixed / 12% free / 5.7% anagram groups; Ur III owner-name syllables 95% / 2% / 1.5%. Indus
   (50% / 8% / 16% anagram groups, 7% without the vouchers) sits with the whole Ur III legend, a fixed formula holding a name, and far from a set.
