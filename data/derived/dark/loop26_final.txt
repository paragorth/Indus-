VERDICT, PLAINLY
1. The middle is NOT a product code of fully independent attributes: independence is rejected at every level (pair dependency 2-3x the planted
   code, 7.5% pair gain vs 1%).
2. It is NOT a lexicon of compounds either: once each distinct text counts once, an element barely predicts its partners (7% of elements; a
   lexicon gives 38%, Ur III names 20%), the first element says nothing about the last (0.9%), and whatever dependency exists does not travel
   between cities (3% across sites; S-DARK-4.2 agrees).
3. What is left is a FREE COMBINATION of a nearly closed inventory (~860 elements) with a short list of frozen pairs: numeral + item terms
   (4+390, 3+900, 17+575, 33+705/706, 32+877), pre-jar pairs (255+435, 220+415, 415+798, 60+550, 407+845, 35+171, 13+840) and the frame
   leftovers the parser does not strip (176+740, inner opener + W2). These cover ~9% of pairs and ~1/4 of tokens; the other 3/4 combine with
   no partner preference. Closest planted model: an attribute-like code whose 'slots' cannot be recovered (cycle 3's method fails its own
   positive control; the only mutually exclusive class found is the numerals: one count per middle).
4. (d) Quantity seals are the most code-like (MI 0.9%, exclusions > compounds: numerals exclude each other); office (jar) seals have no
   exclusions and a few frozen pre-jar compounds; neither looks like compound names. Tablets' apparent lexicon was whole-text repetition.
5. (e) Elements ~860 (matches S309's ~950 for the whole script); distinct middles >= 8,800 (lower bound) - combinatorial, as a code predicts,
   and consistent with S309 (95% of new texts new) and S321/S322/S-DARK-18 (middles do not reuse strings like names do).
Caveats: 86% of middles from MD + Harappa; held-out sites agree on direction (3% gain, 1 partner-predictor of 88, same avoidance excess on 77
pairs) but are small. Ur III dedup by name string is the conservative construction (S-DARK-18.2). The S310 parser leaves frame phrases in the
residue (33+705, 255+435, 176+740); purging them moves every Indus number further toward the code. Cycles 1-2 (undeduplicated) overstate the
lexical side and should be read with cycle 4.
