LOOP 38 FINAL: THE SPACE BUDGET (2026-10-03)
Question: was the text planned to fit the seal face, and does running out of room explain the texts without a closer?
Script: tools/dark_loop38.py (cycles 1-4); cycle files loop38_c1.txt .. loop38_c4.txt; glyph table loop38_glyph_widths.json;
ready-to-paste STRATEGIES.md rows in loop38_cycle1_row.txt .. loop38_cycle4_row.txt (S-DARK-38.1 - 38.4).
Data: data/raw/inscriptions.csv (5,680 rows; horizontal(mm)/vertical(mm)); merge levels from sign_allographs_levels.json;
all tests run at seq_raw, seq_strong, seq_all and agree everywhere (no result depends on a merge).

VERDICTS
1. Planned to the face or squeezed?  Neither 'sized to the text' nor 'written at a fixed size and squeezed at the end'.
   Face width grows as n^0.27 (seals; 0.22 other sites, 0.37 Harappa, 0.45 script-only seals; tablets 0.27-0.43), so the
   extra signs of a long text are absorbed by a smaller sign height chosen for the whole line, and ink per mm rises 2.3x
   from 2 to 8+ signs (38.2). The ink width of the specific signs adds < 1% of variance over the bare sign count and
   nothing on held-out sites (38.1). The first sign's width does not predict the last sign's width or its allograph, and
   narrow allographs do not gather on crowded faces or at text ends (38.4). The layout was decided once per seal, for
   the whole text, before cutting: 'planned at one height', not 'squeezed'.
2. Are missing closers truncations?  No, at all three levels (38.3). Closer-less texts are not denser, not on smaller
   faces, and the crowded ones end in MORE slot-final signs (W90, W400, W151), not fewer. Scribes who did run out of
   room wrapped to a second segment (62 of ~1,540 complete seals) and kept the closer at the ordinary rate (0.645 vs
   0.64, matched on site and length). Closer-less texts are a different ending class, as S289 / S-DARK-35 treat them.
3. Font-proxy caveat.  The lipi glyphs are 15-40% narrower than the cut signs on the one photo checked (M-6) but keep
   the rank order of widths, and every width test here is a rank or shuffle test, so the caveat limits absolute
   statements (e.g. 'ink at 1/3 line height overruns 32% of faces') but not the null verdicts. Allograph 'narrowness'
   is a property of the font drawings; only 10 variant pairs have enough tokens. A photo-measured width table for the
   60 commonest signs would be needed before any positive width effect could be claimed.

NOT CRACKED. No reading, no sound value; the result is a layout fact: Indus seal texts were laid out whole on a face that
was not resized for them, and the absence of a closer is a choice of ending, not an accident of space.
