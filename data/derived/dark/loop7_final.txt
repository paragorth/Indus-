S-DARK-7  FINAL: random-statistic fingerprint across scripts (4 cycles)
================================================================================
Files: loop7_cycle1.txt .. loop7_cycle4.txt (one STRATEGIES.md row each), tables loop7_cycle1_full.txt,
loop7_cycle2_tables.txt, loop7_cycle3_tables.txt, loop7_cycle4_tables.txt; bootstraps loop7_boot*.npz, loop7_meta.json.
Tools: tools/dark7_fingerprint.py, dark7_analyse.py, dark7_unigram.py, dark7_cycle2.py, dark7_cycle3.py, dark7_cycle4.py.

Method in one paragraph: 600 random text statistics (slice selector x 20 per-token properties x text aggregator x
corpus aggregator, 120 ratios, 60 slot entropies) computed on length-matched resamples (texts of 2-8 tokens, Indus
length weights, N=800, B=200 bootstraps) of Indus (seq_raw; seq_all; seals; non-seals; MD+H; other sites; random
halves), Proto-Elamite entry lines, Linear A lines, Linear B lines (words / syllables), Ur III seal legends (words /
syllables), Ur III names (syllables), Linear Elamite segments (syllables), Voynich lines (words / chars). Separation
= |z| > 3.93 (Bonferroni over 600). Controls: Indus split-half (2/600 false separations), merge level (0/600),
within-text shuffle (order dependence), unigram-resampled nulls (inventory vs sequence), held-out sites, text types.

Findings
1. NULL: no accounting-notation fingerprint. Indus, PE and LA agree-but-differ-from-LB/Ur III on 1/600 statistics,
   and that one is an inventory-size effect. Indus's nearest corpus at every level is Linear B (a language in
   accounting use): median |z| 1.34 all statistics, 1.27 non-numeral, 5.6 order-dependent; PE is at 2.48 / 1.81 / 10.9.
   PE and LA form their own pair (2.12). Beyond-unigram structure is LOWEST in Indus (0.24 of statistics differ from
   own unigram null) and HIGHEST in PE and LA (0.47, 0.46): the accounting entries are rigid templates; Indus is not.
2. Indus-specific residue that survives every control (sites, halves, text types, unigram null, 8 comparison corpora):
   numeral placement, 11/600 statistics saying one thing in plain words: Indus writes the number BEFORE the thing it
   goes with, and numerals open texts far more than they close them (927 initial vs 313 final of 4,350; without W1/W2
   846 vs 219). Every accounting corpus does the reverse (PE 168 initial vs 5,570 final; LA 8 vs 1,356; LB 127 vs
   3,526). Ur III, Linear Elamite and Voynich have no numerals. Holds on seals alone and non-seals alone, at MD+H and
   at the other sites. Consistent with S23/S70/S197-S217 (numeral precedes W700/W520) and S68 (counted items late).
3. Inventory fact (not sequence): Indus sign frequencies are flatter (evenness 0.80) than PE/LA/LB/Ur III legends
   (0.73-0.77) but less flat than Ur III names (0.85) and Linear Elamite (0.88); on the inventory-richness axis Indus
   sits with Linear B syllables and Linear Elamite -- a syllabary-sized sign list in use, between word lists and alphabets.
4. Heterogeneity warning: seals vs non-seals differ on 131/600 statistics, MD+H vs other sites on 87/600, random
   halves on 2/600. Pooled 'Indus' comparisons are comparisons of a mixture; S-DARK-7 reports only what holds in
   each part.
5. Voynich as unknown: words sit with Linear Elamite (1.08) and Ur III legend words (1.12), far from Indus (3.50) and
   PE (3.58); chars with Ur III names/syllables (1.85-1.95). It behaves like running text with an open word stock,
   shares nothing with the Indus residue, and is Indus-unlike on every order-dependent axis (median |z| 9.2).

What the statistics say the Indus texts are: short strings of a syllabary-sized sign inventory, sequence-wise most
like Linear B lines and Ur III seal legends (name + title strings), with prefixed numerals that open rather than
close the text; not Proto-Elamite- or Linear A-type accounting entries. Nothing here is a reading of any sign; no
'cracked'. Negative result for the 'Indus = non-linguistic accounting notation' family at this level; mild support
for the seal-legend reading already in GRAMMAR.md (frame + name-like middle), with the numeral-first order as the one
hard, replicated, Indus-specific fact to add (candidate for ANCHORS/GRAMMAR: 'number precedes item; texts do not end
in a number').

Would kill the residue: an Indus reading-order reversal for the tall-stroke tablets (checked: canonical seq is
reading order for 4,601/5,369 texts; S59). Would support it further: the same prefixed-numeral order on post-1977
finds with transcriptions (data/derived/new-texts-outside-corpus.json has none yet).
