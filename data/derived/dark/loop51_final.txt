1. Do the foreign texts trace to specific home cities?  NO.
   31 distinct foreign texts (>= 2 signs; 17 round Gulf-type seals, 7 square, 1 cylinder, 4 pots, 1 sealing, 1 tablet).
   Per-site boundary bigram likelihood ratio (openers, closers, frozen pairs and stock texts together) has real power
   on home texts (LOO 0.42 vs 0.22 chance; 0.27 vs 0.18 with stock copies removed) but assigns the foreign texts
   MORE diffusely than length-matched home texts (top-site share 0.19 vs 0.29 [0.19-0.42]; all three levels, W and M).
   Only robust per-site effect: Mohenjo-daro deficit (1-2 of 31 vs 5-6 expected, P_lo 0.03-0.05). No port signal
   (Lothal 1-2 vs 3.5-4.9). Kalibangan's excess (6-8) is the S149 twins tablets themselves: gone when stock copies
   are collapsed in W space (5 vs 4.9), marginal in M space (8 vs 4.7, P 0.08, depends on proposed bridge entries).
   Foreign texts are assigned with smaller margins (1.02 vs 1.45, P 0.05): unlike every city rather than like one.
   Exact copies at home: 0 of 31 in W space; 3 in M space, all 2-sign texts at the home 2-sign cross-site twin rate (47%).

2. Does S149 survive?  NOT as a text identity; only as a formula.
   Kalibangan 91-56-2-417-890-892 vs Janabiyah 71-31-831-55-121-99-55: edit 7/7, zero shared signs or bigrams in
   Wells and in M space; W71 is not the twins sign. With composites split (56 = 55 55) the two share two 12-grids,
   which 21-23% of unrelated cross-site pairs of these lengths also do. No text anywhere repeats any run of K; the
   W56 link is H-14 and L-115, unrelated to J. The surviving fact is a Gulf formula: person + 12/24-grid in 3/31
   foreign vs 4/3,535 home texts (Fisher 2e-5), K being one of the four home instances (S276's reading confirmed).

3. Is there a reduced export register?  A REGISTER YES, REDUCED NO.
   Round seals abroad vs shape- and length-matched home round seals: frame identical (0.17 vs 0.18; the frame loss
   is the round-seal format, S46), person sign 0.72 vs 0.12 and person-first 0.39 vs 0.00 (P 0.001, every level).
   Inventory: 45 distinct signs vs 37-39 expected from home round (P_hi 0.001) and 50 from home square (P 0.08);
   rare-sign share 0.16 vs 0.08 (P 0.006-0.013), top-50 share lower; concentration at chance. Square seals abroad:
   no significant difference from home square seals (frame 2/7, attested pairs 0.71 vs 0.68). Unknown-provenance
   round seals carry the same register. Not a pidgin: more varied, rarer signs around a person + number core.

4. IM77 West Asian finds (held-out reading): 17 texts, 4 strictly linked to Wells, ~8-11 genuinely new. Same result:
   MD 1 of 17; 8 go to the 34-text 'other sites' pool because they are negative at every named city; register
   replicated (opener 0/16, attested pairs 0.42 vs 0.76, rare 0.13 vs 0.04, distinct 56 vs 48, person 0.25 vs 0.05);
   Wells/IM77 assignment agreement 2 of 4 = chance. Upper bounds: opener-initial < 3/12 (IM77-only), < 3/31 (Wells).

Plain statement: the Indus-script objects found abroad do not point to a home city. The round (Gulf-type) seals
were written in a distinct formula (person + strokes + grid) that is a property of the seal format wherever it
is found, using the full Indus sign inventory with a bias to rarer signs; the square seals abroad are ordinary
Indus seals. S149's Kalibangan-Bahrain 'twin' is not a shared text in either transcription. Nothing here is a
reading; nothing is 'cracked'. Caveats: 31 + ~10 texts; 76% of the home comparison is Mohenjo-daro + Harappa;
M-space results depend on S-DARK-27 proposals; the canonical corpus is the older build (S-DARK-23).
