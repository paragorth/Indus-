LOOP 22 FINAL: FIXED-WIDTH FIELDS. Three cycles, all three merge levels (seq_raw / seq_strong / seq_all), held-out sites, Ur III and
Linear B controls. Engine tools/dark_loop22.py; per-level outputs loop22_cycle{1,2,3}_<level>.txt; summaries loop22_cycle{1,2,3}.txt.
Data: 3,155 complete, direction-recorded texts of >= 2 signs (87% Mohenjo-daro + Harappa; dir '-' texts dropped per S-DARK-8.4).

QUESTION. A designed record format puts its fields at fixed offsets from one edge, keeps field widths fixed, and fills an empty field
with a placeholder. A language does none of these. Which does the Indus text do?

ANSWER. None of the three record-format signatures is present; the known two-edged frame is all there is.
 (a) Anchoring (S-DARK-22.1). 78 signs with >= 30 tokens: 43 tighter from the end, 34 from the start (P = 0.18). Each closed class is
     anchored at its own edge: openers/connectives at the start (d +1.0 to +1.7 bits), closers/suffix/pre-closer titles at the end
     (-1.0 to -2.3); the NAME residue has no preference (18/42, mean d +0.04). Only four signs have under 1 bit of positional entropy
     from an edge (W752, W400, W527, W151, all end-grammar). End/start gain ratio 1.7 (seals 1.7, tablets 1.6, held-out 1.3) vs Ur III
     0.5 (name-first legends) and Linear B 4.8 (ideogram + number records). Indus sits between the two, closer to Linear B but weaker.
 (b) Offset grammar (S-DARK-22.2). Held-out (fit MD+HP, test other sites) nats/token: Markov-1 4.51, Markov-2 4.65, slot grammar 4.78,
     offset-from-end 5.10, offset-from-start 5.07. The offset model loses to the chain by +0.45 [+0.35, +0.55] and to the slot grammar
     by +0.33; end and start offsets tie. Adding 'remaining length' to the chain or slot model makes them worse. Controls: offset model
     loses by +1.79 (Ur III) and +0.86 (Linear B). Relative cost offset/chain: Indus 1.10, Linear B 1.18, Ur III 1.71. The Indus is the
     most positional of the three because its edge signs are frequent, but it never crosses into fixed-offset territory. Limit: no known
     administrative script has token-level fixed widths, so this test can only reject a strict code, which it does.
 (c) Field widths (S-DARK-22.3). TITLE 0/1/2+ = 0.36/0.54/0.10; COUNT 0/1/2 = 0.80/0.07/0.11; NAME 0-10 signs with a smooth geometric
     fall-off; 198 distinct width signatures vs 168 for the corpus's own class-bigram null (more, not fewer). The one excess over the
     null, 'exactly one title sign', is the closer-specific left partner already in GRAMMAR.md (S229, S289).
 (d) Placeholders (S-DARK-22.3). 374 sign x field tests, max-statistic nulls stratified by site, type and length. Fit-set hits (W320,
     W388 when the title is empty; W34 when name/count are empty; W700 when the suffix is empty) are parser tautologies or the known
     Harappa voucher '3 + W700' which excludes the tablet suffix, and none replicates at the held-out sites (0:0, 4:1, 0:0, 3:0). Empty
     fields are skipped: the commonest signatures are the ones with zeros.

WHAT THIS ADDS TO GRAMMAR.md. Nothing new in structure; a calibration. The frame is two-edged (openers from the start, closers from
the end) and everything between the edges is a variable-length string whose signs are predicted by their neighbours, not by their
distance from an edge. This is the profile of a legend formula with a free name field (Ur III-like in design, with the name in the
middle instead of first), not of a fixed-width code or form. If someone proposes a record-format reading (fields = commodity, quantity,
office at fixed offsets), these three results are the controls it must beat: the offset model must out-predict the chain held out,
widths must be fixed, and a filler sign must appear where a field is empty. None holds now.

ARROWS AND CORRECTIONS. Cycle 1: 2 x 78 per-sign tests, Bonferroni. Cycle 2: 5 pre-declared paired differences per corpus. Cycle 3:
11 null comparisons + 374 sign x field tests under per-field max-statistic nulls and a 6-field Bonferroni; held-out replication required
for any hit. Independence caveat (S312): the held-out sites are 404 texts; 'does not replicate' there is an upper bound (< 3/113 for
W320 and W34), not proof of absence. Nothing depends on a merge level except the W320/W388 pair, which appears only under the strong
and probable merges and fails held-out anyway.
