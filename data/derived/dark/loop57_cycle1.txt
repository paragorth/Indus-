Transcription rule: every mark matched by eye against the full Wells inventory rendered from tools/indus_font.ttf (4 sheets, 712 signs); A = identical shape, no rival (X = W645, arrow = W515); B = same family, head number given (strokes -> tall numerals W31-W36, V -> W700, arcs -> W899/904/908, bowtie -> W540, V-with-stroke -> W706); C = loose (waves -> W450/451, lens/circle -> W790, spirals, hashes). No mirrored blind pass: the sources are schematic drawings/typology tables, not photographs, and every matched sign except the arrow and hooks is mirror-symmetric. Non-Wells shapes (horizontal bars, plain T, arches, plus, sun) are kept as rows with a shape description: 100 of 217 rows (355 of 985 marks) have no Wells match.

Reading: the pre-seal pottery marks contain the COUNT layer (stroke numerals, 47% of early tokens) and a dozen simple middle-slot signs (X, V-cup, arcs, bowtie, chevron, tree, comb), and NOT the frame: no opener, no W60 marker, no jar, no arrow, no suffix, no closer-paradigm sign at Mehrgarh, Sarai Khola or Harappa Ravi/Kot Diji (0 of 25 founder types, upper bound 0.12; vs 0.18-0.22 expected even after matching stroke complexity). Same at all three merge levels | **Arrow killed in its strong form.** The closers and markers of the seal code are NOT the old pottery marks: the frame signs are missing from a millennium of potters' marks (3500-2600 BC, three regions) at a rate that survives a complexity-matched control, while the stroke numerals are over-represented (replicating S-DARK-43.4 outside Harappa: Mehrgarh and Sarai Khola count with the same tall strokes). What the seals inherit is the count layer and about fifteen simple geometric middles; the openers, connective, jar, arrow and closer paradigm are the new layer. Caveats: identity of a stroke pair or an X across 1,000 years is shape identity, not demonstrated sign identity; Mehrgarh (857 of 985) is a Baluchistan workshop tradition, not the Indus plain; the transcription is single-pass from drawings (no photo, no mirrored pass possible).
