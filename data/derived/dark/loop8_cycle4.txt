S-DARK-8.4  Per-text direction flip, by recorded direction, object type, site and length (tools/dark8_flip.py; log loop8_cycle4_log.txt)

Why. The permutation search (8.1) is direction-blind (Markov-2 is time-symmetric). But a model trained on texts in one reading order is asymmetric (it knows openers come first and closers last), so for any single text nll(stored) - nll(reversed) says which way it reads better. Model: trigram on 2,321 Mohenjo-daro + Harappa texts recorded R/L (>= 3 signs, no damage), 5-fold so MD+HP texts are scored out of fold; other sites scored by the full model. 'Reverse-better' = the reversed text has lower nll. Baseline = the R/L texts themselves. seq_raw / seq_strong / seq_all agree to within 1-2 texts in every cell; seq_raw numbers quoted.

Results.
Baseline R/L: held-out sites n = 425, reverse-better 10%, mean difference -2.50 nats/token (CI -2.68..-2.30); MD+HP out of fold n = 2,321, 5%, -3.53. So an R/L text almost always reads better as stored, and 5-10% is the noise rate for 'reverse-better'.
dir = L/R (n = 115, 87 of them Harappa tablets): reverse-better 11%, mean -3.03 (CI -3.44..-2.59) = indistinguishable from R/L. Confirms S312(3): L/R texts are stored in reading order; the Wells convention already accounts for them. L/R seals (18): 11%, -2.04. L/R pottery (6): 50% - too few.
dir = T/B (8): 0% reverse-better, -2.45. dir = BUS (10, Wells' own boustrophedon flag): 10%, -1.36 (CI -2.22..-0.58): stored order still preferred, i.e. the recorded line-reversal has already been undone in the stored string.
dir = '-' (direction not recorded; n = 92: 30 seals, 25 pots, 18 tablets, 9 tags): reverse-better 30%, mean -0.77 (CI -1.23..-0.32). This is the one class that differs from the baseline (30% vs 5-10%; Fisher vs held-out R/L: 28/92 vs 42/425, two-sided P = 2e-6). Mixture estimate: if a fraction f of these texts is stored backwards and each class has the baseline error rate 0.10, then 0.30 = 0.10 + 0.80 f gives f = 0.25, i.e. roughly 20-25 of the 92 unrecorded-direction texts are probably stored in the wrong direction. By type: seals 27%, pots 40%, tablets 17%, tags 22%; by site: Mohenjo-daro 35% (26), Harappa 26% (27), Lothal 23% (13).
dir = NR (5) and SYM (4): differences about 0, uninformative (symmetric or single-sign-like texts).
R/L by type: tablets 2% reverse-better (-4.43), seals 6% (-2.90), tags 8%, pots 21% (-1.71, n = 77), misc 23%. Pottery graffiti are the least directional class even when a direction was recorded.
R/L by site: Mohenjo-daro 5%, Harappa 4%, Lothal 7%, Kalibangan 8%, Chanhu-daro 10%, Dholavira 16% (n = 94, mean -2.10). Dholavira's higher rate goes with its distinct bar-seal formula (GRAMMAR.md, S157/S184: opener kept, jar dropped), not with a different writing direction: its mean is still firmly negative.
R/L by length 3-8: reverse-better 11-12% at L = 3-5 on held-out sites, 0-6% at L = 6-8; mean -2.3 to -2.9 at every length, including L = 6 and 7. This settles the L = 6-7 anomaly of 8.1 (the whole-corpus reverse template 'beating' identity by 0.29 there): per text, 94-100% of 6- and 7-sign texts read better as stored. The 8.1 effect was a smoothing artefact of fitting a 720-template search on 273 training texts.
Texts most strongly preferred backwards (candidates for a stored-direction error; W numbers, stored order): C-88 and C-35 (Chanhu-daro tablets, dir '-') 167-705-31 (d = +5.6; reversed starts with W31 = M86, the middle-initial marker); H-2085 (L/R tablet) 824-892-413 (+4.8); M-2062 (R/L pot) 46-31-32-741 (+4.3; reversed = M343-M87-M86-...); H-170 (dir '-' tablet) 827-336-630-511 (+3.8); H-340, H-536, Ns-63, M-1336, H-1663, M-3, L-218, L-232. These are per-text model preferences, not readings; photo check would decide.
