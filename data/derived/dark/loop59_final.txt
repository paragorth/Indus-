LOOP 59 FINAL: HOW MUCH OF THE SCRIPT IS EXPLAINED? (3 cycles; tools/dark_loop59_common.py, dark_loop59_c1.py, dark_loop59_c2.py, dark_loop59_c3.py,
dark_loop59_rows.py; rows to paste: loop59_cycle1.txt (S-DARK-59.1), loop59_cycle2.txt (59.2), loop59_cycle3.txt (59.3); logs loop59_c1_<level>[_rows].txt/.json,
loop59_c2_<level>.txt/.json, loop59_c3.txt/.json). Corpus: merged-corpus-canonical.json (S-DARK-23 caution), complete direction-recorded texts, one per die,
fit = Mohenjo-daro + Harappa (~2,000 texts), scored on 424 held-out-site texts and the 324 IM77-only texts; all three merge levels (every figure within
0.1 bit / 2 points across levels), seq_all also with duplicates kept. Nothing committed.

METHOD. Every model is a predictive distribution over the next sign (or END) given the text so far, the object class and the site, scored in bits.
Ladder: uniform, unigram, Kneser-Ney Markov-1/2/3, the STRUCTURAL model (frame-slot automaton with a pan-Indus shared middle pool + 139-149 rule
pairs + adaptive site and global whole-text caches + unigram, EM-interpolated per zone), STRUCTURAL + KN2/KN3 (best), the S366 generator (Monte Carlo),
the loop 33 automaton. The loop 39 form generator is not a sequential predictor and was not scored.

THE NUMBER. A held-out Indus text costs 6.2-6.5 bits per sign under its own sign frequencies (unigram) and 4.9-5.2 bits under the best model we can
build: 17% (new sites) to 24% (new IM77 texts) of the unigram entropy is explained, 43-47% of the uniform-inventory entropy. Per seal: 33 bits ->
27 bits. By slot (gap closed vs unigram): marker 89%, suffix 57%, END 38-46%, opener 34% (what is unpredictable is whether the seal opens at all),
closer 32%, qualifiers 26%, counts 7-12%, middle 8% (19% once a site's stock texts are cached). So: THE FRAME IS ~40% PREDICTABLE FROM ITS OWN
FREQUENCIES (75% from a uniform inventory) AND THE MIDDLE 8-19% (18-27%).

WHAT EXPLAINS IT. The hand grammar (frame + frozen pairs + whole-text reuse) predicts exactly as well as a second-order Markov chain (5.44 vs 5.37
bits, CIs overlapping fully) and not better; combining them gains 0.2 bits. The S366 generator and the loop 33 automaton, which pass battery and
acceptance tests, are near-unigram predictors (6.14, 6.16 vs 6.23): statistical adequacy on 50 summary statistics is not predictive adequacy.
Where KN2 beats the grammar (0.5-1.6 bits) it is on the frozen pairs and phrase templates already in GRAMMAR.md (705->33, 590->tree, 255->435,
760->jar, 32->fish, 220->arrow, 550->60/527, 142->617, tree->END); where the grammar beats KN2 it is the END/suffix decision after the jar and the
closer after a qualifier, the frame's long-range part. No new rule is hiding in the residual.

THE IRREDUCIBLE PART. Of the 27 bits a held-out seal costs under the best model, 18.7 (69%) sit in its 2-3 middle signs at 7.1-7.5 bits each
(85-90% of their unigram entropy), 4.3 in the frame, 1.5 in the qualifiers, 2.7 in the counts; tablets 21 bits with the middle at 60%. 16% of all
tokens are at unigram level under every model (middle 20%, closer 17%, counts 13%, qualifiers 10%, opener/marker/suffix 0%), spread across the
middle rather than concentrated in one construction; the irreducible signs are not the rare ones. 16-19 bits per seal is 10^5 equiprobable
alternatives, the S-DARK-17.5 credential-stock size and the S-DARK-53 Chao1 floor.

COMPARISON. Through the identical code, Ur III seal legends (fit Umma, score other sites) give: titles 26% closed (words) / 48% (syllables), names
-18% (words; new sites bring new names) / 34% (syllables); names carry 65-71% of the remaining bits. Linear B lines: formulae 19%, sign-groups
-29%, words 50% of the bits. Indus: frame 41%, middle 8-19%, middle 67-69% of the bits. The Indus seal's information budget is distributed as a
name + title legend's, and the within-slot predictability of its unique part is at the level of names, between Sumerian name words (8.7 bits)
and name syllables (4.9 bits) per token.

PLAIN STATEMENT (all three merge levels). About a fifth of the information in an average seal text, measured against the script's own sign
frequencies, is rule-governed (frame order, markers, closers, frozen pairs, stock texts); against a flat inventory, under half. About two thirds of
the bits a seal carries (16-19 of 25-27) are irreducible identifier content in the middle, which no model, hand-written or statistical, predicts
beyond its sign frequencies and a few frozen pairs. That split (frame ~40% predictable, identifier ~10%, identifier = 2/3 of the bits) is the
split of an Ur III name + title legend. Nothing here is a reading; it fixes the size of what a reading would have to supply: ~16 bits per seal,
all in the middle.

CAVEATS. Held-out sites are new material relative to the fit but IM77 is 70% the same objects (S312); the IM77 middle gain (19%) is the site cache
finding Mohenjo-daro/Harappa stock texts among the 'new' IM77 texts. Oracle taken as 0 bits; a better model than ours would move the explained
share up, but KN3 and the combination already saturate (KN3 < KN2 on held-out). The unigram itself is structure (Zipfian frequencies, S356);
numbers against a uniform inventory are given beside it. Ur III title/name labels are a fixed word list, not a parse.
