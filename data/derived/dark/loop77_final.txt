STATED PLAINLY
No Indus document type behaves like a Proto-Elamite list of related persons. Put through the identical statistic, the PE tablet gives 3x element sharing among its entries. Indus faces of one object,
tablets found together, seals from one room and seals stamped on one sealing give chance-level designation sharing, the Linear B 'unrelated individuals' value. They share only (i) the formula: jar
head, titles, fish-phrase signs; and (ii) the same designation repeated, as a copy, an abbreviation or a stock series. So the Indus groups look like several records about ONE designation
(multi-sided object, tablet series) or like independent office-holders under a shared title (room, Lothal team), not like a household or work-team list. With S-DARK-64 (no lineage element) and
S-DARK-73 (the Indus middle fits a PE-type designation slot on frequency statistics), this narrows the reading: if the middles are person or unit designations, they are individual ones, written
one per document, never listed together with relatives or teammates in a way the script would mark.
Limits: Mohenjo-daro + Harappa carry almost all groups (Lothal only for c); 'never' = upper bounds (Harappa same-spot tablets 0/44 < 7%; Lothal 0/19 < 16%); the corpus is a small fraction of what
was written (S309), so an Indus list genre (e.g. perishable ledgers) may exist unseen. PE's 'related persons' reading is others' interpretation, used only as the hypothesis being calibrated.
Would revive a PE-like list in Indus: a newly found multi-text Indus tablet or prism whose different designations share a rare element at >= 2x the permutation null.
