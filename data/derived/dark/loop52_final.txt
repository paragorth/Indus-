## Best estimate
About 670 distinct signs in use after every defensible merge (713 Wells numbers minus 44 variant forms), out of a total of roughly 900 (Chao1 913, 95% CI 830-997; after the calibrated bias correction 650-800 is the conservative band, S309's ~950 the upper one). The unseen quarter is rare signs, not variants: merging removes frequent forms and leaves the singleton count at 219-234 untouched, and the new-material rate (1.4% of tokens in a random IM77 split, 4.5% on objects Wells never matched) does not fall under any merge level.

## What that count rules out
- A syllabary (60-120 signs): no evidence rule reaches it. Even the indefensible maximal merge leaves 288 seen / 450 total and destroys the frame, the closer paradigm and the partial order.
- A small closed code: the spectrum (f1/S 0.33-0.37, GT unseen 1.9%) is that of an open inventory, unlike khipu (0.06 / 0.1%) or ECO (0.27 / 0.4%).
- 'Hundreds of Wells numbers are allographs': the principled graph finds 44 (6%), re-finding 24 of S268's 27 merges and adding 22 pairs (697/700, 840/844, 900/906, 61/63, 595/597, 552/555, 226/232/241, 32/315, 176/388, 70/72, 97/142, 460/463, 467/468, 904/927, 151/154/156, 803/806/812/831/832/838).
Consistent with: a logographic or logosyllabic inventory of Proto-Elamite / early cuneiform size (600-900 base signs), or Linear B's mixed list counted with its ideograms.

## Method lessons
1. 'Variant predicted by the neighbour' (S-DARK-46) is not an allograph detector: 57% of all sign pairs pass it, because two different signs in one slot also have different neighbours. It confirms a shape or reader link; it cannot create one.
2. Two distributional witnesses (contextual + free interchange) agree below chance (0.7x) and, where they do agree, merge paradigm-mates (titles before the jar, closers after 33/806).
3. Shape (fine >= 0.85) and reader (same Mahadevan sign) agree 36x above chance: those are the independent witnesses.
4. Naive text-bootstrap CIs for Chao1 are biased low; use the analytic variance.
5. Loop 21's 'certainly new' IM77 texts cannot test unseen signs (texts with unbridged signs are sent to 'indeterminate').

## Outside tests
- Mahadevan lumps (58 M signs <- 141 Wells signs): the graph without any reader input puts 5 (principled) to 13 (any-2) of 58 inside one class vs 0.1-0.5 by chance (P 0.001); 22 lumps are untestable (< 8 tokens), the rest are the medium-split tree/opener/jar pairs that fail the strict usage cuts.
- New material: on the 1,561 IM77 objects Wells never matched, 265 of 5,846 tokens (4.5%) are M signs unseen on the shared objects, vs 80 +/- 10 (1.4%) for a random split (z +18); selection and bridge gaps explain part of the excess, but both numbers exceed S309's 1.7% token-level Good-Turing figure for the next-sign-is-new rate.

Verdict words: not 'cracked'; nothing here reads a sign. Supported: the Indus inventory is logographic-sized (~670 in use, ~900 total) and open; a syllabary is excluded at every merge level.
