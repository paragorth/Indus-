READING
  1. An end-anchored offset model does not beat the order-2 chain, nor the slot grammar, in any Indus subset or at any merge level.
     The chain wins by 0.45 nats/token held out (about 1.6x in probability per sign). So a fixed-width record format, in which the
     best predictor of a sign is its distance from the edge, is rejected as the main organising principle.
  2. The end and start offsets predict equally well (tie, CI straddles 0), consistent with cycle 1: there is no single anchoring edge.
  3. Calibration from the controls is informative but limiting. Even Linear B lines, a genuine field record, are predicted 0.86 nats
     better by the chain than by the offset model, because the fields are of variable width (the word field varies, and many lines
     carry several ideogram+number pairs). The offset model only wins over a chain when field widths are literally fixed, which no
     known administrative script of this period does at the token level. What the test CAN say is where Indus sits: the offset model's
     relative cost is 1.10 for Indus, 1.18 for Linear B, 1.71 for Ur III. Indus is the most 'positional' of the three (its closed-class
     edge signs carry a large share of tokens), but still on the Linear B side, not beyond it.
  4. The order-1 chain beats the order-2 chain held out (4.51 vs 4.65) in Indus but not in Ur III: Indus texts are short and the site
     split starves trigrams; the sequence information is mostly local (adjacent-pair) plus the two edges.

  level except the END-vs-START ties, which are the null result).
