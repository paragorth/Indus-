S-DARK-60 FINAL: a functional profile for every head (closer-slot sign) -- loop complete (4 cycles)
Cycles: 60.1 object type, carriers, impression match (loop60_cycle1.txt); 60.2 companions + IM77 (loop60_cycle2.txt); 60.3 geography, area, phase, emblem, material, size (loop60_cycle3.txt); 60.4 pairwise distinctness, Wells x 3 levels + IM77 (loop60_cycle4.txt).
Tables: loop60_head_profiles.csv (one row per head per merge level), loop60_pairs.csv (every pairwise test), loop60_head_readings.csv (the readings below).
Scripts: tools/dark_loop60.py (cycles 1-3), tools/dark_loop60_pairs.py (cycle 4).
Data caution: the canonical corpus was built from the older inscriptions.csv (5,369 rows; S-DARK-23); all counts are from that build. 76% of texts are Mohenjo-daro + Harappa. Wells and IM77 are ~70% the same objects: agreement is transcription-robust, not replicated. All 'never' counts are upper bounds (< 3/n).

HOW MANY FUNCTIONALLY DISTINCT HEADS?
- Grammar alone (what the text around the head does): FOUR, at seq_raw, seq_strong, seq_all and in IM77 alike, among heads with >= 40 texts:
  (1) the open seal ending: jar W740 = W151 = box527 (W615/617 joins in Wells);
  (2) the arrow W520 fish-phrase head;
  (3) the short fixed-epithet formula: W156 = W154/158 (Mahadevan already writes both as M15);
  (4) the tablet count unit W700.
  'No head' is the jar's register with the head left off (stat-only difference, excess +0.10-0.16) plus the count lists.
- With object type and site added: 5-6 (Wells) / 5 (IM77) of the >= 40-text heads, because media and cities split (3) and (1).
- Counting the small heads (10-20 texts): 5 in grammar, up to 7 in the full profile; the extra member that holds up is W236. W226 cannot be told from the formula group (3); W595 and W90/91 cannot be told from anything (underpowered, not shown equal).
- Controls: split-half pseudo-pairs never pass; the allographs W154~W158 and W526~W527 come out indistinct in grammar, as they should.
So the ~10-sign closer paradigm (S289) carries about four functions; the rest are the same function carved on another medium or used in another city.

READINGS PER HEAD (function in plain words; grade; would support / would kill). Grades: A established, B likely, C leeway guess.
1. Jar W740 (M342) -- "the ordinary closing word of a seal that is actually used to stamp goods; takes any qualifier." A (function). Basis: 163 qualifiers (H 6.0 bits), only head followed by W90/91, impressed above chance (7.2% vs 5.8%), its seal texts recur on tablets 23 vs 13, every container type alike; W151 and box527 not separable in grammar.
   Would support: new sealing batches keep the jar as the commonest head on impressions (>= 40% of headed sealing texts). Would kill: a new excavated sealing batch (>= 50 texts) where jar texts are below chance on impressions, or a qualifier set that splits into closed sub-registers.
2. Arrow W520 (M211) -- "closing word of a fish title: 'fish word (+ three) + arrow'; a seal that was used to stamp goods." B. Basis: fish word 73% (IM77 76%), T3 before it 20% vs 6%, never a suffix, impressed 12% vs 6% (p<0.01); distinct from the jar in both corpora (excess +0.52).
   Would support: >= 50% of new arrow texts carry a fish word and none takes W90/91. Would kill: a new batch of >= 30 arrow texts with fish <= 35% (the corpus rate).
3. Box W527/W526 (M254) -- "a variant of the ordinary seal ending used with the pincer or fish title, never with a numeral; Lothal-leaning." C. Basis: grammar not separable from the jar (IM77 p 0.08) or W151 (IM77 p 0.11), only statistically from the arrow; fish 54%, no numeral before it 56/56 and 61/61.
   Would support: new box527 texts keep pincer/fish qualifiers and no numeral. Would kill: >= 3 new box527 texts with a numeral directly before it.
4. W151 (M12) -- "the same ending as the jar, kept for seals that are not found stamped (personal or display seals?)." B for 'same function as the jar', C for the 'not stamped' gloss. Basis: grammar = jar in all runs (p 0.26; IM77 0.08); 77% seals, 0 of 46 seal texts found impressed (< 7%), 0 of 165 sealings, concentrated in DK-G South.
   Would support: still no W151 impression after the next ~200 sealings. Would kill: >= 3 W151 texts on new sealings, or a qualifier-set split from the jar in held-out sites.
5. W156 (M15) -- "short formula ending 'three + W156' with a one-word middle, written on incised tablets and small steatite seals." B. Basis: S3 before it 43% (36 vs 3.9), one-sign middles 66%, incised 84% of its tablets, steatite 88%, seals 22 mm (p<0.01); grammar = W154/158.
   Would support: new incised tablets keep 'S3 + W156'. Would kill: W156 on a moulded tablet with the leaf-tree qualifier (that would erase the medium split from 6).
6. W154/W158 (M15) -- "the same short formula ending as W156, with the leaf-tree qualifier W806, on moulded tablets and bar seals (a Harappa form)." B. Basis: grammar = W156 (p 0.33); 806 x27 before it; moulded 81% of tablets, bar seals 19 of 29 (vs 5.5), Harappa 44 vs 32; W400 after it 34%.
   Would support: new Harappa moulded tablets with 806-154/158. Would kill: a set of incised W154/158 tablets taking the S3 epithet.
7. W226 (M60) -- "a short fixed title 'tall two + four-stroke fish' closing Mohenjo-daro steatite seals; grammatically a member of the short-formula group." C (n 20). Basis: T2 before it 35%, empty middles, steatite 15/16, Harappa 4 vs 8.1; not separable from W156/W154-158 or W236 in Wells.
   Would support: new W226 seals keep T2 before it. Would kill: W226 seals with long middles (>= 3 signs) in >= 5 of the next 10.
8. W236 (M66, proposed bridge) -- "a short phrase ending 'opener + connective + W236' (+ W400), with no middle at all: a fixed formula, not a personal seal." C (n 14). Basis: connective 50%, 5 of 14 with empty middle, W400 after it 43%, never a numeral; distinct in grammar from every head except W226.
   Would support: new W236 texts of <= 3 signs with the connective. Would kill: W236 after a free middle of >= 2 signs in >= 3 new texts.
9. W615/617 (M245, proposed bridge) -- "the man-with-staff phrase ending (142 + 617), the copper-tablet and bar-seal form of the ordinary ending, Mohenjo-daro." C. Basis: Wells grammar = jar/W151 (not separable at any level); 12 copper tablets vs 3.9, bar seals, Mohenjo-daro 32 vs 18.8; its IM77 separation is the doubled M245 (segmentation, S-DARK-24).
   Would support: new copper tablets ending 142-615/617. Would kill: W615/617 with the T3 or S3 epithet.
10. W700 (M328) -- "the counted unit of the Harappa tablets: 'tall 2-4 + W700' = so many units; not a seal ending." A (function), word C. Basis: tall 2/3/4 before it 94%, tablets 96%, middle 1.2, distinct from every head in every run (excess >= 0.38), no opener, fish, suffix or connective, both moulded and incised.
    Would support: any new Harappa tablet ending in W700 has a tall numeral before it. Would kill: a set of W700 seals with free middles.
11. W595 (M252) -- "a rare seal closer that replaces the jar (pre-closer, S288); function unresolved." C (n 10). Basis: 91% seals, bar seals 4 vs 0.9, never on sealings; no pair test reaches significance.
    Would support: new W595 seals without the jar. Would kill: W595 + jar in the same text in >= 2 new finds.
12. Marked jar W741/742/745 -- "not a head: the jar as a member inside a compound (96% of tokens medial)." B. Would kill: >= 10 new texts ending in a marked jar.
13. W400-only endings -- "a headed text with the head left off, closed only by the receipt suffix; a tablet word." B. Basis: grammar = jar / W151 / W154-158 in both corpora; faience, never copper (0 vs 3.8), DK-G North; W400 after heads on tablets 21-43%.
    Would support: new W400-only tablets match headed tablet texts minus the head. Would kill: a W400-only text type with its own qualifier set.
14. W90/W91 endings -- "person suffix after the jar only (97 vs 72; 0 after other heads)." Function B (as a jar-only suffix), meaning C; as a stand-alone ending underpowered (11 texts).
    Would kill: W90/91 after the arrow or W700 in >= 3 new texts.
15. No head -- "the list and count register: long middles, numerals and quantity phrases, second units; pots, bangles, copper tablets, and the foreign seals." B. Basis: middle 3.64 vs 3.10, counts 48%, quantity phrases 15% vs 9%, Kalibangan, Chanhu-daro and foreign over-represented; differs from the jar only statistically in grammar.
    Would support: new Gulf seals keep 'no head' above 50%. Would kill: a held-out site where no-head texts are short and numeral-free.

PREDICTION (frozen, testable outside the corpus): on new excavated texts (post-1977 sites, S44, or the IM77-only 324 texts in a later build), a classifier trained on the grammar profile of this corpus will tell the jar from W151/box527 no better than the split-half bar, and will separate the arrow, the W156/W154-158 formula and W700 from the jar at >= 0.70 balanced accuracy. Killed if W151 or box527 separate from the jar at >= 0.70 on >= 30 new texts each.
