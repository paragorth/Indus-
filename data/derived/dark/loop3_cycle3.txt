S-DARK-3 cycle 3: whole-machine controls -- the same 16 transformations x 4 tests on (a) a bigram-generated Indus
corpus and (b) Ur III seal legends (owner names as syllables; whole legends as words)
Code: tools/strat_dark3_run.py control 20 7 (loop3_control_bigram.txt); tools/strat_dark3_run.py ur3 0 5 (loop3_ur3_control.txt).

(a) Bigram-generated Indus (data/codelib/indus_bigram.jsonl: bigram model trained on seq_raw, same text lengths,
5,369 texts). This corpus has no frame beyond what a first-order Markov chain carries and no exact recurring
formulae (namecalib base 1.04, repeat 0.83-0.90, slot -0.19). Question: how many arrows would the machine
'hit' (|z| >= 3) on a corpus we know has no hidden reading?
  Hits on the bigram corpus (|z| >= 3): reverse/namecalib -6.0; interleave/repeat +8.0; middleout/namecalib
  -19.7 and /slot +5.1; sort/namecalib -12.9 and /slot +5.4; family/repeat +3.4 and /slot +8.9; nonnum/slot
  -3.1; dropframe/slot -4.9; bpe/slot +11.8; splitnum/namecalib -4.4 and /repeat -3.0; rotate/namecalib -19.9
  and /slot +4.2; emblem/slot +15.8; objtype/slot +14.0; randfamily/slot +5.4; dropsign/namecalib +5.3.
  That is 19 of 68 arrows at |z| >= 3 on a corpus with nothing to find. The screening bar |z| >= 3 is therefore
  only a screening bar: the arrows that survive it on the real corpus but not here are the ones worth a look.
  Real-corpus survivors absent from this list: family/namecalib (-5.2; killed by randfamily in cycle 2),
  numonly/namecalib (+6.4 vs +2.6 here; died on held-out), dropframe/repeat (+4.7 vs +1.8 here; suggestive),
  bpe/repeat (+5.3 vs +1.1), bpe/arith (+4.4 vs -0.2; died on held-out), emblem/repeat (+4.1 vs +0.5),
  objtype/repeat (+5.6 vs +0.3), interleave/slot (-6.8 vs -2.1), dropsign/slot (-16.3 vs -0.6).
  Sign flips between real and bigram corpus (the informative ones): reverse/namecalib (+5.4 real, -6.0 bigram:
  real texts are end-anchored, the Markov corpus is start-anchored by construction); middleout/slot and
  rotate/slot (negative real, positive bigram: the real edges are low-entropy, the Markov corpus's are not).
  emblem/repeat and objtype/repeat: adding the emblem or object type as a sign lowers the real repetition ratio
  less than the shuffled null (z +4 to +6) but not on the bigram corpus -- i.e. texts on the same emblem/object
  type share signs more than shuffles do; this is S15/S28's small 'form-text' dependence, already known to be weak.

(b) Ur III control: do names stay name-like under the same transformations? (values only, no permutation null;
syllable tokens of the owner's name, 6,040 names; namecalib band for names < 0.7)
  identity 0.43 | reverse 0.42 | interleave 1.05 | middleout 0.32 | sort 0.36 | bpe 0.49 | rotate 0.39 |
  relabel 0.43 | dropsign (10-30%) 0.68.
  Names stay in the name band under every order-changing transformation except interleaving (which makes two
  pseudo-names out of one, 1.05, exactly as it does to Indus, 0.95) and heavy random deletion (0.68, edge of band).
  Whole legends as words: identity 1.13, everything 1.09-1.19 except interleave 1.43. So the name statistic is
  robust to misreading the ORDER of a name but not to splitting it in two. Consequence for Indus: if Indus texts
  were names misread in order (reversed, rotated, middle-out), the ratio would still sit near its true value;
  no order transformation of Indus reaches 0.43-0.62 (best 0.70 by middle-out, which lowers Ur III names by the
  same 0.11, so middle-out is a generic artefact). The 'names' hypothesis cannot be rescued by a reading-order
  error; it is not that we are reading names backwards.

Verdict: the machine fires 28% of its arrows at |z| >= 3 on a null corpus, so cycle-1 hits were expected to be
mostly noise; cycle 2 confirmed that. Ur III names keep their statistic under 7 of 8 order transformations,
Indus does not reach the name band under any of them.
Arrows fired: 68 (bigram control) + 24 (Ur III values, no null) -> running total 168.
