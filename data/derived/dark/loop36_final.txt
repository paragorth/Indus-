LOOP 36 FINAL (2026-10-03): a truly new test set, scored blind. Three cycles (loop36_cycle1-3.txt); rows below are
ready to paste before '## Summary' in STRATEGIES.md. Files: loop36_predictions.txt (frozen first), loop36_newfinds.csv
(25 candidate rows, every one with publication / figure / URL), loop36_overlap_check.txt, loop36_cycle3_raw.txt.
Scripts: tools/dark_loop36_match.py, tools/dark_loop36_score.py. Nothing committed; no licensed PDF copied into the repo
(Gadd 1932 and Shinde & Willis 2014 stay in the scratchpad).

Plain statement
1. How many genuinely new texts: 9 texts with >= 3 concrete signs that match nothing in Wells (5,680 rows, all sites) or
   IM77 (through the bridge + loop-27 proposals) by exact, reversed or edit-distance-1 comparison; 14 with >= 2 signs.
   Of these only 2 are excavated (Umma 2017 survey seal ?-803-?, 2 signs; Binjor 4MSR terracotta seal 90-32-93, 3 signs).
   Seven are museum pieces with open photographs and no provenance (Cleveland 1973.161, 1964.104, 1973.160; Met 49.40.1,
   49.40.3, 49.40.4; a Mohenjo-daro museum seal), five are the Shinde-Willis copper plates (private, authenticity untested,
   row order unknown). Two more texts are known to be new but could not be imaged (Gadd 1932 Ur nos. 3 and 15: the archive.org
   scan's plates are blank; BM and Commons image servers answered 429/403). Target of 40: not met, and not reachable from open
   sources: every post-2014 excavated inscription (Rakhigarhi cylinder seal, Binjor unicorn seal, Dholavira 2019-24, Khirsara,
   Kotada Bhadli, Lakhan-jo-daro, Chanhu-daro 2015-22) exists online only as a verbal description or a sub-100-px photo, and
   prior loops had already shown that ASI reviews to 2014, the Dholavira 2015 report and Laursen 2010 are inside the corpus.
2. From where: Cleveland Museum of Art open-access API (3 CC0 photographs, 3400 px), Met collection API (4), Sindh antiquities
   online exhibition (1), SAHI Tell Jokha 2017 report (1), ASI Binjor 2016 brochure (1), Ancient Asia 2014 PDF (5).
3. Predictions: 16 frozen. 0 pass at P < 0.05; 3 consistent but underpowered (jar/comb final 1/1 each; partial-order chain
   3:0 strict, 5:1 lenient, P = 0.15; unseen signs 0/18 coded, 0-11% with unmatched forms; new texts 9/9 vs S309's 95%);
   2 FAIL (P6 no-repetition: 2 of 4 strict texts repeat a sign, 7x bigram, P = 0.026, P = 0.002 vs home texts of the same
   lengths; P10 reuse/nesting: 0 of 12 texts carry a 3-sign run attested at home vs 5.8 expected, P = 0.002); 9 untestable
   (no opener-connective pair, no numeral + good, no two fish, no frozen pair, no seated person, no >= 6-sign text, no round
   seal abroad, no site split).
4. Reading: the failures sit entirely in unprovenanced museum pieces read from photographs by one reader with a measured
   25% per-sign error (blind control on Dholavira 2015 photos: 3 of 4 signs right). Misreading alone predicts the reuse
   failure; it does not easily explain the repetition failure, because the repeated signs (two tall strokes on Met 49.40.1;
   two hatched grids on Cleveland 1973.160) are the clearest signs on those seals. The corpus's own 24 'Unknown'-site (museum)
   texts already repeat signs at 12.5% vs 4.8% at home, so unprovenanced seals may be a different population (copies,
   forgeries, or a selection effect). No claim about GRAMMAR.md changes: its evidence is excavated; this set is not.
5. Scrupulousness on 'already in the corpus under another label': every candidate was matched against all 5,680 Wells rows
   regardless of site and against all IM77 texts via the bridge; the two previously logged new finds that ARE in the corpus
   (Salut jar = Mohenjo-daro M-1367; Laursen no. 10 = Janabiyah) were excluded; the Umma 2-sign seal and the Binjor unicorn
   seal are 'indeterminate' under the S-DARK-21.1 wildcard cap and were kept out of the strict set.

What a human can do in ten minutes to make this test real: (a) look up 'Cleveland' and 'New York' in the CISI 3.1
concordance; (b) download BM 122946 and W_1931-1010-15 photographs (Gadd 1932 nos. 3-4) and the Commons files named in

excavated or catalogued text with a known reading to calibrate against.
