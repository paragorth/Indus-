| S-DARK-80.2 | **Do the Meluhhan names look foreign? Sign-bigram language model of the Ur III name stock, calibrated on other foreigners.** `tools/dark_loop80_c2.py` (log `loop80_c2.json`). DOMESTIC = 10,344 distinct Ur III names from administrative slots (ki X-ta, kiszib3 X, giri3 X, dumu X, ugula X, X szu ba-ti) and @seal line 1, minus any name also found with a foreign label. Foreign groups from 'NAME (lu2) GN{ki}' and 'NAME mar-tu': AMORITE/WEST 279 (mar-tu, Mari, Ebla, Tuttul, Urshu), HURRIAN/NORTH 43 (Simurrum, Urbilum, Šimanum, Urkiš, Šašrum ...), IRAN/ELAM 38 (Anšan, Marhaši, Zidanum, Kimaš, Hurti ...). Bigram model with absolute discounting (d = 0.75), domestic names scored 10-fold out of sample; score = mean surprisal per transition. Calibration = AUC of each foreign group against held-out domestic names, label-permutation null 500x. Then the percentile of na-na-sa3 and sa6-ma-ar; length in signs; share of names whose first / last sign is one of the 10 commonest domestic initials (a ba e2 geme2 i lu2 lugal nin szu ur) / finals. Indus comparison: NAME-field length of distinct middles (S-DARK-56 parser) | **Calibration works for real foreign stocks: Hurrian AUC 0.74, Iran/Elam 0.66 (both P < 0.002); 'Amorite' does not (0.51, P = 0.22), because many men labelled mar-tu carry Sumerian or Akkadian names (Ur-Baba, Ur-Lamma, Puzur-Mama).** na-na-sa3 scores 7.15 bits (85th percentile of domestic names; 66th of Elamite, 67th of Hurrian), sa6-ma-ar 8.03 bits (93rd domestic; 79th Elamite, 84th Hurrian); the bigrams na-sa3 and sa6-ma never occur in 10,344 domestic names; Fisher combination of the two domestic tail probabilities P = 0.06. The wife a-li-a-hi scores 5.38 bits (51st percentile: an ordinary Akkadian name, as expected). Length: both names are 3 signs, the commonest domestic length (42%; domestic mean 3.31; Elamite 3.58; Hurrian 3.79). Edges: neither uses a Sumerian head or tail (domestic 41% / 34%; chance that both miss by luck 0.34 / 0.44). Indus NAME field: mean 3.13-3.16 signs, 2 signs 29%, 3 signs 23% (seq_raw/strong/all) | **The two Meluhhan names sit where Elamite and Hurrian names sit: foreign to the scribe's name stock, but only two strings, so the result is suggestive (P = 0.06), not a finding.** Sign length cannot discriminate: Ur III phonetic names, foreign names and Indus NAME fields all average about 3 signs, so the length of a heard Meluhhan name does not tell whether the Indus designation is phonetic or logographic. The ethnic label is a poor guide to language: 'Amorite' men often bear Sumerian names, and 13 of the ~16 Meluhha-linked persons do (S-DARK-80.1) |
