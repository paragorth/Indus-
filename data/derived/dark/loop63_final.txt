S-DARK-63 FINAL: ARE INDUS MIDDLES INDISTINGUISHABLE FROM LOGOGRAPHICALLY WRITTEN PERSONAL NAMES? (four cycles; rows in
loop63_cycle1-4.txt; scripts tools/dark_loop63_prep.py, dark_loop63_prep_ko.py, dark_loop63.py <1|2|3|4>; corpora
data/derived/dark/loop63_corpora/ with SOURCES.txt (jsonl gitignored, rebuildable from the URLs / Wikidata query there); numbers
loop63_cycle*.json, logs loop63_cycle*_log.txt). Nothing committed. Data: merged-corpus-canonical.json (S-DARK-23 caution: older
inscriptions.csv build), complete Wells texts only; IM77 via bridge_extended.json + S-DARK-27 proposals (results that use IM77 depend on
proposed bridge entries). Indus middle = S-DARK-26 parser, deduplicated, >= 2 signs.

Comparators: modern Chinese given names (131,735 distinct), ancient Chinese full names (254,630), Japanese kanji given names
(141,252) and given parts of real people's names (18,944), Vietnamese given names (12,265, Sino-Vietnamese syllables), Korean given
names (11,583, Wikidata CC0, Hangul syllable = Sino-Korean morpheme; no open hanja list exists), plus the S-DARK-56 phonetic lists
(Ur III, OB, Linear B, Latin), each with its own frequency-matched random-string null.

(1) Frequency shape and capacity (S-DARK-53 / 56). Logographic given names use 650-5,500 signs more evenly (element Gini 0.46-0.70)
    than Indus (0.74); but the Indus random null has the same Gini (0.72; 0.64 at length 2-4), so this is the small Indus sign
    inventory, not name structure. Capacity utilisation is uninformative for 2-element names (Chinese +0.9, Korean +1.4 log10).
(2) Slot binding: inside the logographic range at every n, length and level (pos_excess Indus 0.17-0.19 vs Chinese 0.16, Korean
    0.15, Japanese 0.16-0.23; held-out Indus 0.09-0.10 vs 0.06-0.20 at the same n). The S-DARK-56.2 phrase 'weaker slot structure
    than any attested name system' is retracted: it held only for inflecting-language names.
(3) Pair structure (bigram reuse, MI, pair predictability): the cycle-1 gap (Indus more structured than any logographic list) was a
    length and Mohenjo-daro-seal effect. At the exact Indus length 2-4 distribution Japanese names carry MORE pair structure than
    Indus (MI 0.10-0.31 vs 0.005), and held-out Indus middles carry less than any list but Chinese. Retracted.
(4) Closed edge set (S-DARK-56.3d): modern Chinese and Korean given names are as edge-free as Indus (strongest edge over own null
    0.96 / 1.05 vs Indus 1.09, variants 0.95-1.25). The 56.3d killer 'no closed edge set, therefore not names' does not apply to
    logographic given names. Japanese (finals 1.8x), Vietnamese (initials 1.7x), ancient Chinese (initials 2.1x, surname inside) and
    all phonetic lists (1.6-1.8x) do have one edge.
(5) Gendered sub-inventories: the spectral two-pool test fires on Japanese (z 12) and Vietnamese (z 8) gender, not on Chinese or Korean
    (z -0.1 / -0.6); it also fires on every structured non-gendered list (z 4-7). Indus z 4.8-6.0 (2.0 at length 2-4, -2.4 to +3.8 at
    held-out sites) and its minority pool is frame signs (M86, M176, M162/169, M391) plus numerals: no second name inventory.
    Absence is not evidence against names (Chinese and Korean show none either).
(6) Surname slot: every logographic surname is independent of its given name (MI excess -1.9% to -0.1%, no exclusive elements). The
    Indus closer (+3.8 to +4.9%, z 4.8-6.5) and the closer-agnostic last sign (+2.9 to +3.1%, z 8.6-10.4) are bound to the middle at
    all four levels, like phonetic affix slots (+3.0 to +6.5%). The Indus head set is not a surname slot.

ANSWER. On frequency shape, positional binding, pair structure, edge openness and free combination of common elements, Indus
middles are statistically indistinguishable from logographically written given names of the Chinese / Korean type; every S-DARK-56
argument that relied on those statistics ('not names') is withdrawn for the logographic case. ONE statistic still separates them
from every logographic list, in all 20 Indus variants (seq_raw / strong / all, IM77, MD, Harappa, held-out sites, numerals removed):
the SUBSTRING SHARE. About 10-12% of 2-4-sign Indus middles recur inside a longer middle, twice the shuffle rate (excess +0.03 to
+0.09); logographic given-name lists sit at or below their shuffle (-0.02 to 0.00). Phonetic name lists share the Indus property
(+0.01 to +0.07: hypocoristics and compounds), so it does not separate Indus from names in general, only from one-word-sign-per-
morpheme given names. A second statistic separates Indus from Japanese, Vietnamese and ancient Chinese but not from modern Chinese
or Korean: no edge of the Indus middle is closed. The structural difference outside the middle remains: the Indus head is chosen with
the middle (affix- or term-like, S-DARK-61), never like a surname.
Caveats: Chinese and Korean given names are almost all 2 elements, so their zero substring share is near-forced and the substring
result rests on Japanese, Vietnamese and ancient Chinese; substrings could partly be parser residue; 76% of Indus texts are
Mohenjo-daro + Harappa (held-out n = 186-261 replicates substring share and open edges, not pair statistics); lists are one copy per
distinct name, Indus middles one per distinct middle.
Would remove the last separator: a logographic name list of 3-4 elements with substring share > 0 at Indus length. Would
kill 'middle = logographic name': new Indus material (post-1977 sites) with substring share <= 0, or a closed edge appearing.
Not 'cracked'; no sign value proposed.
