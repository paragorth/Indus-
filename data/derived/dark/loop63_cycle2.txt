| S-DARK-63.2 | **Do Indus middles show the two signatures of logographic personal names: (a) two partially disjoint element pools (gendered sub-inventories), (b) a head set that behaves like a surname slot?** (a) Spectral bipartition (Fiedler vector of the normalised Laplacian) of the within-name co-occurrence graph of elements with >= 5 tokens; statistic = share of within-name element pairs on the same side, against 50 token shuffles that keep name lengths (z). Calibrated on the gender-tagged lists (Chinese, Japanese, Vietnamese, Korean given names, n = 1,431, gendered names only and all names), with the element's majority gender scored against its spectral side; also run on the non-gendered phonetic lists (Ur III, OB, Linear B, Latin), on Indus middles at seq_raw / seq_strong / seq_all / IM77, NAME-only, seals only, and on the Indus frequency-matched random null. For Indus, seal vs tablet was used as the 'label' (the only binary external tag available). (b) Head = surname of Chinese / Japanese / Vietnamese / Korean full names (2,000 drawn) vs head = Indus parser closer (-> middle) and closer-agnostic last sign (-> rest of the text); statistic = MI(head, middle element) minus its value with heads permuted among names (200x), as % of H(head); plus head inventory (types, top-1, top-10 share) and the share of frequent middle elements found with only one head. Controls: last given-name element used as a fake head (open slot); first / last element of Ur III and Linear B names (prefix / suffix slot).
