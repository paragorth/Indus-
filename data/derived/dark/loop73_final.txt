WHICH STATISTICS PUT INDUS WITH THE PE DESIGNATION SLOT
- Designation-frequency profile at equal lengths: uniqueness, Gini, Zipf slope, Heaps, top-10 share. Indus t = 0.13-0.28
  (0 = PE, 1 = nearest name list) at all three merge levels, 0.26-0.33 in IM77. At >= 2 elements, unmatched:
  PE 0.918 / 0.078 / 0.974 vs Indus 0.913-0.922 / 0.075-0.083 / 0.972-0.975 vs Ur III 0.61 / 0.34 / 0.85, Linear B 0.72 / 0.25 / 0.90.
- No closed initial set (PE -0.047, Indus +0.01, names +0.08 to +0.19; t ~ 0.45) and no large closed final set.
- Adjacent MI at equal lengths (Wells levels t -0.15 to 0.31; IM77 apart).
- Designation share of the entry's bits: PE 0.53 (0.69 for middles >= 2), Indus 0.60-0.62; the comparators' frames are
  longer word strings (0.37).
- Cross-document recurrence of the whole designation: PE 0.10, Indus seals 0.11-0.12, vs Linear B 0.34 and Ur III 0.51.
- Binding of a recurring designation to its head: PE 0.49, Indus 0.42-0.47 (ratio of multi-head share to null) - but
  Linear B names show the same (0.41), so this one is shared by all three.

WHICH SEPARATE THEM
- Substring share: Indus 0.057-0.060 (IM77 0.091) vs PE 0.010 (Ur III 0.032, Linear B 0.011): whole Indus middles
  reappear inside longer ones; the Indus slot is built compositionally, the PE slot is not.
- Length: PE middles are short (53% of entries have a 1-sign middle, 21% none; mean 2.99 at >= 2); Indus 3.87.
- Lone sign: a lone Indus seal sign is hapax-rich (0.16 vs 0.02 null) and from the middle inventory; a lone PE entry sign
  is often the class sign (29%) with no hapax excess. But a lone PE HEADER sign (no opener) is a middle-inventory or
  unseen sign (85%), class sign 1.4% - the Indus 1-sign-seal profile.
- Within-document structure: PE middles on one tablet share elements 3x (adjacent entries 5x, rare elements 31x) and
  repeat whole middles 14x - a tablet-local list vocabulary; Linear B names on a tablet share nothing (1.0x);
  Indus same-room seals 1.6-1.9x, frequent elements only (different unit; rare-element zero underpowered).

STATED PLAINLY
The Indus designation slot behaves like the Proto-Elamite designation slot on the statistics that tell a designation
slot from a name list. Measured the same way, the PE middle (the field most specialists read as a person or
household designation inside a readable accounting frame) is MORE unique, flatter and less name-like than Ur III or
Linear B personnel names, and the Indus middle sits beside it, about a fifth of the way towards the name lists. So the
'code-like, not names' profile of S-DARK-53/56 is exactly what a real accounting designation slot looks like when it
is written partly with logograms. It does not count against person or household designations; on the calibration it
fits them. If PE middles are person / unit designations, the Indus middle fits the same reading at the same strength.
Indus differs from PE in two ways. Its designations are longer, and they are built by extension (whole designations
reused inside longer ones). This fits a more developed or multi-part designation (e.g. person + affiliation) more than
the short ad hoc PE list labels. Neither system shows the 'same named person recurring across an archive' signature
that Linear B has (x4.4). That absence in PE warns against reading the Indus absence (S-DARK-64) as evidence that the
middles are not persons.
Limits: PE is 95% Susa; Indus rests mainly on Mohenjo-daro + Harappa (76% of texts) and Wells/IM77 agreement is
transcription-robust, not replication; PE's 'person designation' status is itself an interpretation by others, used
here only as the hypothesis being calibrated, not as evidence. Nothing is read; nothing is 'cracked'.
WORKING-DICTIONARY suggestion (grade C): Indus middle = person/unit designation of the PE-middle kind. Would support:
a new PE-like archive where the same middle recurs with different heads. Would kill: an accounting designation corpus
with known person designations that sits at the name-list end of the frequency ladder.
