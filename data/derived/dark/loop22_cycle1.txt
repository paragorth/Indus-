READING
  1. The Indus has no single anchoring edge. Both edges anchor, each for its own closed class: openers + connectives from the start
     (d +1.0 to +1.7), closers + suffix + pre-closer titles from the end (d -1.0 to -2.3). The large NAME residue is unanchored (mean d 0.04,
     18/42 either way). This is exactly the two-edged frame of GRAMMAR.md rendered as positional entropy; it adds no new structure.
  2. Against the controls the Indus sits between the two: Ur III (name + title formula) anchors from the start (ratio 0.5), Linear B (a
     true field record ending in IDEOGRAM NUMBER) anchors from the end (ratio 4.8), Indus 1.7 (held-out sites 1.3). The Indus end
     anchoring is weaker than that of a record format whose last two fields are an ideogram and a number.
  3. Fixed-offset signature? For a designed format we would expect many signs with H near 0 from one edge. Observed: only W752 (0.41 bits),
     W400 (0.60), W527 (0.61) and W151 (0.84) are under 1 bit from the end; W60 (0.96) from the start. All of them are grammar signs
     already known to sit at the text edge (closers, suffix, connective); none is a middle sign. In Linear B the comparable tokens are the
     NUM/ideogram fields; in Ur III ARAD2/zu. So Indus edge signs are as tightly placed as the fields of known formulae, but the texts
     have at most two anchored fields per edge, not a run of fixed offsets.
