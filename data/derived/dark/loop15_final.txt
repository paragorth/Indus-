Three cycles run, each on seq_raw, seq_strong and seq_all; results agree across the three sign-identity levels.

Question: what does the Indus code forbid beyond pairwise mutual exclusion (S36, S37, S289, S360)?
Method: fit the grammar's own generative model (order-2 KN chain per object class) on Mohenjo-daro + Harappa, generate 100 synthetic
corpora, list every co-occurrence / gapped ordered pair / skip-bigram / ordered triple over the ~110 commonest signs with expected >= 5 and
observed 0; control with 100 synthetic corpora (chance gaps), a within-text shuffle + refit, a model-free stratified permutation null
(site x object class), held-out sites with their own model, IM77 via the bridge, and deduplication of moulded tablets.

Findings in plain words
1. With all texts the corpus shows 27-34 gaps against a chance maximum of 3-7 per kind, but more than half are ghosts of single
   tablet moulds (29 copies of 501-405-2-240-520 make 'W501 never with the jar'; 14 copies of 806-845-61-407-850-900-740 make
   'W850 never with W240'; 705-33-845-407 x14 makes '705 never before 700').  After one copy per distinct text per site x class, 12-17 gaps remain.
2. Triple gaps are not order rules: the within-text shuffle produces as many (29-38) as the real corpus, and none has E >= 2.5 under the
   permutation null.  Every triple gap reduces to a pair or to a duplicated tablet.  No new multi-sign paradigm class appears.
3. The surviving negative space is the syntax of one grammar word, the marker W2 (M99):
   R1 written at most once per text (E 28, O 0 at home; 1 of 842 held-out; IM77 1 vs 55.7) - the repetition ban (S88) at its sharpest;
      the fish mark W240 is likewise never doubled (E 6; IM77 0 vs 7.2);
   R2 stands before what it introduces: fish marks W240/W233 and the jar never precede it with a gap (0 vs 10-61 expected; reverse 42-263);
   R3 never shares a text with the marked jars W741/W742 of the 920-60-741 opening phrase (0 vs 11-12; held-out 0 vs 3.5, persistent in
      all three levels, chance persistence max 2; IM77 3 vs 16.2) - S333/S334 extended from adjacency to the whole text;
   R4 (weak, E 5) W233 before W220 only as the bound pair 233-220.
4. Quantity rules: the apparent 'minimum lot' (no 1, no 2 on counted goods; 12 of 46 items depleted, Bonferroni P < 0.05) is a writing rule.
   The short one-stroke W1, the tall one-stroke W31 and the two-short-stroke W2 are grammatical markers (S234, GRAMMAR), so the short count
   series starts at 3 for every item it counts; 1 and 2 are written tall and only in fixed terms (II-fish, II-jar, II/III/IV-W700, 3-845,
   3-923, 3-550).  Without W1/W2 tokens, trees (P = 0.19) and W900 (P = 0.83) are not depleted beyond their series.  The '1 tree never /
   2 tree once' hole dissolves: tall 2 + tree occurs 13x at home (32-407 in a seven-copy tablet, 32-390 x4) and the 'short 2 tree'
   cases are the W2 marker after an opener.  Numbered persons: 100 of 728 at home and 18 of 138 at held-out sites (not 'never'); the
   counted figures are fixed terms (3-156 x35, 2/3-140 x14, W1-142 x10); only the plain person W90/91 is never counted (2 of 196, < 1.5%).
5. Caveats: held-out sites are small (842-930 texts), so only 4-7 gaps per run reach E_held >= 2; Wells and IM77 share ~70% of objects
   (transcription-robust, not replicated); 'never' means < 3/n; MD+H carry 76% of texts.

Predictions outside the corpus (pre-registrable): a new text will not contain W2 twice, will not have W2 after a fish mark or after the
jar with a sign between, will not combine W2 with W741/W742, and will not show short strokes 1 or 2 directly before a tree or W900.

Verdict: NOT cracked.  The code's negative space, once moulded duplicates are removed, is almost entirely the placement grammar of
one marker (W2) and the frame it belongs to; there is no hidden class of forbidden content combinations and no lot-size accounting rule.
