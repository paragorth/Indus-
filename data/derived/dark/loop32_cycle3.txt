S-DARK-32 cycle 3: granularity-matched library and an Indus-vs-surrogate feature audit. Tools: tools/dark_loop32_c2.py --gran 0.4 2.5
(loop32_c3_gran_nonum.txt, loop32_c3_gran_withnum.txt), tools/dark_loop32_c3audit.py (loop32_c3_audit.txt).

A. Granularity-matched library. Keep only reference corpora whose vocabulary growth (types per 2,000 tokens) is within 0.4-2.5 x the
   Indus value (274 -> 110-686): khipu, proto_cuneiform, proto_elamite [A]; heraldry, chess, chords [D/G]; linb_syll, ur3_names_syll,
   ur3_syll [L]. Dropped: the character codes (ICD 35, aircraft 40, HTS 99 types / 2,000), the word corpora (Unicode names 832, Latin
   1,164, Linear B words 725, Ur III words 764). 9 corpora, 3 per class.
   - 3-class, numerals dropped: LOCO 2/9 = 0.22 (chance 0.33), P = 0.55; 3-NN 0.22. Binary L-vs-not 0.44 vs 0.67 baseline. Nothing.
   - 3-class, numerals kept: 6/9 = 0.67, permutation null mean 0.21, 95th pct 0.56, P = 0.065; 3-NN 0.56, P = 0.032. The whole gain is
     the two numeral features (chosen in 9 of 9 folds): accounting corpora carry 31-68 % numeral tokens and 31-63 % numeral runs,
     language and designed corpora 0-10 % and 0 %. Indus: 12 % numerals, 7 % runs, i.e. between the two groups (closer to L in share,
     and with the numeral-start bias -0.29 that no L corpus has).
   - Indus under the numeral-kept model: P(L) 0.88-0.89 (raw / strong / all), 0.94 (IM77), P(D) <= 0.01 -- but Markov-2 0.90 and
     bigram 0.88 get the same; slot-shuffled -> A 0.42 / D 0.25. Same failure as cycle 2: the verdict is shared with the surrogates.
   - Binary with the five features the nested selection prefers (det_loy50_max, H_uni_norm, loyal_per_sign, unique_share,
     uniq_vs_bigram): Indus P(L) 0.54-0.58 (a coin), Markov-2 0.46, bigram 0.32, slot-shuffled 0.14; LOCO accuracy 0.44, below baseline.
B. Audit: what separates the REAL Indus from its own surrogates (z over the 8 subsample rows of the control)? Against Markov-2:
   H_last/H_mid 0.72 vs 0.96, top-10 closer share 0.64 vs 0.42, fixed pair share 0.71 vs 0.45, slot-vs-bigram +0.21 vs +0.78 bits,
   n80_last 0.41 vs 0.81, uniq_vs_bigram 0.83 vs 0.94, nesting 0.41 vs 0.58 (all |z| > 19). Against the slot-shuffle: hubs, hub
   mobility and free use (0 in the shuffle), fixed pairs 0.71 vs 0.25, slot preference +0.21 vs -0.15, small-set share 0.15 vs 0.00.
   So the battery sees plainly that Indus is a closer-paradigm frame with within-slot order and lexicalised bigrams -- the S289 /
   S-DARK-19 / S-DARK-25 picture -- and that a Markov-2 chain misses the closer paradigm and the fixed order. EVERY one of these
   separating features, however, lies inside the range of the language corpora (closer paradigms, fixed pairs and low middle
   uniqueness are what Ur III and Linear B lines also have). They identify Indus as 'formulaic frame', not as a type.
C. Features on which Indus lies OUTSIDE the range of all six language corpora (corpus means): det_loy50_max 0.35 (IM77 0.30) vs L
   0.46-0.84 (no loyal class sign, S-DARK-25; Markov-2 reproduces it, 0.36); slot_exclusivity 0.07 vs L 0.10-0.35 (few signs
   confined to one position class; Markov-2 0.02); n80_first/n80_all 1.11 vs L 0.26-1.01 (opener position as open as the whole
   inventory; S331 openers are a small class but most texts start with a 'name' sign); num_end_bias -0.29 vs L 0.00-0.63 and
   num_run_share 0.07 vs L 0.00-0.02 (numerals early and in runs, S197-S199 count use). Of the non-numeral three, only det_loy50_max
   is also outside the designed range on the language side (D and A corpora reach 0.0-0.9, i.e. it is a 'not-language' marker, not
   a 'which non-language' marker), and it is the single metric that most separates Indus from language writing when the numeral
   annotation is held out: Indus has no sign that >= 50 % of its hosts must carry, every language corpus has (Ur III d / ki, Linear B
   ideograms, Latin 'dis manibus'-type formula words, Ur III name-final syllables).
D. Full-space nearest neighbours (all 36 structural features, z over corpus means): seq_raw -> IM77 2.0, Markov-2 Indus 3.8,
   proto-cuneiform 4.3, bigram Indus 4.4, slot-shuffled Indus 4.8, Linear B syllables 4.8, Ur III names 5.1, Proto-Elamite 5.2;
   seq_all the same order; IM77 -> Markov-2 3.0, bigram 3.5, Ur III syllables 4.8, proto-cuneiform 4.8. Indus is closer to a second-
   order Markov chain fitted to itself than to any known system, and the two transcriptions (Wells / Mahadevan) are 2.0 apart, i.e.
   transcription noise is half the distance to the nearest real corpus.
