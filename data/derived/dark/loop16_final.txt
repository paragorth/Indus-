DARK LOOP 16 - USAGE INTENSITY - final summary (5 cycles; engine data/derived/dark/loop16_engine.py; per-cycle write-ups loop16_cycle1..5.txt; raw tables loop16_cycle<N>_seq_{raw,strong,all}.txt; follow-ups loop16_cycle4b_seq_raw.txt, loop16_cycle5b_seq_raw.txt)
Data: 224 TAG lines on 193 objects, aligned 1:1 to data/raw/inscriptions.csv (sides, condition, preservation, mm sizes). All tests run at seq_raw, seq_strong, seq_all; no result depends on a merge. Nulls: labels permuted within site (x object type x material x text length where stated). Arrows counted and Bonferroni-corrected per cycle; held-out = Mohenjo-daro / Harappa / small sites compared by sign.

Verdicts in one line each:
 16.1 impressions per text: 'one or two heavy users + singletons' at Lothal (x11, x10) and Kalibangan (x8), every sealing different at Dholavira (35/35) and Harappa (11/11); no text feature marks heavy use after correction (openers lead, raw p 0.03). Deposit effect, not a credential class.
 16.2 ghost issuers: 83% of sealing texts (90% at >= 3 signs; 67 of 87 occur nowhere else) have no seal anywhere; only 5 of 22 matches are same-site, all 2-sign; capture-recapture gives ~10^4 distinct seal texts (upper-type bound, consistent with S309). Matched texts are jar-final (59% vs 30%, length-controlled p 0.007, not Bonferroni).
 16.3 dead seals: 91% of seal texts never recur on a sealing or tablet; dead and used seals are physically identical once text length is held (size, condition, emblem, material all null); used texts end in jar/arrow (p 0.001, passes correction at the margin) - restates S344 (tablets copy closer phrases).
 16.4 condition as wear: reversed then null. Damage shortens the readable text, so condition predicts text class; among fully preserved seals no class differs (p 0.23-0.40). Drop the condition field as a usage proxy.
 16.5 multi-sided objects: 0 of 793 objects repeat a text on two sides (TAB:B 0/342 vs 4.6, TAB:I 0/340 vs 9.2 expected, p <= 0.008); 415 of 682 Harappa tablets pair one count side 'N . W700' with one title side; Mohenjo-daro TAB:C sides share no signs (1 of 16 vs 4.8, p 0.014). Ledger, not tally. Lothal bales carry one constant shipper seal (x11) plus a varying second seal on four tags.

Sealings repeat texts more than same-site seals at Lothal, Kalibangan, Mohenjo-daro (p < 0.001) but not more than tablets (p 0.4-1.0). Spectrum is 'one or two heavy users + singletons', not Zipf. Identical at all three merge levels | **Null-leaning, descriptive.** Heavy use is a deposit effect (one bale seal stamped 8-11 times in one warehouse), not a class of credential; with 11-35 distinct texts per site anything subtler is below the floor. | S-DARK-16.2 | Usage intensity (b): 'ghost issuers'. Distinct sealing texts (>= 2 signs, 133; >= 3 signs, 87) with no exact seal match anywhere; share per site; chance control = seal texts scrambled within text (300x); Chapman capture-recapture of the seal-text population; ghost vs matched features with the label permuted within site and within site x length (3000x, 31 arrows, Bonferroni 0.0016); seq_raw/strong/all | 111 of 133 sealing texts (83%) have no seal anywhere; at >= 3 signs 78 of 87 (90%), and 67 occur nowhere else at all. Ghost share by site 75-91%, Dholavira 15/15 and Mohenjo-daro 7/7 at >= 3 signs. Capture-recapture: S=1898, T=133, m=22 -> ~11,000 distinct seal texts (within-site m = 0-3, so sites give lower bounds only, ~950 Dholavira). Matched texts are jar-final 59% vs ghosts 30% (length-controlled p 0.007, same sign at Lothal and Dholavira) and never contain W32 (0/22 vs 18/111); nothing passes Bonferroni. Identical at all merge levels | **Descriptive, agrees with S309/S324.** The seal record behind the sealings is almost entirely missing: the seals that were actually used at Lothal, Dholavira and Kalibangan are not in the excavated corpus (lost, abroad, or at unexcavated sites). A capture-recapture figure of order 10^4 distinct seal texts is a rough upper-type bound that matches S309's '95% of new texts are new'. Office (jar) texts are the ones that do recur on surviving seals; W32-containing sealing texts have no seal counterpart (upper bound < 3/22) |
| S-DARK-16.3 | Usage intensity (c): 'dead seals'. Seal lines (>= 2 signs, 2028) whose text never appears on a sealing or tablet (1849, 91%) vs those with attested use (179): size, material, condition, emblem, text class; used label permuted within site x seal type (1000x, 16 arrows) and again within site x type x text length (15 arrows; Bonferroni over 31 = 0.0016); held-out by Mohenjo-daro / Harappa / other; seq_raw/strong/all | Used share is flat across cities (8-12%). Naively, used seals are shorter-texted (-2.2 signs), smaller (-215 mm2), in worse condition (-0.26) and poorer in numerals and fish (all p 0.001) - but every physical difference vanishes when text length is held (area p 0.05, condition p 0.34, emblem p 0.45, material p 0.18). The text does matter a little: closer-bearing (jar/arrow) short texts are the ones that get copied onto tablets, which restates S344 (tablets reproduce closer phrases) rather than measuring seal wear. 'Use' here is 90% tablet copies, so this is a scribal-copying result, not a sealing result; 91% dead is an upper bound on non-use given S309 sampling |
| S-DARK-16.4 | Usage intensity (d): does the CISI condition grade (Poor/Fair/Good/Fine) or preservation field behave like wear, i.e. do office (jar-final) or long texts show more wear than quantity (numeral+tree) texts? 2125 seals; text class or text length permuted within site x material x seal type (3000x, 10 arrows); control = only seals recorded as fully preserved with complete text (1235); seq_raw/strong/all | Reversed and then null. Longer texts are in BETTER condition (r +0.24, p < 0.001) and less damaged (r -0.18); stock seals are the least damaged (16% vs 35%, p < 0.001); the short 'other' class is the worst (46% damaged). Among fully preserved seals every class effect vanishes (office +0.005 p 0.40; stock -0.055 p 0.23) and the length effect shrinks to r +0.12 (p 0.001; same sign at Mohenjo-daro +0.10 and Harappa +0.12). Identical at all merge levels | **Null; the field is not wear.** Damage shortens the readable text and strips the class markers, so condition predicts text class, not the other way round. The residual 'long texts are better preserved' most likely reflects selection (fine, well-cut seals get long texts and survive better) rather than use. The condition field cannot serve as a usage proxy; drop it from the usage family |

Caveats for the whole family: TAG n = 224 (193 objects), 11-70 lines per site; Lothal + Dholavira supply 65% of sealings while Mohenjo-daro + Harappa supply 76% of seals, so every seal-vs-sealing comparison crosses a site boundary unless stratified (all were). Absences are upper bounds (< 3/n). Nothing here is 'cracked'; the family yields two usable facts (ghost share ~85-90%; tablets never repeat a text across sides) and retires the condition field.
