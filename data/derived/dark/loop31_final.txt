S-DARK-31 (dark loop 31) FINAL: parallelogram / vector-offset analogies in the Indus sign space
Files: tools/dark_loop31.py (cycle 1), dark_loop31_c2.py (cycle 2), dark_loop31_c3.py (cycle 3); rows in loop31_cycle1.txt, loop31_cycle2.txt, loop31_cycle3.txt (31.2 and 31.3 already in STRATEGIES.md; 31.1 to be merged before the Summary section).

Question: does the Indus sign system have analogical structure (a - b = d - c), as a language's lexicon does (king:queen :: man:woman) or as a designed code with a few systematic operators would (plain:marked across many bases)?

Method: PPMI + SVD embeddings (dim 30) over neighbours at distance 1-2, position, S310 slot label, object class; MD + H complete texts (2,840 texts, 11,407 tokens); vocabulary 150 commonest signs; three merge levels. Cycle 1 mines offsets blind (4-corner and 2-corner rules) against three nulls (within-slot shuffle, slot-grammar synthetic corpus, within-position shuffle; 50-100 replicates each) and calibrates the pipeline on Ur III legends, Linear B (words and syllables) and Proto-Elamite. Cycle 2 probes known relation kinds as directions and tests predicted pairs on held-out sites and IM77. Cycle 3 tests compositionality of derived signs (strokes, enclosure, doubling, roof, ligature, fish marks, jar marks).

Results
1. Blind mining (31.1): 459-480 offset classes under the 4-corner rule, 1.2-1.3x the within-slot shuffle (P = 0.01-0.02), 2.0-2.4x as many sharp classes (mean cos >= 0.7); but a slot-grammar corpus with an order-1 middle chain reproduces 86-92% of the classes (P = 0.04-0.15) and 60-75% of the sharp ones. Zero classes are both sharp and large (>= 5 disjoint pairs) at any level or rule; null expects 0-3. Calibration: Indus 1.1-1.3x classes / 2-4.5x sharp vs position shuffle, like Proto-Elamite (1.2x / 2.0x), below Ur III syllables (1.5x / 10.5x) and Linear B syllables (1.8x / 4.3x); only Linear B read in words yields sharp+big classes (2 under rule 4, 25 under rule 2).
2. Known relations as directions (31.2): exactly two are real, sharp directions at all three levels: plain fish -> marked fish (offset cos 0.55 vs null 0.03, P = 0.0005; the four marked fish pairwise 0.79-0.82) and closer -> its own qualifier class (0.33-0.38 vs 0.01-0.02, P = 0.0005). Short<->tall numeral of equal value is weak (0.26-0.36, P = 0.002-0.007). Not directions: numeral steps (3 -> 4 -> 5; the stroke numerals are a cluster, not a line), allographs (identities), jar -> marked jar (a shared slot), marked<->marked fish, titles, opener<->opener and closer<->closer (clusters). The blind classes hold known relations at base rate (20-29 of ~1,500 member pairs vs 14-17 expected). Predicted new pairs generalise to held-out sites and IM77 only as same-slot-class membership (3-4x the control hit rate on context similarity), never as pair-specific relations (closer -> qualifier adjacency at chance: 0 of 24-31 held-out).
3. Compositionality (31.3): no derivation operator (add stroke, enclose, double, roof, ligate) is a consistent vector; the two testable ligatures are not sums of their parts (P = 0.53-0.55, n = 2). Derived stroke/family signs retain their base's usage weakly (cos 0.25 vs 0.10, P = 0.0005), which fits graphic variants or sub-types, not composed words. 85% of derived signs have < 4 tokens and are untestable.

How many robust offsets: TWO. (a) The fish mark: plain fish W220 -> any of the marked fish W233/240/235/231 (not the 4-stroke fish W226, which has become a closer). (b) Head -> modifier: a closer (W740, 520, 527, 700, 154/158 ...) -> the qualifier class it selects (S289/S303). Both are relations the frame already documents; neither was found by the blind search, which recovers only slot-cluster-to-slot-cluster movements. A third, short<->tall numeral of equal value, is weak and depends on three pairs.

What they encode: slot-class membership and one graphic-marking paradigm. Offset (b) is the move from the closer cluster to the qualifier cluster; offset (a) is the only case in the sign list where a graphic modification is also a consistent shift in use. Nothing in the geometry orders the numerals, composes ligatures, or relates middles to one another.

Verdict on the geometry: NEITHER a designed code nor a language, on this test. A designed code would give few, sharp, productive operators applied across many bases (the fish mark is the single candidate, and it covers one base); a language would give many weak lexical analogies that at least match Ur III or Linear B syllables on the calibration ladder (the Indus sits lower, at the Proto-Elamite level, and its excess over the frame is 8-14% of classes). The structure that remains after the frame is removed is small and is the frame's own head -> modifier dependency. This is consistent with S321 / S-DARK-18 (middles are not a name stock) and with the slot-code reading of GRAMMAR.md: the sign system behaves like a formulaic frame with about ten head words, each with its own qualifier set, plus one marked paradigm (the fish), and the middles are too sparse and too unique to carry analogical structure at this corpus size.

Caveats: MD + H only (76% of texts); P floors at 0.01-0.02 for cycle 1; absolute class counts and even ratios move with embedding dimension (ratio 1.1 at dim 20, 2.3 at dim 50 for one shuffle), so the cycle-1 verdict rests on the consistent direction of effect across three nulls, three levels and two rules; held-out and IM77 checks are transcription-robust, not replicated; the ligature verdict rests on 2 pairs. What would change the verdict: a corpus two to three times larger in which the blind search yields sharp+big classes whose members are not cross-slot pairs, or an operator other than the fish mark that applies across >= 5 bases as a consistent direction.
