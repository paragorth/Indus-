# Loop 4 final: "the UNITS may be wrong" (4 cycles, 2026-10-03)

Question. Does any re-segmentation of the Indus corpus (merged sign pairs/triples, allograph merge levels, sub-sign
components) make it simultaneously MORE predictable out of sample AND more name-like / lexicon-like than the sign level,
i.e. is there a hidden lexicon under a wrong segmentation?

Answer: no. Four independent tests, all three sequence levels (seq_raw / seq_strong / seq_all), held-out sites
(all other named sites; Dholavira, Lothal, Kalibangan separately; MD<->Harappa swap), arrows-fired nulls (the whole
search re-run on bigram-generated corpora), and two positive controls (Ur III seal legends with known word boundaries;
a planted Tamil logo-syllabic corpus).

Best segmentation per statistic (Indus, seq_raw, held-out sites; others in loop4_rows.txt):
  bits/sign          BPE k=60: 7.588 vs 7.633 (-0.6%)   [null: BPE never helps; Ur III BPE -3.3% = true word level]
  uniqueness ratio   sub-sign 'all'/'marks': 1.015-1.03 vs 1.03 (null gives the same drop; splitting signs lowers it trivially)
  within-text rep.   allograph levels 0.267-0.274 vs 0.274 (no change)
  grammar coverage   sign level or allograph levels 0.444-0.446; BPE cuts it to 0.29 (merged units straddle slots)
  joint (bits AND uniq-or-rep)  7 of 29 segmentations on seq_raw, 4/29 strong, 8/29 all; bigram null 3-7 of 29.
  MDL                bigram code: best k = 0-10, gain <= 0.09%; unigram code +5.5% vs null +4.0-4.5%, no interior minimum.
  cross-site chunks  0 of 90 rows above the bigram null; NAME-residue chunk recurrence 0.54x chaining.

What the merges that do help are: 100+740, 817+2, 760+740, 176+740, 740+400, 705+33+520, 920+60(+741), 692+60 -
the opener+marker, qualifier+jar, arrow-phrase and S331 opener units already in GRAMMAR.md. The small real gain over
the bigram null (~0.04 bits, ~1 MDL point) is this frame, not a lexicon.

Sign-identity level (allographs): merging the real list lowers held-out bits by 0.150, shape-blind frequency-matched
fake lists by 0.068 (median) / 0.097 (best), and the real list on bigram-null data by 0.110. A ~0.04-0.05 bit vote
that the allograph list is better than chance, nothing more; results do not depend on the merge level.

Sub-sign components (41 base+mark pairs, 13 ligatures): decomposition hurts predictability (+0.08 bits median);
ligatures alone neutral. The components are not the alphabet.

Key caveat (cycle 3). Ur III seal-owner names WITH repeats show the lexicon signal strongly (uniqueness 0.278, BPE
-5.4%); the same names counted ONCE each look like the Indus name residue (uniqueness 1.105 vs 1.04-1.07, BPE -0.053
vs -0.02). The Indus corpus is deduplicated by site+text, so every test here runs on the version of the data in which a
real onomasticon would also be invisible. The planted control adds that a large Zipf lexicon (3,000 words) does not pay
under MDL at 13k tokens. These tests therefore exclude a SMALL hidden lexicon (hundreds of recurring multi-sign words)
and a wrong unit level for the frame, not thousands of rare multi-sign names.

Leads for other loops: (1) rerun the uniqueness and BPE tests on undeduplicated impression counts (Lothal and
Kalibangan sealings, Harappa tablets, the S149 twins) where the same text genuinely recurs; (2) add the S331 opener
units 920+60 / 692+60 / 920+60+741 to the S310 parser - they still sit in the 'name residue'.
