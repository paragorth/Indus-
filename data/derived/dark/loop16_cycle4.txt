Numbers (seq_raw; seq_strong and seq_all identical to the third decimal):
 Hypothesis was 'office/long texts show more wear'. The data run the other way: text length correlates POSITIVELY with condition grade (r +0.236, null +0.04, p<0.001) and negatively with damage (r -0.177, p<0.001); office_jar seals are in slightly better condition (+0.07, p 0.024) and less damaged (-0.048, p 0.008); stock seals are the best preserved (damaged 16% vs 35%, -0.23, p<0.001; cond +0.24, p 0.008). 'other' (mean length 3.3) is the worst class (damaged 46%).
 Confound: damage removes signs, so a damaged seal reads as a short 'other' text; a stock or office text can only be classified when its numeral+tree or final jar survived. Condition is causing the text class, not the reverse.
 Control (4b): seals with preservation = complete AND complete = Y (1235), where the text is fully read and the grade is surface wear only: office_jar +0.005 (p 0.40), stock -0.055 (p 0.23), counted_other +0.018, arrow -0.029 - all null.
