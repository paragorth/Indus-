S-DARK-67 FINAL: ARE THE MOHENJO-DARO COPPER TABLETS A KEY (a legend table binding each image to one text)?
Script: tools/dark_loop67.py <1|2|3|4> [nperm]; logs loop67_cycle{1..4}_log.txt; rows in loop67_cycle{1..4}.txt.
Data: data/im77/im77_corpus_lines.csv (135 copper tablets; image side = fs80, or an image written as a single 'sign'
face), data/raw/inscriptions.csv TAB:C (229 Mohenjo-daro faces + K-158) with data/derived/merged-corpus-canonical.json
seq_raw/seq_strong/seq_all, data/derived/dark/loop24_pairs.json for the Wells <-> IM77 object alignment.

PLAIN ANSWER
1. Do the copper tablets bind text to image? Only at the level of the casting die. Copies of one die share text and image
   (identical-text pairs share the image 0.85 vs 0.10 shuffled, P = 0.001; seals 0.76 vs 0.76, P = 0.57), but Harappa's
   moulded tablets show the same (0.94 vs 0.46) because a mould reproduces both faces. Once dies are the unit (29 dies with a
   recorded field symbol), the image predicts nothing about the text (H(text|image) 1.32 bits vs 1.30 shuffled, P = 0.98).
   The binding is many-to-many: 3/25 texts carry two or three different animals (53-67-407-124-169 under ox-antelope, horned
   tiger and tiger; 336-89-296-342-98 under elephant and horned tiger; 345-341-18-252-97-25-245-245 under unicorn and the
   two-headed animal) and 7/14 images carry 2-5 different texts (goat-antelope 5, two-headed 4, rhino 4, ox-antelope 3).
2. Can any text be read as the name or class of its image? No. The bound texts are ordinary legend texts (24 full
   name + frame, 9 designation-only, 12 with the copper-specific 845-407 closer of S306, the rest short labels); their
   closing units recur on seals under the unicorn and other animals (elephant text unit 923-740 / M296-342 on 18 seals,
   13 unicorn, 0 elephant; rhino copper text 204-245-342 on a unicorn and a buffalo seal), they recur elsewhere at the
   ordinary rate (7/62 vs 10.9 [6,16] control), and nothing in a text matches the seal statistics of its animal (2/54
   statistics at P < 0.05, 2.7 expected; Wells 0/19). The 845-407 ending is shared by hare, archer, tiger/ox and knot dies,
   so it marks the object class, not the picture.
3. The images are a closed set of their own, not the seal bestiary: ranks inverted (unicorn 3/110 vs 1,043/1,375 on
   seals; ox-antelope 23/110 vs 2/1,375), four copper-only images (hare, two-headed animal, endless knot, double-axe), no zebu.
4. Replication and prediction. Same objects read twice (Wells, IM77): transcription-robust, not replicated; IM77's field
   symbols are consistent within a die where the Wells symbol field is 'Othr' or self-contradictory. All three merge
   levels identical. Leave-one-die-out: a new die with a known image repeated the earlier text 0/42 times (< 7%); the only
   near-hits (5/42 at edit distance 1) are variant castings of one family; same head sign 7/42 = the shuffled rate.
   Prediction for a newly found copper tablet: if it is another cast of a known die its text is read right from its picture
   about half the time (48/100 leave-one-tablet-out); if it is a new die, not at all. The one falsifiable residue: a second
   hare, archer, endless-knot or unicorn die (1 die each so far) should carry 65-336-89-407-169-183-169 /
   389-407-124-169-373-287-342 / 171-59-407-169 / 345-341-18-252-97-25-245-245; every image with >= 3 dies has already
   failed this test. Caveats: all Mohenjo-daro (one site); 'never' = upper bound < 3/n; the Wells 'Othr' label hides the
   ox-antelope (22/34 aligned cases).

VERDICT: not a key. The copper tablets are a cast series of a few dozen dies, each die with one picture and one legend text,
distributed city-wide (DK, HR, VS, L, SD; 6-11 loci per die). Picture and text are co-produced, not co-referential:
the same text goes out under different animals and the same animal over different texts, exactly as on seals (S325, S329,
S-DARK-34). Nothing here is 'cracked'; the S306/S307 copper closer 845-407 (M407-169) stays a B-grade object-class marker.
