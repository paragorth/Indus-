(1) Catalogue. 213 TAG objects in inscriptions.csv; 33 with >= 2 recorded faces; after collapsing identical dies
    23 sealings carry >= 2 distinct legible impressions (38 pairs) + 4 with an illegible partner: Lothal 12, Dholavira 3,
    Kalibangan 3, Harappa 3, Mohenjo-daro 1, Rupar 1. Share 11-13% (upper bound). Frenez & Tosi 2005 give 31 of 93 Lothal
    sealings with >= 2 impressions, Wells records only 14 of them with >= 2 faces, so Wells is a lower bound.
    The elephant bale sealings L-161-170 (817-2-48-740) have ONE impression each: not two-party documents.
    Identical at all three merge levels.
(2) A-C vs nulls (1,000x; same-site sealing impressions, length-matched, same-site seal pairs).
    A shared middle: 4/38 vs 6.1 expected (P 0.91); Lothal 0/18 vs 2.9 (P_lo 0.04); same middle 0/38. NULL/REVERSED.
    B head pairing: head x head table at chance (P 0.83 Wells, 0.99 IM77); jar+arrow 1 vs 1.4. The only Wells hint,
      jar WITH jar (9/38 vs 6.1, P 0.11; non-Lothal 5/20 vs 1.5, P 0.007), is the opposite of complementary roles and
      does not hold in IM77 (7/58 vs 7.2). NULL.
    C office stamp + private seal: one frame-only 6/38 vs 8.7 (P_hi 0.95); one seal-matched 17/38 = 0.447 vs 0.448;
      in IM77 one-matched 19/58 vs 0.46 expected (P_lo 0.02, reverse). NULL.
    D incidental co-sealing: SUPPORTED. The heads say nothing about roles on one document: when two seals co-sign they
      tend to carry the same head (both jar), so the head is a title class shared by colleagues, not a role on the act.
(3) Lothal / Kalibangan office stamp: the recurring co-sealer 705-500-741-1-55-220-740-90 (IM77 336-209-343-98-121-59-342-1)
    is Lothal's heaviest-used seal; 5 multi-impression sealings and 6 partners vs 4.1 / 5.0 under the frequency null
    (P 0.34). IM77 shows a team of 4-5 seals used together in varying combinations (F&T context W1 'same seals as W'),
    three of them opener-initial, no seal on every document (max 5 of 16). Kalibangan: K-87/88 are one seal stamped
    twice + a jar fragment (IM77 8119); K-89 = IM77 8101 has four different seals sharing only the marker W2/M99.
    Mohenjo-daro IM77: co-sealers are MORE distinct than chance (31 texts in 32 slots, P 0.03): one-off seals.
(4) IM77 replication: 34 true sealings, 58 pairs (Harappa 'sealings' = S93 voucher tablets, separated). All nulls agree
    with Wells; transcription-robust (same objects), not replicated at a held-out site. Bridge check: 14/47 Wells
    impressions found verbatim in IM77 sides; the hub text differs only by W1 -> M97 vs IM77 M98 and W390 -> M161 vs M162.
    seq_strong / seq_all change nothing.
What rests on Lothal alone: the recurring-seal network and half of all pairs. Mohenjo-daro (15 IM77 sealings) is the
only independent-looking block and gives the same nulls. Sample: 38 + 58 pairs; a 20% effect could hide.
Verdict: D. Co-sealing is incidental or collegial (several holders of same-headed titles from one local pool), not a
consignor / inspector / recipient structure; nothing here supports role readings of the heads. Not 'cracked'.
