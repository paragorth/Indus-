# Family: seals = what a site issues, clay sealings (TAG, 224 objects, 185 distinct site x text) = what arrives. Four arrows: (a) vocabulary complementarity, (b) counted-item direction, (c) flow-graph reciprocity/hierarchy, (d) pots vs sealings (+ Lothal fastening type).
# Arrows fired: about 150 per seq level (cycle 1: 30; cycle 2: 60; cycle 3: ~35; cycle 4: ~18). Bonferroni threshold ~0.0003. Nothing passes it after collapsing identical impressions. All results identical on seq_raw / seq_strong / seq_all; no result depends on a merge.

VERDICT: the family is null. What arrives at a site is not complementary to what it issues; counted goods on sealings are the same mix as on seals at every site; pot labels are their own short vocabulary and are not closer to sealings than to seals; the one Lothal seal text is impressed on pottery, boxes, sacks, straw and pegs alike, so the sealing text names the sealer, not the goods.

Three methodological findings worth keeping:
1. Repeated impressions of one seal (10 Lothal bales L-161..170; 8 Kalibangan copies of 91-56-2-417-890-892) manufacture p < 0.001 'effects' in every vocabulary test. Collapse (site, type, text) before any sealing-vs-seal comparison.
2. Correction to S324/S326: 'all 18 matches cross-site' is the seal-count expectation (93%) because sealings come from Lothal, Kalibangan and small sites while 63% of long seal texts are from Mohenjo-daro. 'Flow only outward from Mohenjo-daro' is mostly that share; the residual excess (8 of 9 distinct matched texts from Mohenjo-daro vs 56% expected, p = 0.012 uncorrected) is suggestive only.
3. Power: with 15-61 distinct sealing texts per site, only shifts of ~20 percentage points were detectable; absences (no fish on Kalibangan sealings 0/15; no N·bracket on sealings outside Dholavira/Harappa) are upper bounds.
