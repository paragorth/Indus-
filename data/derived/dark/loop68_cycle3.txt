| S-DARK-68.3 | **A second modern naming population and a designed code with geography: US amateur-radio licensees by state of address (FCC ULS amateur licence database, public domain; 20 largest states, 190 pairs).** Surnames as letter bigrams and whole; call signs (centrally issued by the FCC; the digit is the call district, a deliberate geographic field) as characters; call-sign suffix only (the centrally issued part without the geographic field) as characters. n = 200 / 30 / noise-matched (within 0.142 bits). Licensees are not a random sample of state populations; no <350 km pairs among the 20 largest states with enough licensees (9 pairs, none with all units). | **Surnames, 350-900 km (41 pairs): letter bigrams x1.04 at n = 200 and x1.05 at the noise-matched n = 199 (excess 0.006 bits; 0 of 41 significant); whole surnames x1.02, reuse x1.29 (0 of 41 pairs < 1). No distance gradient (x1.05 / 1.06 / 1.05 across bands).** Call signs with the district digit: x12.3 at n = 200, x1.92 noise-matched (n = 15), 85% of pairs P < 0.05; the digit is the modal district for 72-92% of a state's licensees. Suffix only: x1.00 at n = 200, x1.01 noise-matched, 0 significant. | **The US surname pool is as uniform across states at 600 km as the Indus sign inventory (x1.04-1.05), but whole surnames are local (reuse x1.29). A centrally issued code is uniform (x1.01) when it carries no geographic field and grossly non-uniform (x2-12) when it does: central issuance does not by itself produce uniformity, and uniformity does not by itself imply central issuance.** |
