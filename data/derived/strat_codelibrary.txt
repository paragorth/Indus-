NOTES
- indus_bigram is a self-control (a bigram generator trained on Indus itself), not a known system; it marks how far a Markov copy of Indus sits. Among KNOWN systems Proto-Elamite entry lines are nearest for all four Indus versions (seq_raw, seq_strong, seq_all, seals only), ahead of khipu cord clusters, Linear B lines, heraldic blazons, Ur III seal legends and Latin inscriptions. Bootstrap mean rank: proto_elamite 1.6 vs khipu 3.4 (whole corpus); 2.0 vs linear_b 3.0 (seals).
- What drives the Proto-Elamite match: within-text repetition far below the shuffle null (0.27 vs 0.23; language controls 0.9-1.8), frame counts, mean length, first-position entropy about equal to the middle.
- Where Indus is unlike every library system (the residue): whole texts repeat MORE than their own bigram model predicts (uniq_vs_bigram 0.81-0.83 on the whole corpus; every other system 0.84-1.08; seals alone 0.98). Indus also pairs a very small closed class at the END (n80_last/n80_all 0.40, like Latin 0.33 and heraldry 0.12) with an OPEN first position (1.1, like Ur III legends and Linear B), the reverse of heraldry (field tincture first: 0.02) and of Proto-Elamite (object sign first, numeral last: 1.6 / 0.04).
- Heraldry is a poor match (distance 5.5-5.6): blazons start from a closed class of tinctures, have 10 frames covering 62% of texts (Indus 15%), and more types. Ur III legends are a poor match (6.8-7.0): 100% unique, open at both ends, hapax-rich vocabulary. Khipu is second on the whole corpus only because of unique_share and h2; it has the highest within-text repetition of all (2.1-2.5, repeated colours).
- Not obtained: RGZM Samian potter stamps (login), merchants' or masons' marks (no machine-readable corpus found), tamga corpora. GitHub repository search returned no blazon dataset; the Rietstap OCR was used instead.
- Caveats: Rietstap OCR noise (forms <5 removed); khipu 'sign' is a coding choice (colour x magnitude class); length matching drops single-sign texts (reported separately as single_share); Indus rests on Mohenjo-daro + Harappa.
