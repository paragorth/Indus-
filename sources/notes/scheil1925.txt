Scheil 1925, Umma (Djokha) bulla, Ashmolean EA1931.120 — primary-source check (3 Oct 2026)
==========================================================================================

Question: does the bulla or Scheil's note carry any CUNEIFORM text? What is on the back? What
provenance does Scheil give? What did he print and read?

SHORT ANSWER
- No cuneiform. Scheil describes a fragment of a red-clay bulla with ONE Indus impression; the
  back carries a textile (cloth) impression, nothing written. Buchanan 1984 no. 232 and the
  Ashmolean record list no inscription other than the Indus signs (inscriptionsAndMarks: empty).
- Provenance is dealer's hearsay only: an antiquities dealer who brought it to Scheil "about ten
  years" earlier (so c. 1914-15) said it came from Djokha (Umma). Scheil himself doubts it and
  suggests an Indian may have brought it from India and sold it in Baghdad.
- Scheil counts five signs and compares them one by one with Waddell's plates (W. IV, XIV, XV,
  XVII, XVIII). His note is in fact a polemic against L. A. Waddell, The Indo-Sumerian Seals
  Deciphered (Luzac 1925); hence "pseudo-sumerien".

SCHEIL 1925, RA 22: 55-56 — RECOVERED TEXT (OCR fragments in reading order, French, then English)

p. 55 title: "UN NOUVEAU SCEAU HINDOU PSEUDO-SUMÉRIEN / par V. SCHEIL"

"M. Waddell dans un livre intitulé The Indo-Sumerian Seals deciphered (Luzac, 1925) met louablement
 à la portée des savants, dix-huit sceaux ou empreintes de sceaux à légende hiéroglyphique, trouvés
 à Harappa et à Mohenjo-Daro, dans la vallée de l'Indus, et qui jusqu'à [...] pièces, n'étaient
 connus que par les Illustrated London News, p. 528 f., sept. 20, 1924. M. Waddell, opérant sur un
 nombre si restreint de documents, pense néanmoins en déchiffrer les signes, par la comparaison avec
 les signes archaïques babyloniens ou sumériens [...] qui mène à de tels résultats ne peut être
 qu'une illusion. Pas un assyriologue classiquement formé ne souscrira aux procédés [...] en pays
 voisins, n'emporte pas qu'il y ait parenté latérale ou directe entre elles. M. Waddell en arrive à
 nous apprendre à réformer nos déchiffrements de sceaux babyloniens, tel celui de Ur Lugal edinna
 et celui de noms propres comme Ur-Nina [...]"
 = Waddell's book makes 18 Harappa/Mohenjo-daro seals available; his "decipherment" by comparison
 with archaic Babylonian signs is an illusion; no trained Assyriologist will accept his methods.

"Notre nouveau sceau hindou pseudo-sumérien comprend, comme ceux publiés par W., un bovidé qui
 occupe tout le champ inférieur : symbole [...]"
 = Our new seal has, like those published by Waddell, a bovid filling the whole lower field.

"[...] une empreinte de sceau de même sorte, en terre rouge, qui semble avoir été détachée d'une
 bulle scellant une marchandise (le revers porte l'empreinte de quelque tissu d'étoffe). Au dire de
 l'antiquaire qui me l'a apporté, il y a une dizaine d'années, l'objet proviendrait de Djokha.
 Voilà qui appuierait, semble-t-il, les théories de M. Waddell ! Mais peut-on ajouter foi à un
 renseignement fourni dans ces conditions ? N'est-ce pas plutôt, comme il arrive fréquemment, un
 Hindou qui de son pays a rapporté l'objet, et l'a vendu à Bagdad ? Quoiqu'il en soit, si l'objet
 venait réellement de Djokha, il en résulterait à cette heure et tout au plus, cette présomption
 que des relations commerciales ont pu exister anciennement entre l'Inde et la Babylonie."
 = A seal impression of the same kind, in red clay, apparently detached from a bulla sealing
 merchandise (the BACK bears the imprint of some woven cloth). According to the dealer who brought
 it to me about ten years ago, the object came from Djokha. That would seem to support Waddell's
 theories! But can one trust information supplied under such conditions? Is it not rather, as
 often happens, an Indian who brought the object from his country and sold it in Baghdad? In any
 case, if it really came from Djokha, the most that would follow is the presumption that trade
 relations may once have existed between India and Babylonia.

p. 56 running head: "V. SCHEIL. — UN NOUVEAU SCEAU HINDOU PSEUDO-SUMÉRIEN"
 Sign comparison: "Ceux-ci se retrouvent, le premier à gauche, dans XIV, XV, XVII, XVIII de W. Les
 deux premiers à gauche, dans XVIII de W. Le premier à gauche suivi de trois traits verticaux, est
 reproduit suivi de neuf traits verticaux, dans XVII de W. Le signe à [?] précédé d'une corde
 repliée, se trouve, semble-t-il, dans IV de W. / Que contiennent [ces légendes ?...]"
 = These [signs] recur: the first on the left in Waddell's XIV, XV, XVII, XVIII; the first two on
 the left in W. XVIII; the first on the left followed by three vertical strokes is reproduced
 followed by nine vertical strokes in W. XVII; the sign [...] preceded by a "folded rope" is found,
 it seems, in W. IV. What do [these legends] contain? [...]
 Cross-ref in the same volume (Thureau-Dangin): "une empreinte du même type, relevée sur une bulle
 qui, d'après l'antiquaire qui la lui a vendue il y a une dizaine d'années, proviendrait de Djokha
 (près de Tello). Il n'est donc nullement impossible que le sceau acquis par le Louvre ait bien
 [été trouvé à Tello]" and "On notera que, des cinq signes gravés [...]" (Louvre seal AO 9036).

 Reading note: Scheil reads the line LEFT to RIGHT and describes the leftmost sign as "followed by
 three vertical strokes". In the corpus reading 127-705-2-4-390 (right to left) the leftmost sign
 is W390 followed (leftwards-to-right) by the 4-stroke and 2-stroke numerals. Scheil's "trois
 traits" vs the corpus 4 (and 2) is a stroke-count discrepancy to check on the Ashmolean photo:
 the photo shows, from the left, a hanging-stroke sign, a group of long vertical strokes, a short
 group, then two further signs; the exact stroke counts cannot be fixed at 673 px (a 6x crop of
 the inscription band shows a comb-like sign, a short-stroke group and a tall-stroke group on the
 left, then two complex signs, consistent with five signs but not resolving 3 vs 4 strokes). Parpola 1994
 (no. 25) and Buchanan also count five signs. The "corde repliée" (folded rope) sign is Scheil's
 description of one of the right-hand signs (W705 or W127 in our numbering).
 No cuneiform is mentioned anywhere in the note; the only writing he discusses is the five Indus
 signs. Scheil gives no measurements and does not say he kept the object; it reached the
 Ashmolean in 1931 as "gift of the French Academy".

BUCHANAN 1984 (OCR snippets, catalogueofancie0000ashm)
 "(iii) Indus Valley Seals / 232. Impression on clay sealing; like no. 233. 1931.120; seal 29 x 27;
 gift of the French Academy; said to be from Umma in Iraq: Scheil, 1925, p. 56."
 "233. Circular boss on thick square base; glazed steatite; chipped; Indus script above one-horned
 animal before cult object. 1931.119; 24 x 11; Kish: K. 903: from trench C-9, 5 (5) on Tell
 Ingharra."
 "234. Shape, material, and design like no. 233. 1956.1000; ..." (bought).
 Introduction: "The impression of an Indus Valley seal on a clay tag acquired in Iraq as from Umma
 by Scheil (no. 232 here) is a rare illustration of their use with consignments of goods reaching
 Meso[potamia]."
 Buchanan records no inscription on the back and no cuneiform; he calls it a tag/sealing and
 gives the seal face as 29 x 27 mm (Ashmolean record: 2.9 x 2.7 cm, "jar-sealing", baked clay).

GADD 1932 (p. 4, open text): "an imprint of such a seal upon the fragment of a clay label from a
 bale of cloth had also been published [n.1 Revue d'Assyriologie, xxii. 56] by Father Scheil, and
 this was said to come from the site of Umma, the neighbour city of Lagash."
 So the "bale of cloth" reading of the back is Gadd's (1932) gloss on Scheil's "tissu d'étoffe";
 Parpola 1994 repeats it.

ZETTLER, OIP 111 (1993) p. [Indus seal 13 N 506]: "An impression of such an Indus Valley seal,
 however, was acquired on the antiquities market and was said to have come from Djokha (ancient
 Umma) (Scheil 1925: 55-56)."

ASHMOLEAN RECORD (EA1931.120, non-preferred AN1931.120; irn 486829)
 Title "Sealing with a bovine in profile"; object type jar-sealing; clay, baked; 2.9 x 2.7 cm;
 "circa 2000 BCE"; place of excavation "Jokha, Tell (ancient Sumerian city of Umma)"; place of
 creation Indus Valley; credit "Presented by the French Academy, 1931" (acquisition 1931,
 transfer 2002 to Eastern Art); on display Gallery 19 Ancient Near East. inscriptionsAndMarks: [].
 Literature: Buchanan 1984 no. 232, pl. XV; Ahuja 2018, Art and Archaeology of Ancient India,
 cat. 11.5, pp. 77-79 (text quoted in the record: "a sealing (ea1931.120) ... found in
 Mesopotamia ... the sealing in Umma").
 Note the museum lists Umma as "place of excavation", which overstates Scheil's dealer hearsay.

VERDICT FOR THE ANCHOR LEAD
 - Cuneiform on the bulla: none, by all four independent descriptions (Scheil, Gadd, Buchanan,
   Ashmolean). Upper bound: the back has never been photographed in a source reachable here; a
   human could ask the Ashmolean (Eastern Art) for a photo of the reverse, but Scheil, who held
   it, reports only a cloth impression.
 - Find story: dealer's word, c. 1914-15, Baghdad market; Scheil himself distrusts it. The
   "Umma" label should be rated as unprovenanced (Parpola's caveat confirmed and strengthened).
 - Scheil's sign reading: five signs, read left to right, with a "three vertical strokes"
   numeral after the first sign; compared only with Waddell's plates, no values proposed.
