# Al-Sindi 1999, *Dilmun Seals* (Bahrain National Museum): Indus-sign seals, read from the photos

Source: Cambridge Scan & Deliver copy (pp. 225, 244–248, 357), supplied to the user for private study. The scan itself is **not** kept in the repository. Below are only my own readings and the catalogue data (serial no., record no., find-spot, type, size, weight).

Al-Sindi's own text (p. 245): five Dilmun seals carry Indus signs; all are of the **Early** type. One group shows the strip of Indus signs with an **empty space where the animal would normally be**.

| Al-Sindi no. | Record no. | Find-spot | Type | Laursen 2010 no. | Our corpus reading (Wells) | What the photos show (my reading) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 182 | 2732 | Saar | Early | 9 | 55-220-91-1-93-31 | **The stroke block is 3 rows of 4 = 12** (clear on the seal face and on the impression), so W55 is right and Laursen's drawing (2 × 4) is wrong. Next come **three standing figures joined at the arms**, with triangular heads and V-legs, then one tall stroke at the end. No animal: the lower half is empty, as Al-Sindi notes. The corpus splits the figures as 220-91-1-93; the photo supports 'linked figures' but cannot confirm the fish sign W220. |
| 180 | 4040 | Hamad Town (Laursen/Srivastava: Karzakkan) | Early | 8 | 91-31-455-220 | 'Five symbols' (Al-Sindi). A pair of leaning figures (twins-like 'λλ'), a tall stroke, a triangle-headed sign, and a fish-like sign at the edge. The animal below is a rhinoceros (Al-Sindi), not a gaur. The corpus order runs from the twins end. |
| 279 | 3011 | Bahrain Fort (Qala'at al-Bahrain) | Late | 7 | 190-60-55-90-160 | Fragment. **The stroke block is 3 rows of 4 = 12**, which again supports W55 over Laursen's 2 × 4. Plus a man-like sign and angled strokes; a large bull with a pleated neck below. |
| 181 | 2734 | Saar | Early | – | – | Badly scratched; only one sign is legible above the bull's head. |
| 179 | 2796 | Saar | Early | – | – | Two long-horned animals back to back, smaller animals below, and 'crossed lines like an arrow'. Not an inscription. |
| 160 | 4059 | Hamad Town | Early | 56 | 740-740 | Two animals, upside down to each other, separated by 'two symbols resembling Indus characters' (Al-Sindi). They are motif-like, as Laursen noted. |

Findings:
- The two disputed stroke blocks (nos. 182 and 279) are both **3 rows of 4 = 12**, as the corpus has them. The '2 × 4 = 8' of the Laursen drawings is a drawing error. This strengthens S284 (12 is enriched abroad) and the reading '12' on Gulf seals.
- No. 182 has no animal at all. That fits S314: abroad, the text can stand without the home emblem.
- The animals are not all gaur: no. 180 is a rhinoceros (Al-Sindi's description).
